[SRC-01] [PRIMARY] Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee, 785 F. Supp. 1343 (E.D. Wis. 1992).
         Court: United States District Court for the Eastern District of Wisconsin.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. Terence T. Evans, Chief Judge.
         Date of Decision: March 5, 1992.
         Docket / Record: Civil Action No. 91-C-892.
         Evidentiary Scope: Memorandum decision and order denying defendants' motion to dismiss
         civil rights complaint brought under 42 U.S.C. § 1983; articulates the state-created danger
         doctrine under the Fourteenth Amendment Substantive Due Process Clause, holding that when
         police officers affirmatively intervene, prevent citizen rescuers from assisting, and
         surrender an imperiled child back to a captor, constitutional liability attaches. Sustains
         equal protection claims alleging differential police service based on race.
         URL: https://law2.umkc.edu/faculty/projects/ftrials/conlaw/Sinthasomphone.html

[SRC-02] [PRIMARY] Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee, 838 F. Supp. 1320 (E.D. Wis. 1993).
         Court: United States District Court for the Eastern District of Wisconsin.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. Terence T. Evans, Chief Judge.
         Date of Decision: November 23, 1993.
         Docket / Record: Civil Action No. 91-C-892.
         Evidentiary Scope: Decision and order denying qualified immunity to responding MPD Officers
         John A. Balcerzak, Joseph T. Gabrish, and Rick Porubcan on claims alleging intentional racial
         discrimination under the Equal Protection Clause and substantive due process violations;
         establishes that clearly established constitutional law prohibited discriminatory police
         protection and state-facilitated endangerment.
         URL: https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148468add7b049344b699e

[SRC-03] [PRIMARY] Balcerzak & Gabrish v. City of Milwaukee, 980 F. Supp. 983 (E.D. Wis. 1997),
         affirmed, 163 F.3d 993 (7th Cir. 1998).
         Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.
         Panel: Circuit Judges Walter J. Cummings, Richard D. Cudahy, and Terence T. Evans
         (Judge Cummings authoring opinion).
         Date of Decision: December 18, 1998 (District Court decision October 23, 1997).
         Docket / Record: Seventh Circuit Nos. 97-3932 & 97-4001.
         Evidentiary Scope: Comprehensive appellate decision affirming summary judgment dismissing
         § 1983 claims brought by reinstated MPD Officers Balcerzak and Gabrish against Chief Philip
         Arreola and the City of Milwaukee; details the statutory procedural history of the officers'
         September 1991 terminations, the October 1992 Board of Fire and Police Commissioners
         hearings (wherein both officers pled guilty to rule infractions), and the April 1994
         statutory appeal order by Reserve Circuit Judge Robert J. Parins reinstating them.
         URL: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/163/993/494395/

[SRC-04] [PRIMARY] Weinberger v. State of Wisconsin, 906 F. Supp. 485 (W.D. Wis. 1995),
         affirmed, 105 F.3d 1182 (7th Cir. 1997).
         Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.
         Panel: Circuit Judges Daniel A. Manion, Michael S. Kanne, and Diane P. Wood
         (Judge Manion authoring opinion).
         Date of Decision: February 4, 1997.
         Docket / Record: Seventh Circuit No. 96-1077.
         Evidentiary Scope: Affirmance of summary judgment dismissing 42 U.S.C. § 1983 wrongful death
         lawsuit brought by the estate of Jeremiah Weinberger against Wisconsin Department of
         Corrections probation agents; establishes that state probation supervision does not create
         a "special relationship" or affirmative constitutional duty under DeShaney v. Winnebago
         County to protect members of the general public from probationers.
         URL: https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/105/1182/498877/

[SRC-05] [PRIMARY] Bell v. City of Milwaukee, 746 F.2d 1205 (7th Cir. 1984).
         Court: United States Court of Appeals for the Seventh Circuit.
         Author: Circuit Judge Walter J. Cummings.
         Date of Decision: September 4, 1984.
         Docket / Record: Nos. 82-2102, 82-2117, 82-2139.
         Evidentiary Scope: Foundational Seventh Circuit civil rights precedent establishing municipal
         liability under 42 U.S.C. §§ 1983 and 1985 for a 20-year institutional police conspiracy
         covering up the 1958 fatal shooting of Daniel Bell, an unarmed 22-year-old Black man, by MPD
         patrolman Thomas Grady; serves as the vital institutional backdrop for federal scrutiny of
         MPD racial bias and cover-up culture prior to 1991.

[SRC-06] [PRIMARY] In re Discipline of John A. Balcerzak and Joseph T. Gabrish,
         Milwaukee County Circuit Court Case No. 92-CV-016254.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. Robert J. Parins, Reserve Circuit Judge (Brown County).
         Date of Order: April 27, 1994 (Written Order entered April 28, 1994).
         Statutory Authority: Wis. Stat. § 62.13(5)(i) (Statutory Appeal of Police Commission Orders).
         Evidentiary Scope: Formal state judicial order vacating the Milwaukee Board of Fire and Police
         Commissioners' discharge of Officers Balcerzak and Gabrish; holds that while officers showed
         poor judgment in the Konerak Sinthasomphone incident, termination was disproportionate to
         historical MPD discipline; reduces penalty to a 60-day administrative suspension without pay;
         orders immediate reinstatement to active duty with approximately $55,000 back pay each.

[SRC-07] [PRIMARY] Milwaukee Common Council, Resolution Approving Settlement of Civil Action
         in Estate of Sinthasomphone et al. v. City of Milwaukee et al., Resolution No. 941829.
         Legislative Body: Common Council of the City of Milwaukee (14-2 Affirmative Vote).
         Date of Enactment: April 25, 1995.
         Evidentiary Scope: Official legislative proceeding authorizing the City Comptroller to pay
         $850,000 from the municipal damages reserve fund to the estate of Konerak Sinthasomphone;
         explicitly incorporates comprehensive mutual releases establishing that the negotiated
         compromise constitutes no admission of constitutional fault, unlawful racial discrimination,
         or individual officer liability by the City of Milwaukee.

[SRC-08] [PRIMARY] State of Wisconsin v. Christopher J. Scarver,
         Columbia County Circuit Court Case No. 94-CF-208.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. Daniel George, Circuit Judge.
         Date of Judgment: May 15, 1995.
         Prosecuting Authority: Columbia County District Attorney's Office.
         Evidentiary Scope: Criminal information, competency proceedings, plea transcript, and judgment
         of conviction for two counts of First-Degree Intentional Homicide under Wis. Stat. § 940.01(1)
         arising from the November 28, 1994 bludgeoning deaths of Jeffrey Dahmer and Jesse Anderson at
         Columbia Correctional Institution; documents Scarver's no-contest plea and the imposition
         of two consecutive life sentences without eligibility for parole.

[SRC-09] [PRIMARY] Wisconsin Department of Corrections, Division of Adult Institutions,
         Internal Administrative Security Inquiry: Fatal Assaults on Inmates Jeffrey Dahmer
         and Jesse Anderson at Columbia Correctional Institution.
         Investigating Body: WDOC Office of Internal Affairs & Security Audit Team.
         Date: December 1994.
         Evidentiary Scope: Formal administrative findings reviewing security protocols at CCI on
         November 28, 1994; establishes that three high-profile inmates with violent records and severe
         mental health/racial tensions were assigned to an unmonitored gymnasium cleaning detail for
         20 minutes with access to a 20-inch, 5-pound steel weight bar; details institutional protocol
         revisions governing the recreation and work details of high-notoriety prisoners.

[SRC-10] [PRIMARY] State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer,
         Milwaukee County Circuit Court Criminal Case No. F-912542.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. Laurence C. Gram Jr., Circuit Judge.
         Counsel of Record: E. Michael McCann (District Attorney) and Michael Erdmann (Deputy DA)
         for the State; Gerald P. Boyle and Wendy Patrickus for Defendant Dahmer.
         Date of Proceedings: July 23, 1991 – February 17, 1992.
         Evidentiary Scope: Complete judicial record: Initial Criminal Complaint (July 25, 1991, 4 counts);
         Amended Complaint (August 6, 1991, 12 counts); Preliminary Examination & Information (August 22,
         1991, 15 counts: 13 counts under post-1988 Wis. Stat. § 940.01(1) and 2 counts under pre-1988
         statute); Dual Plea of Guilty but NGI (January 13, 1992); Sanity Phase Trial Transcripts
         (January 30 – February 15, 1992); Special Jury Verdict finding sanity on all 15 counts (10-2);
         Judgment of Conviction and Sentencing Transcript (February 17, 1992, 15 consecutive life terms).

[SRC-11] [PRIMARY] State of Ohio v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer,
         Summit County Court of Common Pleas Case No. CR-91-08-1686 / CR-78-8-800.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. James R. Williams, Common Pleas Judge.
         Prosecuting Authority: Lynn Slaby, Summit County Prosecuting Attorney.
         Date of Judgment: May 1, 1992.
         Evidentiary Scope: Summit County Grand Jury Indictment (September 24, 1991) charging Aggravated
         Murder under Ohio Revised Code § 2903.01 for the June 18, 1978 homicide of Steven Mark Hicks;
         guilty plea transcript and judgment entry imposing Dahmer's 16th consecutive life sentence.

[SRC-12] [PRIMARY] Collins et al. v. City of Milwaukee et al.,
         United States District Court for the Eastern District of Wisconsin, Case No. 17-CV-00234.
         Presiding Jurist: Hon. Lynn Adelman, District Judge.
         Parties / Counsel: Represented by ACLU of Wisconsin and NAACP Legal Defense Fund.
         Date of Settlement Order: July 23, 2018.
         Evidentiary Scope: Class action consent decree resolving constitutional challenge to MPD's
         pedestrian and vehicular stop-and-frisk practices; establishes external monitoring by Crime and
         Justice Institute and mandatory bias data collection, serving as the modern empirical benchmark
         for evaluating persistent racial disparities in Milwaukee municipal policing.

TIER 3: SCHOLARLY MONOGRAPHS & PEER-REVIEWED ACADEMIC STUDIES
================================================================================

[SRC-20] [SCHOLARLY] Tithecott, Richard (1997). Of Men and Monsters: Jeffrey Dahmer and the
         Construction of the Serial Killer.
         Publisher: Madison: University of Wisconsin Press (ISBN: 978-0299156848).
         Evidentiary Scope: Foundational cultural and media analysis; deconstructs how cultural tropes
         of the "serial killer as monster" functioned to obscure and absolve institutional culpability,
         police indifference, and racial segregation; critiques the voyeuristic gaze of media.

[SRC-21] [SCHOLARLY] Takach, Michail (2022). Milwaukee Drag: A History of Queer Culture and Community.
         Publisher: Charleston: The History Press (ISBN: 978-1467149112).
         Evidentiary Scope: Comprehensive historiography of Milwaukee LGBTQ+ resilience; deconstructs
         the media myth that Dahmer created queer visibility; details pre-existing community networks,
         activism around HIV/AIDS, and Scott Gunkle's role in pioneering MPD recruit sensitivity training.

[SRC-22] [SCHOLARLY] Jentzen, Jeffrey M. (2017). "Micro Disasters: The Dahmer Case—A Forensic
         Pathology and Anthropological Model for Mass Fatality Identification."
         Journal: Academic Forensic Pathology, 7(3): 384–398.
         Evidentiary Scope: Peer-reviewed forensic retrospective documenting the chain of custody,
         bone reassociation protocols, odontological comparisons, and multidisciplinary techniques
         developed during the processing of Apartment 213; serves as an international training model.

[SRC-23] [SCHOLARLY] Masters, Brian (1993). The Shrine of Jeffrey Dahmer.
         Publisher: London: Hodder & Stoughton / New York: Coronet (ISBN: 978-0340591949).
         Evidentiary Scope: Comprehensive early psychological biography authored with direct access to
         Dahmer, defense attorney Gerald Boyle, and trial transcripts; detailed analysis of paraphilic
         necrophilia, fantasy life, and psychological development; critically appraised for perpetrator-
         centering and uncritical credulity toward Dahmer's self-reports.

[SRC-24] [SCHOLARLY] Lankford, Adam, & Hayes, Jenna K. (2022). "Did Jeffrey Dahmer Suffer from
         a Compulsion or Addiction to Killing?"
         Journal: Sexual Health & Compulsivity, 29(3–4): 112–136.
         Evidentiary Scope: Empirical behavioral analysis evaluating Dahmer's behavior against clinical
         addiction criteria; documents how situational restraint, concealment, and targeted victim
         selection disproved irresistible compulsion in the 1992 sanity verdict.

[SRC-25] [SCHOLARLY] Massey, Douglas S., & Denton, Nancy A. (1993). American Apartheid: Segregation
         and the Making of the Underclass.
         Publisher: Cambridge: Harvard University Press (ISBN: 978-0674018211).
         Evidentiary Scope: Foundational sociological text establishing quantitative metrics of urban
         racial segregation; documents Milwaukee's residential dissimilarity index exceeding 80 (hyper-
         segregation), contextualizing the spatial isolation of the Near West Side and Northside.

[SRC-26] [SCHOLARLY] Egger, Steven A. (2002). The Killers Among Us: An Examination of Serial
         Murder and Its Investigation (2nd Edition).
         Publisher: Upper Saddle River: Prentice Hall (ISBN: 978-0130930118).
         Evidentiary Scope: Introduces and formalizes the criminological concept of the "less-dead"—
         marginalized populations (racial minorities, gay youth, sex workers, transient individuals)
         whose disappearances are systematically devalued and deprioritized by law enforcement.

[SRC-27] [SCHOLARLY] Jentzen, Jeffrey M., Palermo, George B., et al. (1994). "Destructive Hostility:
         The Jeffrey Dahmer Case—A Psychiatric and Forensic Discussion."
         Status: Withdrawn from Publication (Marquette University / MCME Research Papers).
         Evidentiary Scope: Early joint forensic-psychiatric manuscript analyzing pathology and crime
         scene dynamics; noted in research historiography as formally withdrawn from peer-reviewed
         circulation, underscoring the methodological challenges of initial post-trial analyses.
