IMPORTANT WORKFLOW RULE
-----------------------
Do not build an incomplete app.

The app should only be built after the structure, design specification, and sufficient content have been finalized and the user explicitly confirms that it is time to start building.

CONTENT WORKFLOW
----------------
Content will be developed one major tab at a time.

For each tab:
1. User provides research/content.
2. Validate the material.
3. Check factual consistency, chronology, terminology, unsupported claims, and internal contradictions.
4. Refine wording and structure while preserving the user's research intent.
5. Fact-check externally where necessary and distinguish:
   - documented facts
   - attributed interpretations
   - disputed accounts
   - later theories/speculation
6. Shape the material for the web:
   - article sections
   - tables
   - timelines
   - callouts
   - citations
   - internal hyperlinks
7. Review and finalize the tab.
8. Treat finalized content as locked unless a later correction or improvement is deliberately approved.

Recommended content order:
Overview -> Victims -> Timeline -> Context -> Case -> Analysis -> Impact -> Sources

CORE EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
------------------------
The project should read like a serious historical/research publication rather than a sensational true-crime site.

The subject matter should be handled:
- factually
- non-sensationally
- with attention to victims as people
- with clear separation between evidence and interpretation
- without graphic material
- without turning the perpetrator into the sole focus of the publication

OVERALL INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE
---------------------------------
Primary header navigation:

OVERVIEW | VICTIMS | TIMELINE | CONTEXT | CASE | ANALYSIS | IMPACT | SOURCES

There are 7 main editorial tabs plus a separate Sources destination.

The current grouping logic is:

1. OVERVIEW
   The comprehensive entry point and factual spine of the project.

2. VICTIMS
   Dedicated space for victim biographies and the full victims overview.

3. TIMELINE
   The complete chronological reconstruction of the case.

4. CONTEXT
   Historical and biographical context surrounding the case.

5. CASE
   The offense series, investigation, arrest, and legal proceedings.

6. ANALYSIS
   Systemic failures, psychological evaluation, and theories/motivations.

7. IMPACT
   Community effects, reform discussions, media/cultural portrayal, modern
   re-examination, and the final full conclusion.

8. SOURCES
   Full research sources, further reading, and methodology/source notes.

The broad tab grouping is approved.
Exact dropdown item lists can be refined while the content for each tab is
finalized.

HEADER / NAVIGATION BEHAVIOR
----------------------------
Desktop:
- Single horizontal editorial header.
- Main tabs have downward-arrow indicators where dropdowns are present.
- Clicking a tab label opens that tab's main page.
- Clicking the arrow opens the dropdown.
- Hovering may also open the dropdown on desktop.
- Dropdown links go directly to the corresponding article or page.
- Active section should have a subtle visual indicator, preferably red or
  another restrained accent.

Mobile:
- The long horizontal navigation collapses into one simple menu.
- The menu expands vertically.
- Avoid cramped horizontal navigation.
- Favor vertical scrolling and readable article layouts.
- Do not require users to zoom or horizontally navigate the article itself.

NOTE ON CONCLUSION
------------------
There are intentionally two different conclusions.

1. OVERVIEW CONCLUSION
   - Short.
   - Appears at the end of the comprehensive Overview article.
   - Summarizes the case and gives the reader a concise takeaway.
   - Can be an anchor/section within /overview rather than a completely
     separate article.
   - The Overview article may contain internal hyperlinks to full articles in
     other sections.

2. IMPACT CONCLUSION
   - Full final conclusion of the research project.
   - Appears at the bottom of the Impact dropdown after Modern Re-examination.
   - Synthesizes the entire body of research:
     historical record, victims, institutional failures, psychological
     research, social consequences, media portrayal, and modern
     re-examination.
   - This is the true closing essay of the publication.

The two conclusions must not simply repeat each other.

OVERVIEW ARTICLE / HOMEPAGE FEATURE
------------------------------------
There are two different "Overview" experiences.

1. HOMEPAGE OVERVIEW ARTICLE
   - This is the prominent front-page feature.
   - It is descriptive and relatively concise.
   - Its job is to introduce the case and orient a first-time reader.
   - It should cover the basic who / what / when / where and briefly explain
     what the wider research project examines.
   - It should not attempt to reproduce the full research article.
   - At the end, include a clear "Read more" link that opens the full
     Overview page from the header navigation.

2. FULL OVERVIEW PAGE (/overview)
   - This is the detailed Overview article.
   - It is comprehensive enough that a reader can understand the case in
     substantial depth without immediately opening every other page.
   - It should contain:
     - geographic and temporal scope
     - major documented events
     - fuller case progression
     - concise explanation of the victims
     - key institutional contacts/issues
     - investigation and arrest
     - trial/legal outcome
     - why the case remains historically significant
     - internal hyperlinks to the deeper research articles
     - short Overview Conclusion at the end

The homepage feature and the /overview page therefore share the same subject
and editorial voice, but they are intentionally different in depth.

INTERNAL LINKING PRINCIPLE
--------------------------
The homepage Overview feature should end with a "Read more" link to /overview.

The full /overview article should then link naturally to the detailed pages for
topics such as Victims, Timeline, Historical Context, Systemic Failures,
Investigation, Trial, Psychological Profile, and other relevant sections.

The header's Overview tab always opens the full /overview page.

Internal hyperlinks should be used naturally.

Example principle:
A brief mention of institutional failures in Overview can link directly to
the full Systemic Failures article.

HOMEPAGE / FRONT PAGE
---------------------
The homepage is a curated editorial front page, not simply the full Overview
article and not a dashboard.

Approved hierarchy:

1. MAIN FEATURE: OVERVIEW
   - Largest/dominant feature.
   - Descriptive homepage feature introducing the case.
   - More concise than /overview.
   - Ends with a "Read more" link to the full /overview page.
   - /overview establishes the detailed factual spine of the project.

2. SECONDARY FEATURES: HISTORICAL CONTEXT + VICTIMS
   - Historical Context: Milwaukee 1987-1991
   - Victims section with emphasis on who the victims were as people.
   - The homepage should not duplicate the full victims table.

3. MAJOR VISUAL ELEMENT: SUMMARIZED TIMELINE
   - A concise "Case at a Glance" timeline.
   - The homepage version is a summary.
   - Link to the full Master Chronological Timeline.

4. INTELLECTUAL FRAMEWORK: RESEARCH QUESTIONS
   Suggested questions:
   - What happened?
   - Who were the victims?
   - What did institutions know?
   - How has the case been remembered/re-examined?

5. FEATURED RESEARCH + RESEARCH & SOURCES
   - Small editorial selections near the bottom.
   - These are not additional top-level navigation categories.

HOMEPAGE CARD DESIGN
--------------------
Use different card/article sizes instead of placing every feature in an
identical grid.

Goal:
- create editorial hierarchy
- make the main feature visibly dominant
- use medium and smaller modules for secondary material
- retain generous whitespace
- avoid a dashboard/card-grid aesthetic

The layout should feel like a curated newspaper front page or research
publication rather than a SaaS dashboard.

FEATURED RESEARCH
-----------------
This is a curated gateway to deeper articles, not another TOC level.

Suggested featured pieces:
- Systemic Failures
- Psychological Profile & Evaluation
- Media & Cultural Portrayal
- Modern Re-examination

Each can have:
- title
- one or two lines of description/deck
- simple "Read article" link

These can change later as the research content develops.

RESEARCH & SOURCES ON HOMEPAGE
------------------------------
The homepage should contain only a compact source/research introduction.

Purpose:
- explain what kinds of evidence the project uses
- communicate research credibility
- link to the complete Sources page

Possible categories:
- Primary Records
- Contemporary Reporting
- Books & Scholarship
- Documentaries & Interviews

Do NOT put the complete bibliography on the homepage.

HEADER SOURCES PAGE VS HOMEPAGE SOURCES
----------------------------------------
They serve different purposes.

HEADER -> SOURCES
- Complete source repository.
- Further reading.
- Source notes.
- Methodology/research notes.

HOMEPAGE -> RESEARCH & SOURCES
- Short explanation of the evidence base.
- Small selection or categories.
- Link to full Sources page.

METHODOLOGY
-----------
Include a small methodology/research section.

It does not need to be a major header tab.

It can live under Sources and/or be linked from the minimal footer.

It should eventually explain:
- source types used
- how conflicting accounts are handled
- how documented facts are separated from interpretation
- limits of surviving records
- how later claims/theories are treated

- spacious

- readable

The inspiration is the visual language of a modern newspaper or long-form

COLORS
------
Approved palette concept:
- black: primary text/navigation
- warm off-white: primary page background
- shades of grey: secondary text, borders, metadata

- gradients
- excessive accent colors
- ornamental UI effects
- bright multi-color dashboards

TYPOGRAPHY
----------
Approved principle:

- serif similar in character to Newsreader / Georgia / Source Serif
- sans-serif similar in character to Inter / Helvetica / Arial

IMPORTANT EDITORIAL DISTINCTIONS
--------------------------------
Victims:
- emphasize who they were
- avoid reducing people to names, ages, or case numbers
- full detail belongs on the Victims page

The Offense Series:
- brief and non-sensational
- focus on pattern, development, and escalation
- avoid unnecessary graphic detail

Systemic Failures:
- separate documented institutional contacts from interpretation
- use the four research subthemes listed above

Psychological Profile & Evaluation:
- distinguish documented evaluations from later interpretation
- avoid treating speculation as established fact

Theories & Motivations:
- explicitly distinguish:
  documented statements
  professional interpretations
  later theories
  unsupported speculation
- focus on what research actually supports

Media & Cultural Portrayal:
- distinguish historical record from dramatic reconstruction
- place contemporary coverage and later cultural portrayals in context

Modern Re-examination:
- examine how the case has been revisited decades later
- identify changes in scholarship, public memory, and interpretation

GENERAL IMPLEMENTATION RULES
----------------------------
1. Do not begin building the app until the user confirms to start.
2. Do not invent missing content while pretending it is verified research.
3. Keep the design minimal and editorial.
4. Preserve internal hyperlinks between related pages.
5. Make every major TOC entry reachable through the header/dropdown system.
6. Keep Overview comprehensive but readable.
7. Keep the homepage curated rather than encyclopedic.
8. Prefer evidence and attribution over unsupported certainty.
9. Use free-to-reuse/licensed imagery only.
10. Treat this document as the living project guide.
