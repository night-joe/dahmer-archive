CORE EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
------------------------
The Victims section should focus on the people who were killed: who they were,
what is publicly known about their lives, and what can responsibly be
reconstructed from the record.

Where the record is thin, leave it thin rather than filling gaps with
speculation.

The page should not:
- rank victims
- number them as a hierarchy of importance
- sensationalize their deaths
- use later media controversies as substitutes for biographies

TABLE 1
-------
Columns:
- Name
- Age
- Background
- Who They Were

The "Who They Were" field is the human-centered part of the table.

Important data rule:
Ages in historical sources are not always consistent. Where a meaningful
discrepancy exists, the final page should retain a source note instead of
pretending certainty.

Do not use "Date of death" as a standard table field. Prefer a "Last known
date" or similar wording where the historical record is uncertain.

PROFILE EDITORIAL PATTERN
-------------------------
Each profile should be compact enough to read, but can vary in length according
to the amount of reliable information available.

Suggested visual pattern:

    NAME

    Age · Place / Background

    Short biographical profile

    Brief case context

    Sources [1] [2]

The killing itself should normally receive no more than the minimum context
needed to orient the reader.

FACT-CHECKED / REFINED PROFILES
-------------------------------

1. STEVEN HICKS, 18
-------------------
Steven Mark Hicks had recently graduated from high school in Coventry
Township, Ohio. His father, Richard, described him as a deeply caring young
man; in an Associated Press account, he recalled Hicks becoming distraught
after shooting a rabbit during a hunting trip. Hicks was hitchhiking to a
rock concert at Chippewa Lake Park when he was picked up by Dahmer. He was
last seen on June 18, 1978, and his remains were not located until after
Dahmer confessed in 1991.

Refinement:
- Keep family and recent-graduate context.
- Remove unsupported superlatives such as "always open to making new friends"
  unless a source is cited.
- Use June 18 as the last-known date.
- Avoid presenting an exact death date as independent forensic fact.

2. STEVEN TUOMI, 28
-------------------
Steven W. Tuomi grew up in Ontonagon in Michigan's Upper Peninsula and worked
as a short-order cook at a Milwaukee restaurant. Former classmates remembered
him as quiet but artistic; one recalled a stained-glass lamp he made in art
class. Tuomi was last seen in Milwaukee on September 15, 1987. Dahmer later
admitted killing him, but Tuomi was the only Milwaukee victim for whom Dahmer
was not charged because investigators did not have sufficient physical
evidence.

Refinement:
- Change the draft's "around 24" to 28 in the main presentation.
- Do not state a precise death date.
- The lack of recovered remains and absence of a murder charge should be
  clearly noted as part of the record.

3. JAMES "JAMIE" DOXTATOR, 14
-----------------------------
Jamie Doxtator was the oldest of four children and was of Stockbridge and
Oneida ancestry. He was nearly six feet tall and was remembered as enjoying
pool and riding his bike. His mother, Debbie Vega, later spoke publicly about
him and his family. Doxtator was last seen on January 16, 1988.

Refinement:
- Keep the ancestry wording cautious and source-attributed.
- Remove the claim that he had recently left home because of an abusive
  stepfather unless the final article has a direct, reliable source for that
  detail.
- Use January 16, 1988 rather than only "January 1988."

4. RICHARD GUERRERO, 25*
-------------------------
Richard Guerrero came from a family of Mexican descent. His sister, Janie
Hagen, described him as close to his family and said he sometimes helped
their mother with errands and babysat her young daughter. Guerrero disappeared
in March 1988 and was last seen around that period. His family later described
the difficulty of getting answers and hired a private investigator who
defrauded them.

Refinement:
- Use 25 as the commonly reported age in the principal Milwaukee Journal
  Sentinel retrospective.
- Add a source note because the FBI file lists a December 12, 1965 birth date,
  which would imply a younger age at the time of his disappearance.
- Do not silently resolve the discrepancy.
- Keep Hagen's later criticism of Dahmer tourism primarily in Impact / Media.

5. ANTHONY SEARS, 24
--------------------
Anthony Sears was a Milwaukee restaurant manager who had recently received a
promotion at Baker's Square. He aspired to become a model and was saving money
with plans to leave Milwaukee. His mother said he had spoken about marrying
his girlfriend once he had enough money. He was last seen on March 25, 1989.

Refinement:
- Change the draft's age from 26 to 24.
- Keep his work, modeling ambitions, family celebration, and marriage plans.
- These details strongly support the page's purpose of showing who he was.

6. RAYMOND "RICKY BEEKS" SMITH, 33*
-------------------------------------
Raymond Lamont Smith, commonly known as Ricky Beeks, had been living with a
half-sister after his release from prison. He had a ten-year-old daughter who
lived in Rockford, Illinois. He was last seen on May 29, 1990.

Refinement:
- Prefer the name "Raymond 'Ricky Beeks' Smith" in the formal profile.
- Note that the FBI file gives a birth date of August 10, 1957, which would
  imply age 32 at the time; the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective
  reports 33.
- Use a source note rather than choosing silently.
- Avoid the draft's suggestion that his disappearance was unremarkable;
  instead simply state the documented family circumstances.

7. EDWARD "EDDIE" SMITH, 28*
-----------------------------
Eddie Smith aspired to become a professional model and was known among friends
for wearing a turban-like head wrap, which led to the nickname "the Sheikh."
He was reported missing in June 1990. His sister Carolyn later received a
telephone call, apparently from Dahmer, indicating that Eddie was dead.
Family statements presented in court described him as having been raised in a
loving Christian home and as being close to his family.

Refinement:
- Use 28 as the principal reported age, but note the FBI birth-date discrepancy
  if age is displayed numerically.
- Keep the modeling aspiration and family description.
- State sexual orientation only when supported by an identified family or
  primary source, and use attribution rather than presenting later statements
  as contemporaneous facts.

8. ERNEST MILLER, 24*
---------------------
Ernest Miller was preparing to begin classes at an arts college in Chicago and
hoped to become a professional dancer. His aunt, Vivian Miller, described him
as a talented dancer who had sung and performed when he was younger and had
sung at church. Miller had graduated from Milwaukee High School of the Arts
and was visiting relatives in Milwaukee. He was last seen on September 2,
1990.

Refinement:
- The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective gives his age as 24.
- The FBI file lists a May 5, 1967 birth date, which would imply a younger age;
  preserve this as a documented discrepancy.
- Keep his education, dancing, and family context.

9. DAVID THOMAS, 23
-------------------
David Thomas was the father of a two-year-old daughter, Courtia Beanland.
His former girlfriend, Chandra Beanland, remembered him as fun-loving.
Although Thomas sometimes stayed away for periods, Beanland reported him
missing in September 1990. He was last seen on September 24, 1990.

Refinement:
- Remove the phrase "passionate hustler" from the draft because it is vague
  and not useful to the site's biographical purpose.
- Keep the daughter/family context, with careful attribution.
- Avoid importing the full victim-impact statement into the biography.

10. CURTIS STRAUGHTER, 17-18*
-----------------------------
Curtis Straughter was a high-school dropout who had joined Gay Youth Milwaukee
at age 15. He worked as a nursing assistant and was planning to obtain a
high-school equivalency certificate and attend modeling school. He lived with
his grandmother, and his mother later spoke at the trial. He was last seen in
early March 1991.

Refinement:
- There is a documented age discrepancy.
- The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective reports 18.
- The Wisconsin criminal complaint gives a date of birth of April 16, 1973,
  and records his grandmother as last seeing him on February 18, 1991,
  implying that he was 17.
- The final table should not silently choose. Display "17*" or another clearly
  sourced formulation with a note explaining the discrepancy.
- The last-seen date also appears as March 7 in the later Journal Sentinel
  retrospective, while other contemporaneous records give February 18.
  This should be resolved in the Timeline/source notes rather than treated as
  settled without qualification.

11. ERROL LINDSEY, 19
--------------------
Errol Lindsey was the youngest of six children. A friend recalled that he
worked making plaster figures, and his eighth-grade art teacher kept a
watercolor he had made. His mother remembered him as someone who usually went
to work and came home. Lindsey was last seen on April 7, 1991. His sister,
Rita Isbell, later delivered a victim-impact statement at Dahmer's sentencing.

Refinement:
- Keep the art, work, and family details.
- The unborn daughter and Rita Isbell's later media criticism can be included
  with careful sourcing, but the Netflix controversy belongs primarily in
  Media & Cultural Portrayal.
- Do not make the later dramatization the defining fact of Lindsey's profile.

12. ANTHONY "TONY" HUGHES, 31
-----------------------------
Anthony Hughes lived in Madison and had returned to Milwaukee to visit family.
He was deaf after an illness with pneumonia in infancy and communicated using
lip-reading, sign language, and written notes. He was remembered by family as
outgoing and socially connected. His mother, Shirley Hughes, was prominent
in later reporting and court proceedings. Hughes was last seen on May 24,
1991.

Refinement:
- Keep deafness and communication because they are important parts of his
  life, not merely case details.
- Do not use "deaf and mute" as an unqualified label when better wording is
  available; describe the communication circumstances directly.
- Keep his Madison/Milwaukee connection.
- Move later entertainment/media criticism to Impact / Media.

13. KONERAK SINTHASOMPHONE, 14
------------------------------
Konerak Sinthasomphone was one of a large Laotian immigrant family that
settled in Milwaukee in 1980. A contemporary Milwaukee Sentinel report stated
that the family came from Laos in November 1980 after worsening conditions
following the 1975 communist takeover. His father had been a farmer in Laos.
Konerak was about three when the family arrived, was a freshman at Pulaski
High School, and regularly played soccer at Mitchell Park. He was reported
missing in May 1991.

Konerak's profile should contain a separate short subsection:
    WHY HIS CASE MATTERS

That subsection can briefly note that he was the younger brother of Somsack
Sinthasomphone, whom Dahmer had sexually assaulted in 1988, and that Konerak's
May 27, 1991 police encounter later became central to scrutiny of institutional
response.

Refinement:
- Replace the earlier draft's 1979/1980 refugee chronology with the better-
  supported contemporary wording above.
- The exact details of canoe crossing, refugee-camp duration, and Catholic
  relocation assistance should be used only with direct citations to the
  relevant source.
- Detailed police-failure analysis belongs in Systemic Failures.

14. MATT TURNER, 20
-------------------
Matt Turner was a native of Flint, Michigan, who lived in Chicago and aspired
to become a model. He met Dahmer after Chicago's Gay Pride parade and agreed
to travel to Milwaukee. Turner had previously left home and had lived at a
Chicago halfway house. He occasionally used the name Donald Montrell. He was
last seen on June 30, 1991.

Refinement:
- The FBI file confirms the Donald Montrell alias and gives a July 3, 1970
  birth date.
- Keep the Chicago and halfway-house context.
- Avoid graphic details of the killing in the biography.

15. JEREMIAH WEINBERGER, 23
----------------------------
Jeremiah Weinberger was a native of Puerto Rico who lived in Chicago and
worked as a customer service representative for a video store. His roommate
remembered him as meticulous, artistic, neatly dressed, and attentive to his
appearance. Weinberger encountered Dahmer in Chicago and traveled with him to
Milwaukee by Greyhound bus. He was last seen on July 6, 1991.

Refinement:
- Use "video store" rather than "video company" unless a stronger source
  supports the latter.
- Keep the roommate's observations with attribution.
- The specific bar/location context can be added later if directly sourced.

16. OLIVER LACY, 23*
---------------------
Oliver Lacy was the youngest of three sons. He had a two-year-old child named
Emmanuel and was engaged to be married. Originally from Oak Park, Illinois,
he ran track at River Forest High School. His mother, Catherine Lacy,
described him as outspoken. He wore a cross that had belonged to his late
father and had moved to Milwaukee from Chicago within months of his father's
death. He was last seen on July 12, 1991 and was the first of Dahmer's victims
identified after the arrest.

Refinement:
- Use 23 as the main age reported by the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel
  retrospective.
- Note that the FBI file gives a June 23, 1967 birth date, which would imply
  age 24; preserve the discrepancy in a source note.
- Remove "raised by a single mother" unless the final article has a direct,
  reliable source for that detail.
- Keep the cross, family, engagement, child, and athletics details.

17. JOSEPH BRADEHOFT, 25
------------------------
Joseph Bradehoft had recently moved to Milwaukee and was looking for work.
His brother Donald had rented a Milwaukee apartment for him. He had recently
lived in Illinois and Minnesota and had a wife and three children in Minnesota,
ages two to seven. He loved sports and fishing. He left for a job interview
on July 16, 1991 and never returned. He met Dahmer near the Marquette
University campus and became Dahmer's final known victim.

Refinement:
- Use July 16 as the last-known date based on the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel
  retrospective.
- Do not state "killed in late July" as the primary date field.
- Keep the father/husband/work-search context.
- The exact encounter and murder chronology can be documented on the full
  Timeline page with source notes.

SOURCE-HANDLING RULE
--------------------
Do not treat a later retrospective article as equivalent to a contemporaneous
official record.

Where the sources disagree:
- identify the disagreement
- prefer the strongest primary/contemporaneous source for factual fields
  where appropriate
- retain the uncertainty in notes when it cannot be resolved
- do not manufacture a false single answer
