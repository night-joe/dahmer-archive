The Case: Offense Series, Arrest & Trial

Court: Milwaukee County Circuit Court • Presiding: Judge Laurence C. Gram Jr.

A legal and documentary review of the criminal series, the apprehension triggered by Tracy Edwards' escape, the subsequent forensic investigation, and the 1992 sanity trial.

1. The Offense Series: Pattern & Escalation

Between 1978 and 1991, seventeen homicides were committed across three distinct phases:

  • Phase 1: Bath Township, Ohio (1978): Solitary homicide of Steven Hicks, followed by a nine-year period of non-fatal functioning.
  • Phase 2: West Allis Residence (1987–1990): Homicides committed in the basement of his grandmother's home (Steven Tuomi, James Doxtator, Richard Guerrero, Anthony Sears).
  • Phase 3: Oxford Apartments (1990–1991): Rapid escalation in frequency, accounting for twelve homicides in fourteen months.

2. July 22, 1991: Escape & Apprehension

At approximately 11:30 PM on July 22, 1991, thirty-two-year-old Tracy Edwards escaped from Apartment 213 wearing a handcuff attached to his left wrist. Edwards flagged down Milwaukee Police patrol officers Robert Mueller and Rolf Mueller near 25th and Kilbourn.

Edwards guided the patrolmen back to Apartment 213 to retrieve the handcuff keys. While searching a nightstand drawer, Officer Mueller discovered Polaroid photographs documenting dismembered human remains, leading to immediate physical apprehension.

3. Investigation & Confessions

Interrogations were conducted primarily by MPD Detectives Patrick Kennedy and Dennis Murphy. Over a two-week period, Dahmer waived his right to legal counsel and provided extensive, voluntary audio-recorded confessions detailing all seventeen homicides.

The Wisconsin State Crime Laboratory and the Armed Forces Institute of Pathology (AFIP) coordinated forensic anthropology and dental identifications to verify victim identities from osteological remains.

4. The 1992 Sanity Trial (State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer)

Because Dahmer pled guilty to fifteen counts of first-degree intentional homicide, the court convened exclusively to adjudicate the second phase of Wisconsin's bifurcated trial system: whether the defendant was legally insane (not guilty by reason of mental disease or defect - NGI) under Wisconsin Statute § 971.15.

The Legal Standard: Under Wisconsin law, the defense had to prove that, as a result of mental disease or defect, the defendant lacked substantial capacity either to appreciate the wrongfulness of his conduct or to conform his conduct to the requirements of the law.

On February 15, 1992, after two weeks of psychiatric testimony, the jury returned a 10–2 verdict finding Dahmer legally sane at the time of all offenses. He was sentenced to fifteen consecutive terms of life imprisonment without parole eligibility.
