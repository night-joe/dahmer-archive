Case Overview • Documentary Record

The Jeffrey Dahmer Case

17 Confessed Killings • 16 Convictions • Milwaukee & Ohio • 1978–1991

Between 1978 and 1991, Jeffrey Dahmer murdered seventeen men and boys, most of them in Milwaukee. The crimes unfolded over more than a decade, with a long gap between his first known killing in Ohio in 1978 and the series of murders that resumed in Milwaukee in 1987.

By the time Dahmer was arrested in July 1991, the case had become one of the most extensive and consequential serial-murder investigations in the United States. Federal Bureau of Investigation files record that eleven victims' remains were recovered from his Milwaukee apartment, while Dahmer ultimately confessed to seventeen killings across Wisconsin and Ohio.

The case cannot be understood only through the crimes themselves. Many of Dahmer's victims were young men from marginalized communities, including a large proportion of Black victims, while others were Asian, white, Latino, and Native American. Many encounters occurred around Milwaukee's LGBTQ nightlife and public venues where Dahmer met potential victims. The historical record places the case squarely within a Milwaukee experiencing significant racial, economic, and geographic divisions, making questions of visibility, vulnerability, and municipal institutional response central to any serious examination.

The case finally came to light on the night of July 22, 1991, when Tracy Edwards escaped from Dahmer's apartment with a handcuff attached to one wrist and flagged down Milwaukee police officers at approximately 11:30 p.m. Officers followed Edwards back to 924 North 25th Street, where they arrested Dahmer and discovered human remains and photographic evidence inside Apartment 213. Investigators soon learned that the material in the apartment was connected to a prolonged, multi-jurisdictional series of killings.

What followed was far more than a routine criminal investigation. It exposed repeated previous police contacts, probation supervision oversights, unheeded neighborhood complaints, the catastrophic mishandling of fourteen-year-old Konerak Sinthasomphone, and the structural ways in which race, poverty, and sexuality shaped institutional response. This repository examines the documentary record beyond sensational headlines: the victims' biographies, the forensic chronology, Milwaukee's historical environment, the legal proceedings, documented psychiatric evaluations, systemic institutional failures, and decades of subsequent civic and cultural reassessment.

Date Documented Event Institutional Contact Significance
June 18, 1978 First murder committed in Bath Township, Ohio. Steven Hicks (18) is picked up hitchhiking and killed at Dahmer's childhood home. Remains concealed on property. Local Police (Missing Report) Series Origin
Sept 15, 1987 Murders resume after nine-year gap. Steven Tuomi killed at the Ambassador Hotel in Milwaukee. Dahmer later relocates to grandmother's residence in West Allis. None Recorded Series Resumption
Sept 25, 1988 Sexual assault of Sombat Sinthasomphone (13). Dahmer arrested; convicted in May 1989 of second-degree sexual assault; receives one-year Huber Law work release sentence. MPD / Milwaukee Courts Prior Conviction
May 1990 Move to Oxford Apartments (Apartment 213). Dahmer leases unit at 924 N. 25th Street while on formal probation supervision. Killings accelerate significantly. State Probation Division Location Shift
May 27, 1991 The Konerak Sinthasomphone police encounter. Fourteen-year-old escaped victim encountered by bystanders on street; returned to Dahmer's apartment by MPD officers. Murdered later that night. MPD Officers / 911 Dispatch Critical Failure
July 22, 1991 Tracy Edwards escapes; arrest of Dahmer. Edwards flags down Officers Mueller and Rauth; search of Apartment 213 reveals photographs and physical remains. MPD Patrol Officers Case Discovery
Feb 15, 1992 Wisconsin jury rejects insanity defense. Following a two-week trial on mental responsibility, jury finds Dahmer legally sane on all fifteen counts. Milwaukee County Circuit Court Judicial Verdict
May 1, 1992 Guilty plea entered in Ohio. Dahmer pleads guilty to aggravated murder of Steven Hicks before Judge William Spicer; receives sixteenth life sentence. Summit County Court of Common Pleas Ohio Disposition
Nov 28, 1994 Dahmer killed at Columbia Correctional Institution. Inmate Christopher Scarver fatally assaults Dahmer and inmate Jesse Anderson in prison gymnasium work detail. Wisconsin Dept. of Corrections Perpetrator Death

Research Standards & Evidence Base

Documentary Evidence & Research Provenance

This archive is strictly compiled from verified primary judicial files, official Federal Bureau of Investigation dockets, contemporary court reporter records, and peer-reviewed scholarly literature. Speculative claims and fictionalized dramatizations are documented as cultural artifacts rather than established facts.

Primary Records

FBI FOIA Vault files (Parts 01–14), Milwaukee County Circuit Court charging documents, and trial transcripts.

Contemporary Reporting

Day-by-day investigative wire filings from UPI, The New York Times, Washington Post, and Milwaukee Journal (1991–1995).

Academic Scholarship

Urban history of Milwaukee, peer-reviewed criminological and forensic psychiatric analyses, and legal case studies.
