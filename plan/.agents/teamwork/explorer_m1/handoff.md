## 2. Logic Chain
1. **Economic Restructuring (Levine & Chicago Fed)**:
   - Primary data confirms City of Milwaukee manufacturing employment fell from 119,284 in 1963 to 63,900 in 1987 (a loss of >46%), while the PMSA lost >50,000 manufacturing jobs between 1979 and 1991.
   - Anchor corporate contractions (Allis-Chalmers bankruptcy June 29, 1987; A.O. Smith layoffs with 15,000–20,000 applicants for few hundred jobs; Briggs & Stratton moving 11,000 union jobs to the South) illustrate the structural collapse.
   - Black workers were hardest hit: 55% of Black males worked in manufacturing in 1970; by 1990, non-employment among working-age Black males reached 46.6% (up from 41% in 1980), and poverty in the inner-core North Side climbed above 40%.
2. **Residential Segregation (Massey & Denton, Census CENSR-3)**:
   - Massey & Denton (1989) identified Milwaukee as hypersegregated across all five dimensions.
   - In 1990, the Black-White Dissimilarity Index stood between 82.8 and 84.4 (CENSR-3 Table 5-2; Farley & Frey 1994; Frey 2005), ranking Milwaukee #1 most segregated large metro in the nation. Black Isolation Index ($P^*$) was 71.3.
   - The city was bounded physically: Menomonee River Valley ("Mason-Dixon Line"), 16th Street Viaduct (James E. Groppi Unity Bridge), I-43 / Bronzeville demolition, and Holton Street.
3. **Avenues West & Oxford Apartments**:
   - Located at 924 N. 25th Street (east side between Kilbourn and State, ZIP 53233, Census Tract 137).
   - 49-unit brick building, >85% renter occupancy, dense mix of Marquette students and transient tenants, absentee landlordism.
   - Marquette University's $50M Campus Circle Project purchased the property in 1992 and demolished it between November 16 and 20, 1992.
4. **MPD History & Institutional Distrust**:
   - Harold Breier (Chief 1964–1984) held statutory lifetime tenure and resisted civilian oversight until the legislature enacted Senate Bill 56 in April 1984, prompting his retirement.
   - Longstanding community distrust was anchored by the Daniel Bell cover-up (1958 shooting, 1979 conviction, 1981 $1.6M federal verdict) and Ernest Lacy in-custody death (July 9, 1981 at 23rd & Wisconsin, Coalition for Justice for Ernest Lacy, $600,000 settlement, Wisconsin "Lacy Law").
   - Philip Arreola (appointed Nov. 1989 as first Hispanic and outsider chief) implemented community policing but faced fierce union backlash: the MPA voted 94% "no confidence" on August 7, 1991 after Arreola suspended the Sinthasomphone officers.
5. **LGBTQ Community & AIDS Response**:
   - On February 25, 1982, Gov. Lee Sherman Dreyfus signed Chapter 112, Laws of 1981 (AB 70), making Wisconsin the first state in the nation to outlaw sexual orientation discrimination in employment, housing, credit, and accommodations.
   - Established nightlife hub in Walker's Point (Club 219, This Is It, 1961 Black Nite Brawl).
   - AIDS infrastructure: BESTD Clinic (founded 1974), first Wisconsin AIDS case in 1982, Milwaukee AIDS Project (MAP founded Jan 1985 by Doug Nelson/BESTD, later ARCW / Vivent Health).
   - Police vice stings, bathhouse raids, and public newspaper outing generated pervasive fear of contacting law enforcement among gay residents, leaving predators shielded from victim reports.
