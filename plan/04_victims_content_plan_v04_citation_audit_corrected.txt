CORE EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
------------------------
The Victims section should focus on the people who were killed: who they were,
what is publicly known about their lives, and what can responsibly be
reconstructed from the record.

Where the record is thin, leave it thin rather than filling gaps with
speculation.

The page should not:
- rank victims
- number them as a hierarchy of importance
- sensationalize their deaths
- use later media controversies as substitutes for biographies

TABLE 1
-------
Columns:
- Name
- Age
- Background
- Who They Were

The "Who They Were" field is the human-centered part of the table.

Important data rule:
Ages in historical sources are not always consistent. Where a meaningful
discrepancy exists, the final page should retain a source note instead of
pretending certainty.

Do not use "Date of death" as a standard table field. Prefer a "Last known
date" or similar wording where the historical record is uncertain.

1. STEVEN HICKS, 18
-------------------
Steven Mark Hicks had recently graduated from high school in Coventry
Township, Ohio. [1] His father, Richard, described him as a deeply caring young
man; in an Associated Press account, he recalled Hicks becoming distraught
after shooting a rabbit during a hunting trip. [1] Hicks was hitchhiking to a
rock concert at Chippewa Lake Park when he was picked up by Dahmer. [1] He was
last seen on June 18, 1978. [1] His remains were not located until after
Dahmer confessed in 1991. [4, 17] At the 1992 Ohio sentencing, his father also
spoke about the loss of his son. [5]

Refinement:
- Keep family and recent-graduate context.
- Remove unsupported superlatives such as "always open to making new friends"
  unless a source is cited.
- Use June 18 as the last-known date.
- Avoid presenting an exact death date as independent forensic fact.

2. STEVEN TUOMI, 28*
-------------------
Steven W. Tuomi grew up in Ontonagon in Michigan's Upper Peninsula and worked
as a short-order cook at a Milwaukee restaurant. [1] Former classmates remembered
him as quiet but artistic; one recalled a stained-glass lamp he made in art
class. [1] The MJS retrospective reports that Tuomi was last seen or heard from in Milwaukee on September 15, 1987. [1] Dahmer later
admitted killing him, but Tuomi was the only Milwaukee victim for whom Dahmer
was not charged because investigators did not have sufficient physical
evidence. [2 (Part 03, PDF p. 10), 15, 16]

Refinement:
- Display age as 28* and preserve the discrepancy note: FBI Part 01 PDF p. 43 records a DOB detectives “believe” was December 19, 1962, which implies age 24 on September 15, 1987, while the retrospective reports 28. Treat this as a source/age-reference discrepancy, not a resolved civil-record fact. [1, 2 (Part 01, PDF p. 43), 13]
- Do not state a precise death date. Dahmer’s FBI Part 03 interview recollection was approximate (“somewhere around 1986 or 1987”); retain that as a separate statement rather than treating it as an exact date. [2 (Part 03, PDF p. 10)]
- The lack of recovered remains and absence of a murder charge should be clearly noted as part of the record. [15, 16]

3. JAMES "JAMIE" DOXTATOR, 14
-----------------------------
Jamie Doxtator was the oldest of four children and was of Stockbridge and
Oneida ancestry. [1] He was nearly six feet tall and was remembered as enjoying
pool and riding his bike. [1] His mother, Debbie Vega, later spoke publicly about
him and his family. [1] Doxtator was last seen on January 16, 1988.
[1]

Refinement:
- Keep the ancestry wording cautious and source-attributed.
- Remove the claim that he had recently left home because of an abusive
  stepfather unless the final article has a direct, reliable source for that
  detail.
- The MJS retrospective [1] gives January 16, 1988; contemporary UPI reporting [14] supports only January 1988. Keep the exact day flagged as dependent on the retrospective until a direct day-level source is located.

4. RICHARD GUERRERO, 25*
-------------------------
Richard Guerrero came from a family of Mexican descent. [1] His sister, Janie
Hagen, described him as close to his family and said he sometimes helped
their mother with errands and babysat her young daughter. [1] Guerrero disappeared
in March 1988 and was last seen around that period. [1] His family later described
the difficulty of getting answers and hired a private investigator who
defrauded them. [1]

Refinement:
- Use 25 as the commonly reported age in the principal Milwaukee Journal
  Sentinel retrospective.
- Add a source note because FBI Part 06 PDF p. 13 lists a December 12, 1965 birth date, implying age 22 on the March 29, 1988 missing-report date. UPI [11] reports that he was reported missing in March 1988, not an independent last sighting; preserve the three-source age discrepancy. [1, 2 (Part 06, PDF p. 13), 11]
- Do not silently resolve the discrepancy.
- Keep Hagen's later criticism of Dahmer tourism primarily in Impact / Media.

5. ANTHONY SEARS, 24
--------------------
Anthony Sears was a Milwaukee restaurant manager who had recently received a
promotion at Baker's Square. He aspired to become a model and was saving money
with plans to leave Milwaukee. His mother said he had spoken about marrying
his girlfriend once he had enough money. [1]
He was last seen on March 25, 1989. [1]

Refinement:
- Change the draft's age from 26 to 24.
- Keep his work, modeling ambitions, family celebration, and marriage plans.
- These details strongly support the page's purpose of showing who he was.

6. RAYMOND "RICKY BEEKS" SMITH, 33*
-------------------------------------
Raymond Lamont Smith, commonly known as Ricky Beeks, had been living with a
half-sister after his release from prison. [1] He had a ten-year-old daughter who
lived in Rockford, Illinois. [1] He was last seen on May 29, 1990. [1]

Refinement:
- Prefer the name "Raymond 'Ricky Beeks' Smith" in the formal profile.
- Note that the FBI file gives a birth date of August 10, 1957, which would imply age 32 at the time; the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective reports 33. [1, 2 (Part 01, PDF p. 25), 10]
- Use a source note rather than choosing silently.
- Avoid the draft's suggestion that his disappearance was unremarkable;
  instead simply state the documented family circumstances.

7. EDWARD "EDDIE" SMITH, 28*
-----------------------------
Eddie Smith aspired to become a professional model and was known among friends
for wearing a turban-like head wrap, which led to the nickname "the Sheikh." [1]
He was reported missing in June 1990. His sister Carolyn later received a
telephone call, apparently from Dahmer, indicating that Eddie was dead. [1, 11]
Family statements presented in court described him as having been raised in a
loving Christian home and as being close to his family. [1, 8]

Refinement:
- Use 28 as the principal reported age, but note the FBI birth-date discrepancy
  if age is displayed numerically. [1, 2 (Part 06, PDF p. 13), 11]
- Keep the modeling aspiration and family description.
- State sexual orientation only when supported by an identified family or
  primary source, and use attribution rather than presenting later statements
  as contemporaneous facts.

8. ERNEST MILLER, 24*
---------------------
Ernest Miller was preparing to begin classes at an arts college in Chicago and
hoped to become a professional dancer. His aunt, Vivian Miller, described him
as a talented dancer who had sung and performed when he was younger and had
sung at church. Miller had graduated from Milwaukee High School of the Arts
and was visiting relatives in Milwaukee. [1] He was last seen on September 2, 1990. [1, 11]

Refinement:
- The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective gives his age as 24.
- The FBI file lists a May 5, 1967 birth date, which would imply a younger age;
preserve this as a documented discrepancy. [1, 2 (Part 06, PDF p. 12), 11]
- Keep his education, dancing, and family context.
- Record the separate legal chronology without using it to support September 2: the complaint gives the aunt’s statement that Miller left at approximately 1:30 a.m. on September 3 and that relatives reported him missing September 4; it alleges the offense on or about September 3. [3]

9. DAVID THOMAS, 23
-------------------
David Thomas was the father of a two-year-old daughter, Courtia Beanland.
His former girlfriend, Chandra Beanland, remembered him
as fun-loving. [1]
Although Thomas sometimes stayed away for periods, Beanland reported him
missing in September 1990. [1, 11] He was last seen on September 24, 1990. [1]

Refinement:
- Remove the phrase "passionate hustler" from the draft because it is vague

- Keep the daughter/family context, with careful attribution.
- Avoid importing the full victim-impact statement into the biography.

10. CURTIS STRAUGHTER, 17-18*
-----------------------------
Curtis Straughter was a high-school dropout who had joined Gay Youth Milwaukee
at age 15. He worked as a nursing assistant and was planning to obtain a
high-school equivalency certificate and attend modeling school. [1] He lived with his
grandmother, and his mother later spoke at sentencing. [1, 8] He was last seen in
early March 1991. [1]

Refinement:
- There is a documented age discrepancy.
- The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective reports 18.
- The FBI Part 01 record gives a date of birth of April 16, 1973, implying
  that he was 17 on the February 18, 1991 date recorded in FBI Part 06 and the
  criminal complaint. The FBI says he was not reported missing until police
  interviewed his grandmother on July 24. UPI [10] calls him 18 at identification.
  [2 (Part 01, PDF p. 25; Part 06, PDF p. 11), 3, 10]
- The final table should not silently choose. Display "17*" or another clearly
  sourced formulation with a note explaining the discrepancy.
- The MJS retrospective gives March 7, 1991; FBI Part 06 and the complaint
  record the grandmother’s last sighting as February 18. UPI [10] reports the
  July identification and reported age, not an independent last-seen date.
  Keep the two last-sighting accounts distinct. [1, 2 (Part 06, PDF p. 11), 3, 10]
  This should be resolved in the Timeline/source notes rather than treated as
  settled without qualification.

11. ERROL LINDSEY, 19

Errol Lindsey was the youngest of six children. A friend recalled that he

to work and came home. [1] Lindsey was last seen on April 7, 1991. [1] His sister,

Refinement:

- The unborn daughter and Rita Isbell's later media criticism can be included

12. ANTHONY "TONY" HUGHES, 31
-----------------------------
Anthony Hughes lived in Madison and had returned to Milwaukee to visit family. [1]
He was deaf after an illness with pneumonia in infancy and communicated using
lip-reading, sign language, and written notes. [1] He was remembered by family as outgoing
and socially connected. [1] His mother, Shirley Hughes, was prominent
in later reporting and court proceedings. [1, 8] Hughes was last seen on May 24,
1991. [1]

Refinement:
- Keep deafness and communication because they are important parts of his
  life, not merely case details.
- Do not use "deaf and mute" as an unqualified label when better wording is
  available; describe the communication circumstances directly.
- Keep his Madison/Milwaukee connection.
- Move later entertainment/media criticism to Impact / Media.

13. KONERAK SINTHASOMPHONE, 14
------------------------------
Konerak Sinthasomphone was one of a large Laotian immigrant family that
settled in Milwaukee in 1980. [1, 6] The family had come from Laos following the 1975 communist takeover; the exact
arrival month remains unverified in this working copy. [1, 6] His father had been a farmer in Laos. [1]
Konerak was about
three when the family arrived, was a freshman at Pulaski High School, and
regularly played soccer at Mitchell Park. [1] He was reported
missing in May 1991. [1]

Konerak's profile should contain a separate short subsection:
    WHY HIS CASE MATTERS

That subsection can briefly note that he was the younger brother of Somsack
Sinthasomphone, whom Dahmer had sexually assaulted in 1988 [2 (Part 01); 3, 9], and that Konerak's
May 27, 1991 police encounter later became central to scrutiny of institutional
response. [7, 10]

Refinement:
- Keep the family-arrival year at 1980. Do not restore the exact month November
  unless the described Milwaukee Sentinel clipping is located and its specific
  FBI Part 04 PDF page/image anchor is verified.
- The exact details of canoe crossing, refugee-camp duration, and Catholic
  relocation assistance should be used only with direct citations to the
  relevant source.
- Detailed police-failure analysis belongs in Systemic Failures.

SOURCE-HANDLING RULE
--------------------
Do not treat a later retrospective article as equivalent to a contemporaneous
official record.

Where the sources disagree:
- identify the disagreement
- prefer the strongest primary/contemporaneous source for factual fields
  where appropriate
- retain the uncertainty in notes when it cannot be resolved
- do not manufacture a false single answer
