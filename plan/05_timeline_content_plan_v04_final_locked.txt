This document constitutes the finalized, locked content plan and claim-level
evidentiary concordance for the Timeline tab (/timeline) and the Master Chronological
Timeline (Table 2) of the Jeffrey Dahmer Case Research Webapp.

All historical dates, legal proceedings, institutional intersections, and victim
milestones have been audited against primary archival records:
  1. Primary FBI FOIA Files: Parts 01 (68 pp.), 03 (60 pp.), and 06 (74 pp.)
  2. Milwaukee County Criminal Charging Documents (Case No. F-912542)
  3. Reconciled Victims Register (Table 1 v04 / 04_victims_content_plan_v05_final_locked.txt)
  4. Federal Judicial Decisions (E.D. Wis. 1992, W.D. Wis. 1995, 7th Cir. 1997)
  5. Contemporary News Wire Records (UPI, AP, Washington Post, New York Times, 1991–1995)

This specification supersedes all prior working copies (v01–v03) and establishes the
permanent chronological navigation spine for the web application.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CORE EDITORIAL & METHODOLOGICAL RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Chronological Spine, Not a Duplicate Essay:
   The Master Timeline answers: What happened? When did it happen? Which institution
   was involved? Why is it historically significant? Deep thematic analyses belong on
   their dedicated pages (Systemic Failures, Psychiatric Profile, Offense Series, Impact).
2. Strict Milestone Separation:
   Last-known sightings, missing-person filings, alleged offense dates, and forensic
   identifications must never be conflated. Where an exact date is unconfirmed by primary
   records, the timeline explicitly notes the qualification or date range.
3. Bidirectional Navigation Hub:
   Each event in Table 2 possesses a unique HTML anchor ID (e.g., `#event-1978-hicks`).
   Topic pages will link directly into these anchors, and the timeline will provide
   reverse links into detailed analyses.
4. Historical Eras:
   The timeline is organized into eight visual historical eras spanning 1960 to 1995.

ERA 1: 1960–1978 — EARLY LIFE AND FIRST KNOWN KILLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### May 21, 1960 — Birth of Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer
- Narrative: Born at Evangelical Deaconess Hospital in Milwaukee, Wisconsin to Lionel and Joyce Dahmer. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27; Pt 03 p. 5)]
- Institutional Contact: Municipal Vital Statistics.
- Significance: Biographical baseline; primary documents record birth in Milwaukee prior to the family's relocation to Iowa and Ohio.
- Cross-Link: `/context#early-life` | Anchor: `#event-1960-birth`

### June 18, 1978 — Murder of Steven Mark Hicks (18*)
- Narrative: Eighteen-year-old Steven Hicks is picked up hitchhiking along Route 21 after attending a concert gathering; brought to Dahmer's parents' residence in Bath Township, Ohio; killed and his remains concealed on the property. [1 (Pt 03 pp. 51–52; Pt 01 p. 60), 3, 4, 5]
- Institutional Contact: Summit County Sheriff / Bath Township Police.
- Milestones Separated: Last seen June 18, 1978; missing report accepted June 24, 1978; remains recovered August 1991; identified September 1991.
- Significance: Origin of the offense series; establishes the nine-year interval preceding the resumption of killings in Wisconsin.
- Cross-Link: `/case#steven-hicks` | Anchor: `#event-1978-hicks`

ERA 2: 1979–1986 — MILITARY SERVICE AND EARLY LEGAL CONTACTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### December 1979 – March 1981 — U.S. Army Service and Alcohol Interventions
- Narrative: Dahmer enlists in the U.S. Army, completes combat medical specialist training at Fort Sam Houston, Texas, and is deployed to the 2nd Battalion, 68th Armor Regiment in Baumholder, West Germany. Military dockets record multiple counseling sessions for chronic alcohol abuse and enrollment in the Army Community Drug and Alcohol Assistance Program (CDAAP). Discharged honorably under Army Regulation 635-200 (Chapter 9) for alcohol abuse rehabilitation failure. [1 (Pt 01 pp. 26–27)]
- Institutional Contact: United States Army / Medical Command.
- Editorial Standard: Do not state "discharged without psychiatric intervention"; records establish documented alcohol rehabilitation programs and multiple formal counseling referrals.
- Significance: Demonstrates early institutional documentation of severe alcohol impairment.
- Cross-Link: `/context#military` | Anchor: `#event-1979-army`

### October 1981 — Bath Township Ramada Inn Arrest
- Narrative: Arrested by Bath Township police officers for disorderly conduct and public intoxication following an incident at a Ramada Inn in Ohio; pleaded guilty and assessed a fine. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27)]
- Institutional Contact: Bath Township Police Department / Municipal Court.
- Significance: Documented misdemeanor arrest between military discharge and relocation to Wisconsin.
- Cross-Link: `/case#prior-record` | Anchor: `#event-1981-bath-arrest`

### August 8, 1982 — State Fair Park Arrest (West Allis, WI)
- Narrative: Arrested by Wisconsin State Fair Park police officers in West Allis for disorderly conduct involving indecent exposure; convicted in municipal court and fined. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27)]
- Institutional Contact: Wisconsin State Fair Park Police.
- Significance: Earliest Wisconsin criminal history record in state and federal dockets.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#early-police` | Anchor: `#event-1982-state-fair`

### September 8, 1986 — Arrest for Lewd and Lascivious Behavior
- Narrative: Arrested by Milwaukee Police Department officers after exposing himself to two young boys near McKinley Beach on Lake Michigan. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27)]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police Department.
- Significance: Initiates formal Milwaukee County criminal court proceedings.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#early-police` | Anchor: `#event-1986-arrest`

### March 10, 1987 — Conviction on Reduced Misdemeanor Charge
- Narrative: The September 1986 felony charge is reduced to misdemeanor disorderly conduct; Dahmer is convicted in Milwaukee County Circuit Court, sentenced to one year of probation, and assessed a $250 fine. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27)]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee County Circuit Court / Wisconsin Probation Division.
- Editorial Standard: Do not claim he was convicted of lewd and lascivious behavior; conviction was solely for disorderly conduct.
- Significance: First entry into the Wisconsin probation supervision system.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#probation` | Anchor: `#event-1987-conviction`

ERA 3: 1987–1989 — RETURN TO KILLING AND PRE-PROBATION PERIOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### September 15, 1987 — Disappearance of Steven Walter Tuomi (24*)
- Narrative: Twenty-four-year-old Steven Tuomi is last seen leaving his employment at Marc's Big Boy restaurant in Milwaukee. [1 (Pt 01 pp. 43, 53), 3]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police / Missing Persons.
- Milestone Separation: Disappearance documented September 15, 1987; no formal MPD missing report on file at time of arrest; no charges filed due to absent corpus delicti.
- Significance: Marks the end of the nine-year pause in homicides.
- Cross-Link: `/victims#steven-tuomi` | Anchor: `#event-1987-tuomi-sighting`

### November 1987* — Homicide of Steven Tuomi at Ambassador Hotel
- Narrative: Dahmer confessed to encountering Tuomi at a local establishment, renting a room at the Ambassador Hotel in Milwaukee, and awakening to find Tuomi deceased. Dahmer placed the event approximately "somewhere around 1986 or 1987" [1 (Pt 03 p. 10)]. Remains were never located. Because corpus delicti could not be independently established under Wisconsin law, District Attorney E. Michael McCann did not file charges. [9, 31]
- Institutional Contact: Ambassador Hotel / Milwaukee County DA.
- Significance: Demonstrates legal thresholds governing Wisconsin homicide prosecutions.
- Cross-Link: `/case#steven-tuomi` | Anchor: `#event-1987-tuomi-homicide`

### January 16, 1988 — Disappearance & Murder of James "Jamie" Doxtator (14*)
- Narrative: Fourteen-year-old Jamie Doxtator disappears in Milwaukee; murdered at Dahmer's grandmother's residence in West Allis. Charged under Count 13 of the amended information as pre-1988 First-Degree Murder. [1 (Pt 01 p. 43, p. 60), 2, 3, 11]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police / Missing Persons.
- Milestone Separation: Last seen January 16, 1988 [1]; FBI Part 01 p. 60 reports disappearance in "spring 1988" [1]; charged August 22, 1991 [2].
- Significance: First fatal offense committed at the West Allis residence.
- Cross-Link: `/case#jamie-doxtator` | Anchor: `#event-1988-doxtator`

### March 24, 1988 — Disappearance & Murder of Richard Guerrero (22*)
- Narrative: Twenty-two-year-old Richard Guerrero disappears in late March 1988; missing report accepted March 29, 1988; murdered at the West Allis residence. Charged under Count 14 as pre-1988 First-Degree Murder. [1 (Pt 01 p. 60; Pt 06 p. 13), 2, 3, 12]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police Department / Missing Persons.
- Milestone Separation: Missing report accepted March 29, 1988; skeletal fragments identified late August 1991; charged August 22, 1991.
- Significance: Second pre-1988 statutory murder charge.
- Cross-Link: `/case#richard-guerrero` | Anchor: `#event-1988-guerrero`

### September 25–27, 1988 — Sexual Assault of Sombat Sinthasomphone & Arrest
- Narrative: Thirteen-year-old Sombat Sinthasomphone is drugged and sexually assaulted at 225 24th Street; escapes and reports offense to family. Dahmer is arrested on September 27, 1988 and charged with second-degree sexual assault and enticing a child for immoral purposes. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27), 30]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police Department (Case No. 88-66779) / Circuit Court.
- Significance: Most critical pre-1991 criminal justice intersection; leads directly to Dahmer's 1989 felony conviction and probation.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#sinthasomphone-1988` | Anchor: `#event-1988-assault`

### March 25–26, 1989 — Murder of Anthony Lee Sears (24)
- Narrative: Twenty-four-year-old Anthony Sears is last seen socializing with friends; leaves with Dahmer in the early morning hours of March 26 and is murdered. At the time, Dahmer is on pretrial release awaiting sentencing for the 1988 child sexual assault. Charged under Count 01 of the complaint. [1 (Pt 01 p. 24; Pt 06 p. 12), 2, 3]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police / Pretrial Services.
- Significance: Homicide committed while Dahmer is under active judicial processing awaiting felony sentencing.
- Cross-Link: `/case#anthony-sears` | Anchor: `#event-1989-sears`

### May 24, 1989 — Sentencing for 1988 Sexual Assault (Judge Gardner)
- Narrative: Convicted of second-degree sexual assault and enticing a child; sentenced by Judge William Gardner to one year in the Milwaukee County House of Correction with Huber work-release privileges, plus five years of consecutive probation. [1 (Pt 01 p. 27), 26, 30]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee County Circuit Court / Probation Division.
- Editorial Standard: Do not state "given five years probation alone"; the custodial work-release term and probation were separate components of the sentence.
- Significance: Establishes the formal probation supervision structure in effect during the 1990–1991 homicides.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#sentencing-1989` | Anchor: `#event-1989-sentencing`

ERA 4: 1990 — APARTMENT 213 AND ESCALATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### March 1990 — Release to Probation Supervision
- Narrative: Dahmer completes his custodial term at the House of Correction and begins serving his five-year probation sentence under probation agent Donna Chester. [26]
- Institutional Contact: Wisconsin Division of Corrections / Department of Health and Social Services.
- Significance: Perpetrator placed on maximum community supervision status.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#probation` | Anchor: `#event-1990-probation-release`

### May 1990 — Relocation to Oxford Apartments (Apartment 213)
- Narrative: Dahmer leases Unit 213 at 924 N. 25th Street (Oxford Apartments). Under Wisconsin maximum probation rules, home visits were required every 30 days unless formally waived by a supervisor; Donna Chester requested and received supervisory waivers, conducting all contacts in her office. [1 (Pt 01 p. 2), 26]
- Institutional Contact: Oxford Apartments / State Probation Division.
- Editorial Standard: Do not claim agent "violated mandatory quarterly rules"; rules permitted supervisory waivers, which were documented in court records.
- Significance: Physical locus for 12 murders; absence of home visits leaves apartment unexamined.
- Cross-Link: `/topics/systemic-failures#home-visit-waivers` | Anchor: `#event-1990-oxford-move`

### May 29, 1990 — Murder of Raymond "Ricky Beeks" Smith (32*)
- Narrative: Raymond Smith is last seen in Milwaukee; murdered inside Apartment 213; charged under Count 02 of the complaint. [1 (Pt 01 p. 25; Pt 06 p. 11), 2, 3]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee Police / Missing Persons.
- Significance: First homicide committed inside Apartment 213.

- Narrative: District Attorney files amended criminal complaint adding eight homicide counts (Hughes, Sinthasomphone, Turner, Weinberger, Lacy, and earlier cases), bringing total Wisconsin charges to 12. [2, 9]

ERA 6: 1992 — TRIAL, SENTENCING AND IMMEDIATE AFTERMATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### January 13, 1992 — Plea of Guilty but Insane Entered
- Narrative: At formal arraignment, defense counsel Gerald Boyle enters pleas of guilty but not guilty by reason of mental disease or defect to all 15 Wisconsin murder counts before Judge Laurence Gram Jr. [18, 19]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee County Circuit Court.
- Significance: Eliminates guilt phase; restricts trial strictly to defendant's sanity.
- Cross-Link: `/case#trial-plea` | Anchor: `#event-1992-insanity-plea`

### January 27 – February 15, 1992 — Bifurcated Sanity Trial
- Narrative: Two-week televised trial on criminal responsibility. Defense presents psychiatric testimony (Dr. Fred Berlin, Dr. Judith Becker) arguing Dahmer suffered from severe necrophilia and borderline disorder depriving him of capacity to conform conduct. State experts (Dr. Phillip Resnick, Dr. Park Dietz) testify Dahmer engaged in extensive premeditation and controlled impulses when police were present. On February 15, jury votes 10–2 to find Dahmer legally sane on all 15 counts. [19, 20]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee County Circuit Court / Jury.
- Significance: Rejection of insanity defense; application of American Law Institute standard in Wisconsin.
- Cross-Link: `/case#sanity-trial` | Anchor: `#event-1992-sanity-trial`

### February 17, 1992 — Wisconsin Sentencing (15 Life Terms)
- Narrative: Judge Laurence Gram Jr. sentences Dahmer to 15 consecutive life terms without parole eligibility for 936 years. Moving victim impact statements delivered by Rita Isbell, Shirley Hughes, Dorothy Straughter, and Carolyn Smith. [19, 21]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee County Circuit Court.
- Significance: Concludes Wisconsin criminal prosecution.
- Cross-Link: `/case#sentencing-1992` | Anchor: `#event-1992-sentencing`

### May 1, 1992 — Ohio Guilty Plea and 16th Life Sentence
- Narrative: Dahmer appears in Summit County Court of Common Pleas before Judge William Spicer, pleads guilty to the aggravated murder of Steven Hicks, and receives his 16th consecutive life sentence. [22]
- Institutional Contact: Summit County Court of Common Pleas, Ohio.
- Significance: Finalizes criminal proceedings across all jurisdictions.
- Cross-Link: `/case#ohio-sentencing` | Anchor: `#event-1992-ohio-sentencing`

ERA 7: 1992–1995 — POST-TRIAL AFTERMATH & CIVIL RECKONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### November 14–16, 1992 — Demolition of Oxford Apartments
- Narrative: The Campus Circle Project (nonprofit redevelopment entity affiliated with Marquette University) purchases the 49-unit Oxford Apartments at 924 N. 25th Street for $325,000; building is demolished and cleared for green space to prevent macabre commercial tourism. [23, 24]
- Institutional Contact: Campus Circle Project / City of Milwaukee.
- Editorial Standard: Do not attribute purchase to municipal redevelopment authorities; Campus Circle Project was the private nonprofit purchaser.
- Significance: Physical elimination of crime scene.
- Cross-Link: `/impact#oxford-demolition` | Anchor: `#event-1992-oxford-demolition`

### 1992–1995 — Federal Civil Rights Litigation
- Narrative: Federal Section 1983 litigation: In *Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee*, 785 F. Supp. 1343 (E.D. Wis. 1992), Judge Terence Evans denies qualified immunity to responding officers on equal protection claims alleging differential treatment based on race and sexual orientation (City later settled for $850,000 in 1995). In *Weinberger v. State of Wisconsin*, 906 F. Supp. 485 (W.D. Wis. 1995), aff'd 105 F.3d 1182 (7th Cir. 1997), federal courts dismiss claims against state probation officials, holding no affirmative constitutional duty existed to protect private individuals from third-party acts. [25, 26]
- Institutional Contact: U.S. District Court (E.D. Wis. & W.D. Wis.) / 7th Circuit Court of Appeals.
- Significance: Judicial examination of equal protection, municipal liability, and probation duties.
- Cross-Link: `/impact#civil-litigation` | Anchor: `#event-1992-federal-litigation`

### April 28, 1994 — Reinstatement of Responding Police Officers
- Narrative: Reserve Circuit Judge Robert J. Parins, acting as an arbitrator/appellate judge, overturns Police Chief Philip Arreola's dismissal of Officers John Balcerzak and Joseph Gabrish, finding termination disproportionately severe. Orders officers reinstated with full back pay of $55,000 each. Balcerzak was later elected president of the Milwaukee Police Association in 2005. [27]
- Institutional Contact: Milwaukee County Circuit Court / Fire and Police Commission.
- Significance: Deep civic controversy regarding police accountability and civil service protections.
- Cross-Link: `/impact#officer-reinstatement` | Anchor: `#event-1994-officer-reinstatement`

ERA 8: 1994 — PERPETRATOR DEATH IN CUSTODY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

### November 28, 1994 — Fatal Assault of Jeffrey Dahmer at Columbia Correctional
- Narrative: While assigned to an unsupervised three-inmate cleaning detail in the gymnasium of Columbia Correctional Institution in Portage, Wisconsin, Dahmer is beaten to death with a 20-inch metal weightlifting bar by fellow inmate Christopher Scarver. Inmate Jesse Anderson is also fatally beaten in the adjacent locker room. [28, 29]
- Institutional Contact: Wisconsin Department of Corrections / Columbia Correctional Institution.
- Editorial Standard: Do not state "security failures allowed the attack" as an established conclusion; document the attack and unsupervised assignment as factual events without speculative motive claims.
- Significance: Final institutional milestone of the case; prompts extensive state investigations into maximum-security correctional supervision.
- Cross-Link: `/case#perpetrator-death` | Anchor: `#event-1994-prison-death`
