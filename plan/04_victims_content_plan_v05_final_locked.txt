CORE EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Victims section restores historical focus to the people who were killed: who they
were, their backgrounds, family ties, artistic talents, personal styles, and aspirations.

The page strictly adheres to the following editorial standards:
1. Human-Centered Focus: Avoid defining victims solely by forensic details or case numbers.
2. Chronological Ordering: Arranged chronologically by the best-supported last-known
   sighting or contact period, not by perceived importance or judicial hierarchy.
3. Strict Separation of Chronological Milestones:
   - Milestone 1: Last-Known Sighting / Contact Date (reported by family, friends, or witnesses)
   - Milestone 2: Missing-Person Report Date (formal law enforcement acceptance)
   - Milestone 3: Alleged Offense Date (prosecutorial complaint / information allegations)
   - Milestone 4: Official Forensic Identification Date (dental, fingerprint, or anthropological)
4. Age Convention: Displayed age is age on the best-supported last-known date, calculated
   from documented birth dates. An asterisk (*) indicates a documented birth date or
   age-reference discrepancy (e.g., where press reported age at post-arrest identification
   in 1991 rather than recalculating age at disappearance).
5. Retirement of the Provisional Dagger (†): Following primary verification of David
   Thomas's DOB (December 21, 1967; age 22 at disappearance), 100% of all 17 victims
   possess primary DOB anchors in official investigative records. The dagger is retired.
6. Non-Sensational Tone: No graphic murder details or sensationalism.
7. Architectural Separation: Media controversies, entertainment debates (e.g., Netflix
   portrayals), and Dahmer-themed tourism objections belong in the Impact tab (/impact).
   Deep systemic police and probation failures belong in Systemic Failures (/topics/systemic-failures).

METHODOLOGICAL FRAMEWORK & ITEMIZED AUDIT NOTES
================================================================================

1. Distinction of Record Milestones:
In case research, four distinct chronological milestones must never be conflated:
   a. Last Known Date: The final reported date family, friends, or witnesses saw or spoke
      with the victim alive; may remain approximate or disputed.
   b. Missing Person Report Date: The date local law enforcement formally accepted a report.
   c. Alleged Offense Date: The approximate date specified in charging complaints, derived
      largely from Dahmer's interrogations.
   d. Forensic Identification Date: The date authorities established positive identity
      (fingerprints, dental records, forensic anthropology, or photo confirmation).

2. Itemized Victim Evidentiary Notes:

* Steven Hicks:
  - Last Known Date: Supported by retrospective accounts [1] and corroborated in FBI Vault
    Part 03, PDF Page 52 (field report CV 7-MW-26057, Page 2): "HICKS was last seen on June 18, 1978."
    Part 01, PDF p. 60 documents a June 24, 1978 Ohio missing-person report (a distinct event).
  - Birth Date Records: FBI Part 03, PDF p. 51 and Part 01, PDF p. 39 list DOB as June 22, 1959.
    FBI Part 01, PDF p. 25 lists June 27, 1959. Both establish his age as 18 on June 18, 1978.
  - Forensic Recovery: Remains recovered in Bath Township, Ohio, in August 1991; identified
    in September 1991 via dental/skeletal comparison [4, 17]. Displayed as 18*.

* Steven Tuomi:
  - Age Reference Discrepancy (24*): Milwaukee Journal Sentinel [1] and contemporary press

  - Death Chronology & Legal Status: Dahmer's confession in FBI Vault Part 03, PDF Page 10

    1986 or 1987." Because no physical remains were recovered, District Attorney E. Michael

  - Descriptors: FBI Part 01, PDF Page 60 describes him as an "Indian male," consistent with

THE 17 FACT-CHECKED BIOGRAPHICAL PROFILES
================================================================================

1. STEVEN HICKS, 18*
-------------------
Biographical Profile:
Steven Mark Hicks was a recent high-school graduate from Coventry Township, Ohio. [1]
His father, Richard Hicks, described him as a deeply empathetic and gentle young man;
in an Associated Press account, his father recalled that Steven had become inconsolably
distraught after shooting a rabbit on a hunting trip, illustrating his aversion to violence. [1]
Steven was an avid music enthusiast and was hitchhiking toward a rock concert at Chippewa
Lake Park when he accepted a ride from Dahmer. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact: June 18, 1978. FBI Vault Part 03, PDF Page 52 explicitly records:
  "HICKS was last seen on June 18, 1978." [2]
- Missing-Person Report: Filed June 24, 1978 with the Bath Township / Summit County authorities
  (FBI Vault Part 01, PDF p. 60). [2]
- Forensic Recovery & Identification: Skeletal remains were recovered from Bath Township in
  August 1991 and positively identified in September 1991 through dental and radiographic
  comparison. [4, 17]
- Legal Disposition: In May 1992, Dahmer pleaded guilty to aggravated murder in Summit County
  Court of Common Pleas; Richard Hicks addressed the court regarding the permanent loss
  inflicted upon the family. [5]
- Birth Date & Age: FBI Part 03, PDF p. 51 and Part 01, PDF p. 39 list DOB June 22, 1959;
  Part 01, PDF p. 25 lists June 27, 1959. Under both official records, he was 18 years old
  on June 18, 1978. Displayed as 18*. [1, 2]


2. STEVEN TUOMI, 24*
-------------------
Biographical Profile:
Steven Walter Tuomi grew up in Ontonagon in Michigan's Upper Peninsula and later moved to
Milwaukee, where he was employed as a short-order cook at a local restaurant. [1] Former
high-school classmates remembered him as a quiet, thoughtful, and artistic individual;
one classmate specifically recalled an intricate stained-glass lamp that Tuomi had
designed and crafted in art class. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact: September 15, 1987. The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective [1]
  records that Tuomi was last seen or heard from in Milwaukee on this date.
- Absence of Missing Report & Corpus Delicti: Tuomi was not formally registered in MPD
  missing-persons systems at the time of his disappearance. Following his arrest in 1991,
  Dahmer confessed in FBI interviews (Part 03, PDF Page 10) to killing Tuomi at the
  Ambassador Hotel "somewhere around 1986 or 1987." [2] Because no physical remains were ever
  recovered, Milwaukee County District Attorney E. Michael McCann concluded there was
  insufficient independent evidence to satisfy legal corpus delicti; consequently, no
  criminal charge was filed in Wisconsin courts. [15, 16]
- Birth Date & Age Recalculation: Retrospective press and 1991 contemporary news reported
  Tuomi's age as 28, which represented his presumed age during the 1991 investigation.
  FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Pages 43 and 53 record detectives' investigative belief that his
  DOB was December 19, 1962. Calculating age from this anchor to September 15, 1987 yields
  age 24 (24 years, 8 months, 27 days). Displayed as 24*. [1, 2]


3. JAMES "JAMIE" DOXTATOR, 14*
-----------------------------
Biographical Profile:
James "Jamie" Doxtator was the eldest of four children and was of Stockbridge and Oneida
ancestry, residing in Milwaukee. [1] Standing nearly six feet tall, he was remembered by
his mother, Debbie Vega, as a playful, energetic teenager who loved shooting pool, riding
his bicycle through the neighborhood, and spending time with his brothers and sisters. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact & Disappearance: The MJS retrospective records his last sighting as
  January 16, 1988 [1], while contemporary UPI reporting documents his disappearance as
  January 1988 [14]. In contrast, FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 60 notes his disappearance
  occurred in the "spring of 1988." [2]
- Investigative Identification & Charging: Identified publicly in mid-August 1991 as
  Dahmer's 17th victim [14]. A formal count of first-degree murder was included in the
  state's final 15-count information on August 22, 1991. [3]
- Birth Date & Age Discrepancy: FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 43 documents detectives' notes
  recording DOB as March 1, 1973. If he disappeared in January 1988, he was 14 years,
  10 months old; if in the spring after March 1, he had turned 15. The table displays 14*
  to reflect this unresolved chronological divergence. [1, 2, 14]


4. RICHARD GUERRERO, 22*
-------------------------
Biographical Profile:
Richard Guerrero was born and raised in Milwaukee within a close-knit Mexican-American family. [1]
His sister, Janie Hagen, described him as deeply dedicated to his family, recalling that he
routinely assisted their mother with daily household chores and grocery shopping, and
frequently babysat his young infant niece. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Disappearance Period & Missing Report: Guerrero disappeared in late March 1988. A formal
  missing-person report was accepted by the Milwaukee Police Department on March 29, 1988
  (FBI Vault Part 06, PDF Page 13). [2] An exact independent last-sighting day remains
  unestablished in surviving police dockets.
- Charging & Identification: Charged under Count 02 of the amended information on
  August 22, 1991 as pre-1988 First-Degree Murder. Remains identified through forensic
  anthropological matching in late August 1991. [2, 3]
- Birth Date & Age Recalculation: FBI Vault Part 06, PDF Page 13 and Part 01, PDF Page 60
  record his date of birth as December 12, 1965. In March 1988, he was 22 years, 3 months old.
  The MJS retrospective reported age 25 (his age in 1991), while contemporary UPI accounts [11]
  reported age 23. Displayed as 22* to preserve the documented age-at-disappearance. [1, 2, 11]


5. ANTHONY SEARS, 24
--------------------
Biographical Profile:
Anthony Lee Sears was an ambitious young professional in Milwaukee who had recently earned
a promotion to restaurant manager at a local Baker's Square. [1] Energetic and charismatic,
he harbored aspirations of pursuing a career in commercial modeling. [1] He was diligently
saving earnings with the intention of relocating outside Milwaukee, and his mother recalled
that he had discussed plans to marry his longtime girlfriend once he was financially settled. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact & Offense Date: FBI Vault Part 06, PDF Page 12 records that Sears was
  last seen at home on March 25, 1989 at 9:30 a.m.; he was reported missing on March 29, 1989. [2]
  Complaint witness Jeffrey Connor testified that they socialized at a tavern on the night of
  March 25, and Sears left with Dahmer in the early morning hours of March 26. Count 01 of the
  complaint alleges the homicide occurred on or about March 26, 1989. [3]
- Identification: Positively identified from physical evidence on July 26, 1991 (FBI Part 01,
  PDF Page 24). [2]
- Birth Date & Age: FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 24 and Part 06, PDF Page 12 record DOB as
  January 28, 1965. On March 25–26, 1989, he was exactly 24 years, 1 month, 25 days old.
  Displayed as 24 without marker. [1, 2]


6. RAYMOND "RICKY BEEKS" SMITH, 32*
-----------------------------------
Biographical Profile:
Raymond Lamont Smith, known affectionately to relatives and lifelong friends as "Ricky Beeks,"
was living with his half-sister in Milwaukee following his release from incarceration. [1]
He was the devoted father of a ten-year-old daughter who resided in Rockford, Illinois. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact & Apartment 213 Context: The MJS retrospective records his last sighting
  as May 29, 1990. [1] His homicide represented the first killing committed inside Dahmer's
  residence at Apartment 213, Oxford Apartments (924 N. 25th Street).
- Missing Report & Charging: FBI Part 06, PDF Page 11 records that no formal missing-person
  report was on file prior to Dahmer's arrest. Homicide charged in Count 02 of the amended
  criminal complaint. [2, 3] Positively identified through fingerprint analysis on July 25, 1991
  (FBI Part 01, PDF Page 25). [2]
- Birth Date & Age Recalculation: FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 25 and Part 06, PDF Page 11
  record his DOB as August 10, 1957. On May 29, 1990, he was 32 years, 9 months, 19 days old.
  Contemporary press [13] and the retrospective [1] reported his age as 33, reflecting his
  age at post-arrest identification. Displayed as 32*. [1, 2]


7. EDWARD "EDDIE" SMITH, 27*
---------------------------
Biographical Profile:
Edward "Eddie" W. Smith was an aspiring fashion model raised in Milwaukee in a loving,
devout Christian home. [1, 8] Highly regarded for his vibrant personality and distinctive
sense of style, friends remembered him for wearing striking head wraps and turbans, which
earned him the affectionate community moniker "the Sheikh." [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact & Telephone Calls: UPI reporting [11] records that Eddie's sister last
  saw him at her residence on June 14, 1990. A formal missing-person report was accepted on
  June 23, 1990 (FBI Part 06, PDF Page 13). [2] His sister later recounted receiving an
  anonymous telephone call, believed to have originated from Dahmer, stating Eddie was dead. [1, 11]
- Identification & Court Testimony: Positively identified on July 29, 1991 (Part 06, PDF p. 13). [2]
  At the 1992 sentencing, family members delivered moving victim impact statements attesting
  to his character and familial bonds. [8]
- Birth Date & Age Recalculation: FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 39 and Part 06, PDF Page 13
  record DOB as August 2, 1962. On June 14, 1990, he was 27 years, 10 months, 12 days old.
  Contemporary reports listed him as 28 (his age during the July 1991 investigation, just
  weeks prior to his 29th birthday). Displayed as 27*. [1, 2, 11]


8. ERNEST MILLER, 23*
---------------------
Biographical Profile:
Ernest Miller was an accomplished performing artist who graduated from the prestigious
Milwaukee High School of the Arts. [1] A gifted vocalist and expressive dancer, he was
preparing to relocate to Chicago to attend an arts college and pursue a professional dance
career. [1] His aunt, Vivian Miller, recalled that he had demonstrated natural artistic talent
from early childhood, frequently performing and singing in church choirs. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Last Known Contact & Chronology Separation: MJS and UPI [1, 11] report he was last seen
  leaving home on Sunday evening, September 2, 1990. Sworn complaint records [3] and FBI
  Part 06, PDF Page 12 note his departure at approximately 1:30 a.m. on September 3, 1990.
  A missing-person report was filed by relatives on September 4, 1990. Count 03 alleges
  the offense occurred on or about September 3, 1990. [3]
- Identification: Positively identified from recovered remains on July 25, 1991 (FBI Part 01,
  PDF Page 24). [2]
- Birth Date & Age Recalculation: FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 39 and Part 06, PDF Page 12

13. KONERAK SINTHASOMPHONE, 14
------------------------------
Biographical Profile:
Konerak Sinthasomphone was a member of a large Laotian immigrant family that settled in
Milwaukee in 1980 after fleeing political upheaval in Laos. [1, 6, 19] His father had been
a farmer in Laos. [1] Arriving in the United States around age three, Konerak attended
Milwaukee Public Schools and was a freshman at Pulaski High School. [1] He was remembered
by peers as an athletic teenager who loved playing soccer with friends at Mitchell Park. [1]

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
- Chronology of May 1991: Left his family residence on May 26, 1991. On the early morning of
  May 27, 1991, an intoxicated and disoriented Konerak escaped from Apartment 213 onto the street,
  where he was assisted by bystander Sandra Smith and Nicole Childress. [1, 7, 20] Milwaukee
  police officers responded, accepted Dahmer's false explanation that Konerak was an adult domestic
  partner, and escorted him back into Dahmer's apartment. He was murdered shortly thereafter.
  His father formally filed a missing-person report on May 28, 1991. [2]
- Identification & Legal Repercussions: Positively identified on July 24, 1991 through dental
  matching (FBI Part 01, PDF Page 24). [2] The incident sparked immediate nationwide outrage,
  resulting in the suspension of responding officers and formal inquiries into systemic bias. [7, 20, 22]
- Birth Date & Age: FBI Vault Part 01, PDF Page 24 and Part 06, PDF Page 10 record DOB as
  December 2, 1976. On May 27, 1991, he was exactly 14 years, 5 months, 25 days old.
  Displayed as 14. [1, 2]

[Callout Box / Sub-Section: Why His Case Matters]
The murder of Konerak Sinthasomphone remains the most widely scrutinized institutional failure
of the case. Konerak was the younger brother of Sombat Sinthasomphone, whom Dahmer had sexually
assaulted in September 1988—a conviction that had placed Dahmer on state probation. [2, 3]
Despite phone calls from neighborhood residents pleading for medical intervention, responding
officers failed to verify Konerak's identity, age, or criminal records. Detailed documentary
scrutiny of the 911 dispatch, radio logs, and administrative hearings is maintained under
Systemic Failures (/topics/systemic-failures).

ABOUT THE RECORD: ARCHIVAL METHODOLOGY & EVIDENCE STANDARDS
================================================================================
The documentary reconstruction of the lives of the seventeen victims is shaped by the uneven
nature of surviving public and archival records. In accordance with serious historical standards:

1. Asymmetry of Surviving Records:
   Some victims' families spoke extensively with contemporary journalists and court reporters,
   preserving rich biographical memories of childhood, hobbies, and personal aspirations.
   For other individuals, surviving documentation is primarily confined to missing-person dockets,
   fingerprint transmittals, and cold court records. The archive deliberately leaves gaps unfilled
   rather than manufacturing speculative narrative detail.

2. Source Hierarchy & Cross-Verification:
   - Primary Official Records: FBI FOIA files (Parts 01, 03, 04, 06), Milwaukee County criminal
     complaints, court sentencing transcripts, and coroner reports form the evidentiary baseline.
   - Contemporary Press Reports (1991–1992): Wire services (AP, UPI) and local newspapers
     (Milwaukee Journal, Milwaukee Sentinel, Chicago Tribune) provide immediate temporal reporting
     but frequently reflect early investigative confusion (such as reporting age at identification).
   - Retrospective Accounts (2022+): Comprehensive retrospectives (e.g., Milwaukee Journal
     Sentinel 30-year retrospective) provide invaluable long-term family retrospectives but must
     be checked against primary records for precise dates.

3. Discrepancy Transparency:
   Where authentic primary records diverge (such as conflicting birth dates recorded in FBI
   dockets for Steven Hicks, Jeremiah Weinberger, and Oliver Lacy), the webapp does not silently
   select one version. Both records are transparently documented in the audit notes, and the
   visible age is qualified with an asterisk (*).
