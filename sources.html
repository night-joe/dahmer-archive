Research Governance • Evidentiary Protocols

Editorial Methodology & Research Standards

1. Epistemological Certainty Tiers

Every factual statement in this archive is evaluated against four distinct certainty tiers to maintain epistemological rigor:

  • [ESTABLISHED]: Confirmed by primary physical evidence, verified judicial dockets, certified charging instruments, or uncontested sworn testimony under cross-examination.
  • [DOCUMENTED]: Sourced to contemporaneous investigative reporting, police incident reports, institutional correspondence, or verified municipal filings.
  • [ATTRIBUTED]: First-person confessions, psychiatric interview self-reports, or retrospective memoirs. Always explicitly framed in prose as statements rather than objective historical facts.
  • [DISPUTED]: Conflicting statements, retracted academic claims (e.g. Jentzen et al. 1994 "Destructive Hostility"), or unresolved historical discrepancies (e.g. D-01 through D-05). Explicitly noted with divergent accounts presented transparently.

2. Statutory Distinctions

Wisconsin homicide law underwent a structural legislative overhaul effective January 1, 1989 under 1987 Wisconsin Act 399. Homicides committed prior to 1989 (Steven Hicks, Steven Tuomi, James Doxtator, Richard Guerrero) were charged and adjudicated under former Wis. Stat. § 940.01 ("First-Degree Murder"). All 13 homicides from 1989 onwards were charged under repealed and recreated Wis. Stat. § 940.01(1) ("First-Degree Intentional Homicide").

3. Sanity Phase Adjudication

Under Wis. Stat. § 971.15 and § 971.165, Dahmer entered pleas of guilty but not guilty by reason of mental disease or defect to 15 Wisconsin homicide counts, waiving the guilt phase. The jury trial in Milwaukee County Circuit Court (Hon. Laurence C. Gram, Jr. presiding) was strictly an adjudication of legal responsibility during the offenses. Under Wis. Stat. § 805.09(2), the verdict required a 10–2 (five-sixths) concurrence. Describing Dahmer as "convicted by a jury of murder" is legally inaccurate and prohibited; he pled guilty to the killings, and the jury found him legally sane.

4. Non-Graphic Evidentiary Standard

In strict accordance with project ethics standards, zero crime-scene photographs, autopsy imagery, or photographs of human remains are published. All visual plates depict official documents, public judicial proceedings, contemporary streetscapes, or analytical maps, maintaining victim dignity.

5. Primary Public Record Repositories

The documentary spine of this archive draws from six primary public record repositories:

  1. Milwaukee County Circuit Court Records Department (Milwaukee County Courthouse, Milwaukee, WI): Criminal complaint (Case No. F-912542), preliminary hearing transcripts, 15 homicide charging schedules, and sanity-phase trial exhibits.
  2. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Electronic Reading Room / The Vault: 19-part declassified Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) archive on Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer.
  3. Wisconsin Historical Society Library & Archives (Madison, WI): Broadcast news archives (WTMJ-TV, WITI-TV, WISN-TV), contemporary press morgues, and civic records.
  4. Milwaukee Public Library Central Branch Periodical Archives: Complete microfilm holdings of The Milwaukee Journal and The Milwaukee Sentinel (July 1991 – December 1994).
  5. U.S. District Court for the Eastern & Western Districts of Wisconsin: Dockets and published opinions for Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee, Balcerzak & Gabrish v. City of Milwaukee, and Weinberger v. State of Wisconsin.
  6. National Archives and Records Administration (NARA), Chicago Regional Facility: Regional civil rights compliance documentation and federal agency records.
