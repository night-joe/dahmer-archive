CORE PURPOSE
------------
The Offense Series should explain the documented development of Dahmer's
offending pattern without reproducing graphic material.

It should answer:
- How did each victim encounter Dahmer?
- What approach or method is documented?
- What happened afterward?
- What institutional contact, if any, occurred?
- What does the event contribute to understanding the broader pattern?

The page should be detailed enough to reconstruct the case but should not become
a graphic catalogue of violence.

EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
-------------------
The series should be about:
    event + pattern + evidence + aftermath

It should NOT be about:
    gore + spectacle + increasingly graphic descriptions

No graphic descriptions of:
- mutilation
- sexual acts
- anatomy beyond what is necessary for forensic/historical understanding
- remains

Use neutral wording such as:
    drugged
    strangled
    killed
    dismembered
    remains were disposed of
    remains were preserved
    forensic evidence was recovered

Where a method is unusually important to the historical record, describe it
briefly and clinically.

THREE-PHASE FRAMEWORK
---------------------
The user's three-phase framework is retained because it gives the page a clear
editorial structure.

Important wording rule:
These are a research/organizational framework for this website, not a formal
criminological classification.

1. PHASE ONE — BATH TOWNSHIP, OHIO
   1978

2. PHASE TWO — GRANDMOTHER'S WEST ALLIS HOME
   1987–1989

3. PHASE THREE — OXFORD APARTMENTS, APARTMENT 213
   1990–1991

This framework is supported by the documented locations and chronology, but
the page should avoid claiming that these phases were objectively defined by
criminologists.

PHASE ONE — BATH TOWNSHIP, OHIO
-------------------------------
Steven Hicks — June 18, 1978

Approach / Background:
Dahmer, recently graduated from high school, encountered 18-year-old Steven
Hicks while Hicks was hitchhiking to a concert.

Documented method:
Dahmer later confessed that Hicks was killed at the family residence. His
account and later biographical sources describe a combination of blunt-force
attack and strangulation.

Aftermath:
Dahmer dismembered Hicks's remains and initially buried them on the family
property. He later exhumed the remains, further processed them, and scattered
the bone fragments.

Institutional contact:
Hicks was a missing person. The disappearance did not lead to a homicide
investigation connecting Dahmer to the death at the time.

Significance:
This is the first known homicide in the 17-killing confession and the only
known killing before the long gap from 1978 to 1987.

Important refinement:
- Use "first known homicide" rather than "beginning of a serial pattern" as
  an absolute claim; the latter is a later analytical framing.
- June 18 should be described as the last known date/encounter unless the final
  source supports a precise death date.
- Keep disposal details brief.

PHASE TWO — GRANDMOTHER'S WEST ALLIS HOME
-----------------------------------------
Timeframe:
1987–1989

Important location correction:
Dahmer continued living with his grandmother, Catherine, in West Allis until
he moved to 924 N. 25th Street in May 1990. The phrase "1987–1989" refers to
the period containing the four known killings at or connected with the
grandmother's home; the residence itself continued into 1990.

STEVEN TUOMI — SEPTEMBER 1987
-----------------------------
Approach:
Dahmer met Steven Tuomi in Milwaukee and took him to a room at the Ambassador
Hotel.

Documented method:
According to Dahmer's later account to investigators, he intended to drug
Tuomi but awoke after drinking to find Tuomi dead beside him. Dahmer said he
did not remember the killing.

Aftermath:
He purchased a large suitcase, transported Tuomi's body to his grandmother's
home, and dismembered and disposed of the remains there.

Institutional contact:
Tuomi was reported missing. No homicide charge was filed against Dahmer for
Tuomi's death because investigators did not have sufficient physical evidence.

Significance:
This marks the return to homicide after the nine-year gap.

Important uncertainty:
The exact date of Tuomi's death is not independently established. The FBI
interview record has Dahmer placing it "somewhere around 1986 or 1987," while
Tuomi's last known sighting was September 15, 1987.

Do not present a precise November 20, 1987 death date as settled fact without a
source capable of supporting that precision.

JAMES "JAMIE" DOXTATOR — JANUARY 1988
-------------------------------------
Approach:
Dahmer encountered 14-year-old James Doxtator in Milwaukee and offered money
for nude photographs.

Documented method:
Dahmer drugged and strangled Doxtator.

Aftermath:
The body was kept in the grandmother's basement for a period before
dismemberment and disposal.

Institutional contact:
Doxtator was a missing person.

Significance:
Represents the beginning of a repeatable Milwaukee approach involving
enticement, drugging and strangulation.

RICHARD GUERRERO — MARCH 1988
------------------------------
Approach:
Dahmer encountered Richard Guerrero in Milwaukee and offered money in the
context of photographs.

Documented method:
Guerrero was drugged and strangled.

Aftermath:
His remains were dismembered and disposed of.

Institutional contact:
Guerrero was reported missing; his family later pursued outside investigative
help.

Significance:
Adds another unresolved disappearance to the developing pattern.

ANTHONY SEARS — MARCH 1989
---------------------------
Approach:
Dahmer met Anthony Sears at a bar and brought him to his grandmother's home,
where photographs and sexual contact were part of the pretext.

Documented method:
Dahmer drugged and strangled Sears.

Aftermath:
Dahmer preserved some of Sears's remains. His preserved head and genitalia were
later found in Apartment 213 after the 1991 arrest.

Institutional contact:
Sears was reported missing.

Significance:
This is the point at which trophy retention becomes an especially important
documented part of the pattern.

Refinement:
- The "first permanent trophy retention" is supported by later accounts and can
  be described as a documented development in Dahmer's behavior.
- Avoid reproducing detailed preservation techniques.
- Do not say the skull itself was "kept as a trophy" unless the source wording
  supports that interpretation; "preserved remains were retained" is cleaner.

PHASE THREE — OXFORD APARTMENTS, APARTMENT 213
----------------------------------------------
Timeframe:
May 1990 – July 1991

Location:
924 North 25th Street, Apartment 213, Milwaukee.

The final phase contains twelve known killings.

The transition should be described as:
- a new living environment
- continued enticement and drugging
- more systematic storage/preservation of remains
- increasing frequency of killings
- additional experimentation
- accumulation of evidence
- eventual inability to conceal the activity

Avoid the broad evaluative phrase:
    "sharp jump in both frequency and severity"

Prefer:
    "The frequency of killings increased substantially, while the documented
    methods of concealment, preservation and experimentation also became more
    elaborate."

WHY THE APARTMENT MATTERS
-------------------------
By the time of the arrest, investigators recovered the remains of eleven
victims from Apartment 213. The FBI documents extensive physical evidence,
including photographs, body parts and a 55-gallon drum containing several
torsos. The crime-scene record provides the physical evidence base for the
later reconstruction of the twelve Apartment 213 killings.

Do not over-describe the crime-scene contents in the introductory Phase Three
section. Detailed forensic evidence can be handled in Investigation & Arrest.

STANDARD ENTRY STRUCTURE
------------------------
Each entry should use the same editorial pattern:

    VICTIM NAME
    date · location

    BACKGROUND / ENCOUNTER
    How Dahmer encountered the victim and what is documented about the
    initial pretext.

    METHOD / PATTERN
    Concise, non-graphic description of incapacitation and killing method
    where reliably established.

    AFTERMATH
    What happened to the body/remains, whether anything was retained, and
    how/when the victim was later identified.

    INSTITUTIONAL CONTACT
    Missing-person report, police contact, court/probation intersection, or
    other relevant institutional record.

    SIGNIFICANCE
    One short paragraph identifying what the case contributes to understanding
    the larger pattern.

    SOURCES [1] [2]

This keeps the page detailed while avoiding repetitive free-form narratives.

EXPERIMENTAL "ZOMBIE" / DRILLING EPISODES
------------------------------------------
This material should be retained, but treated very carefully.

Documented finding:
During the 1992 sanity trial, prosecution psychiatrist Frederick Fosdal
testified that Dahmer had drilled holes into the heads of four victims and
introduced acid or boiling water in attempts to render them permanently
submissive. The testimony framed these as attempts at control rather than
simply killing.

Use this as an analytical subsection:

    EXPERIMENTATION AND CONTROL

Do not write:
    "Dahmer was trying to keep them alive rather than kill them."

More accurate:
    "Dahmer told investigators that he was experimenting with methods intended
    to produce a permanently submissive, controllable state. Some of these
    attempts proved fatal."

Known documented cases include:
- Errol Lindsey
- Tony Hughes
- Konerak Sinthasomphone
- Jeremiah Weinberger

The exact medical description should remain brief and clinical.

Source:
Washington Post/AP coverage of the 1992 sanity trial; forensic review in
Academic Forensic Pathology.

NECROPHILIA
-----------
Retain as a documented recurring behavior, but do not make it the organizing
theme of every offense.

Suggested formulation:
    "Dahmer acknowledged engaging in sexual acts with the bodies of some
    victims after death. The behavior formed part of the broader pattern
    examined by psychiatric experts during the 1992 trial."

This is better than implying it occurred identically in every case.

CANNIBALISM
-----------
Retain as a documented component of the later offense history, but keep it
brief.

Suggested formulation:
    "Dahmer also told investigators that he had consumed parts of some
    victims. The prosecution and defense both discussed this behavior during
    the 1992 sanity trial."

If individual cases identify specific retained/consumed tissues, that can be
mentioned only when the source is explicit.

Do not confidently state a fixed list such as "at least two" when the broader
record contains differing accounts about which victims or parts were
consumed.

PHOTOGRAPHY
-----------
Photography became a recurring component of the later pattern.

Keep:
- Polaroid photographs documented the victims and aspects of the disposal
  process.
- The photographs became important evidence after the arrest.

Avoid:
- describing individual graphic photographs
- reproducing them
- using graphic photographic imagery on the website

TROPHY / REMAINS RETENTION
--------------------------
This developed over time.

Early:
- Hicks: remains disposed of rather than retained as a long-term collection.
- Tuomi: Dahmer later described a temporary attempt to preserve his head/skull.
- Sears: later accounts document permanent retention of preserved remains.
- 1990–1991: retention and preservation became a major part of the Apartment
  213 evidence.

This progression can be visualized as:
    disposal -> temporary retention -> preserved remains -> extensive collection

Use this as an analytical pattern, not as a claim that every stage was neat
or inevitable.

SENSITIVITY / PRESENTATION RULES
--------------------------------
- No graphic images.
- No crime-scene photographs used as decorative material.
- No sensational section titles.
- No theatrical descriptions such as "house of horrors," "hell," "monster,"
  "grotesque museum," etc.
- No unnecessary anatomical detail.
- Keep quotations from victims/families only where they add historical value.
- Attribute statements from Dahmer himself as his account, not automatically
  as objective fact.
- Use forensic findings when they materially clarify the historical record.

SOURCE PRIORITY
---------------
Preferred sources:
1. FBI Vault / Department of Justice records
2. Court records and opinions
3. Contemporary newspaper/archive reporting
4. Forensic/academic publications
5. Strong secondary historical biographies

Later summaries can help locate material but should not override primary or
contemporaneous records.
