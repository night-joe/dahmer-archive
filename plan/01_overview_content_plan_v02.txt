SECTION 1 — INTRODUCTION
-----------------------
Purpose:
Introduce the case, the 1978–1991 span, Milwaukee context, victims, and the
broader research scope.

Key editorial points:
- Dahmer confessed to 17 killings between 1978 and 1991.
- He was convicted of 16 murders: 15 in Wisconsin and 1 in Ohio.
- Eleven victims' remains were recovered from Apartment 213.
- The case should be introduced as a historical/institutional subject, not
  only as a crime story.
- Avoid unsupported claims about an "unusually high number" of disappearances
  or that "no one had connected" all cases before 1991.
- Avoid loaded terms such as "monster", "depraved predator", "domestic hell",
  "house of horrors", and similar language in the site's own voice.

Suggested editorial opening:
    "Between 1978 and 1991, Jeffrey Dahmer murdered 17 men and boys, most of
    them in Milwaukee. The crimes unfolded over more than a decade, with a
    long gap between his first known killing in Ohio in 1978 and the series of
    murders that resumed in Milwaukee in 1987. By the time Dahmer was arrested
    in July 1991, investigators were confronting a case that spanned multiple
    victims, locations, years and jurisdictions."

The exact final prose will be source-cited when the Overview page is locked.

SECTION 2 — THE NIGHT TRACY EDWARDS ESCAPED
-------------------------------------------
Purpose:
Use Tracy Edwards's escape as the narrative entry into the discovery of the
case.

Core facts:
- July 22, 1991
- Edwards was 32
- He escaped with a handcuff attached to one wrist.
- He encountered Milwaukee police at approximately 11:30 p.m.
- Officers Rolf Mueller and Robert Rauth accompanied him back to Apartment 213.
- Edwards said Dahmer had threatened him with a knife.
- Police then discovered evidence inside the apartment.

Editorial rule:
- Present Edwards's account as testimony/statement when describing subjective
  experiences or Dahmer's statements.
- Do not dramatize the episode beyond the documented sequence.

SECTION 4 — CONFESSION & RECONSTRUCTION OF THE CASE
----------------------------------------------------
Dahmer gave extensive statements to Milwaukee homicide detectives after his
arrest.

Key points:
- Detectives Patrick Kennedy and Dennis Murphy conducted extended interviews.
- Contemporary accounts describe roughly 60 hours of interviews.
- Dahmer confessed to 17 killings.
- His statements were combined with physical, forensic and documentary
  evidence to reconstruct the victim series.

Important:
- Do not say the confession alone "proved everything."
- Attribute Dahmer's own descriptions as his statements rather than objective
  fact.
- Link to:
    /timeline
    /case/offense-series
    /victims

SECTION 7 — TRIAL & LEGAL PROCEEDINGS
-------------------------------------
Key points:
- Dahmer pleaded guilty but insane to the 15 Wisconsin murder charges.
- The legal issue was mental responsibility, not factual guilt.
- Competing psychiatric experts testified.
- The jury found Dahmer legally sane on all 15 counts.
- Judge Laurence Gram Jr. imposed 15 consecutive life terms.
- In Ohio, Dahmer pleaded guilty to the 1978 murder of Steven Hicks and received
  an additional life sentence.
- Total murder convictions: 16.

Important legal distinction:
Do NOT write:
    "The jury convicted him on all 15 counts."

Correct framework:
    "Dahmer had already pleaded guilty. The jury was asked to determine
    whether he was legally responsible under Wisconsin's mental-responsibility
    standard."

Link:
    Trial & Legal Proceedings -> /case/trial
    Psychological Profile -> /topics/psychological-profile

APPROVED HOMEPAGE ARTICLE — WORKING FINAL VERSION
-------------------------------------------------
Title:
    THE JEFFREY DAHMER CASE

Text:

"Between 1978 and 1991, Jeffrey Dahmer murdered 17 men and boys, most of them
in Milwaukee. The crimes unfolded over more than a decade, with a long gap
between his first known killing in Ohio in 1978 and the series of murders that
resumed in Milwaukee in 1987. By the time Dahmer was arrested in July 1991,
the case had become one of the most extensive and consequential serial-murder
investigations in the United States. The FBI records that 11 victims' remains
were recovered from his Milwaukee apartment, while Dahmer ultimately confessed
to 17 killings."

"The case cannot be understood only through the crimes themselves. Many of
Dahmer's victims were young men from marginalized communities, including a
large number of Black victims, while others were Asian, white, Latino and
Native American. Many encounters occurred around Milwaukee's LGBTQ nightlife
and other public places where Dahmer met potential victims. The historical
record also places the case within a Milwaukee experiencing significant racial
and economic divisions, making questions of visibility, vulnerability and
institutional response an important part of its later examination."

"The case finally came to light on the night of July 22, 1991, when Tracy
Edwards escaped from Dahmer's apartment with a handcuff attached to one wrist
and flagged down police at about 11:30 p.m. Officers followed Edwards back to
924 North 25th Street, where they arrested Dahmer and discovered human remains
and other evidence inside Apartment 213. Investigators soon learned that the
material in the apartment was connected to a much larger series of killings."

"What followed was more than a criminal investigation. It became an
examination of previous police contacts, probation supervision, neighborhood
complaints, the treatment of Konerak Sinthasomphone, and the ways in which
race, poverty and sexuality shaped the circumstances surrounding the victims.
This project examines the case beyond the headline: the victims' lives, the
chronology of the killings, Milwaukee's historical context, the investigation
and trial, documented psychological evaluations, institutional failures,
social consequences and the ways the case has been represented and
re-examined over the decades."
