   The narrative strictly adheres to objective medical, psychiatric, and legal terminology.

2. The Foundational Conceptual Divide:
   The website enforces the foundational legal-psychiatric distinction:
     CLINICAL MENTAL DISORDER  ≠  LEGAL INSANITY / CRIMINAL RESPONSIBILITY
   A psychiatric diagnosis is a medical classification of distress or behavioral deviation.
   Legal insanity under Wis. Stat. § 971.15 is a formal legal standard requiring proof that
   a mental disease or defect deprived the defendant of substantial capacity either to
   appreciate the wrongfulness of his conduct or to conform his conduct to the law.
3. Strict Attribution of Expert Opinions:
   The website does not adopt any psychiatric theory or diagnostic label as settled objective
   fact. Every diagnosis (necrophilia, borderline personality disorder, sadism, schizophrenia)
   and motivational claim is strictly attributed to the evaluating clinician who testified to it.

   Evaluations in 1991–1992 were conducted under the standards of DSM-III-R (1987). The website
   reports historical clinical findings within their contemporary framework rather than
   retroactively converting them into modern DSM-5-TR classifications.

   The website explicitly emphasizes that homosexuality is not a psychiatric disorder (removed
   from the DSM in 1973) and played no causal role in the crimes. Homosexuality must be strictly
   distinguished from paraphilic disorders such as necrophilia.

SECTION 1: THE FOUNDATIONAL DIVIDE: CLINICAL DISORDER VS. LEGAL INSANITY
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 The Legal Sanity Standard under Wisconsin Law
Anchor: #sanity-trial-standard (Aliases: #wisconsin-sanity-standard, #legal-insanity)
Cross-Links: -> /timeline#event-1992-sanity-trial | -> /case#sanity-trial | -> /case#statutory-split
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 1992 Milwaukee trial (*State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer*, Case No. F-912542) was
frequently misunderstood in popular coverage as a debate over whether Jeffrey Dahmer was
"mentally ill" or "crazy." In fact, mental health experts across all parties agreed that Dahmer
suffered from profound psychological, paraphilic, and addictive disorders [1: WaPo Feb 7, 1992;
2: Court TV 1992].

The trial was not a determination of factual guilt. On January 13, 1992, Dahmer entered formal
guilty pleas to all 15 Milwaukee County homicide charges, conceding the physical commission
(*actus reus*) of the crimes [1, 3: WaPo Jan 14, 1992].

The trial before Judge Laurence C. Gram Jr. (January 27 – February 15, 1992) was exclusively a
bifurcated Phase 2 sanity trial under Wis. Stat. § 971.165 to determine criminal responsibility [2].

A. Wisconsin Statutory Test (Wis. Stat. § 971.15):
   Wisconsin adheres to a modified version of the American Law Institute (ALI) Model Penal Code
   mental responsibility standard:
   - Subsection (1): "A person is not responsible for criminal conduct if at the time of such
     conduct as a result of mental disease or defect the person lacked substantial capacity
     either to appreciate the wrongfulness of his or her conduct or to conform his or her
     conduct to the requirements of law."
   - The Cognitive Prong: Lacking substantial capacity to *appreciate wrongfulness*.
   - The Volitional Prong: Lacking substantial capacity to *conform conduct* to the law.

B. Statutory Exclusions:
   - Subsection (2): "As used in this section, the terms 'mental disease or defect' do not
     include an abnormality manifested only by repeated criminal or otherwise antisocial
     conduct." This statutory clause deliberately excludes standard Antisocial Personality
     Disorder (psychopathy/sociopathy) from serving as a qualifying mental disease.
   - Voluntary Intoxication Exclusion: Under Wisconsin jurisprudence, temporary impairment
     resulting from voluntary alcohol or substance intoxication does not constitute a qualifying
     mental disease or defect.

C. Burden of Proof & Jury Mechanics:
   - Under Wis. Stat. § 971.15(3), the state is not required to prove sanity beyond a reasonable
     doubt. The defendant bears the affirmative burden of proving that he suffered from a mental
     disease or defect and lacked substantial capacity by the greater weight of the credible
     evidence (a civil preponderance standard).
   - Under Wis. Stat. § 805.09(2), Wisconsin permits a five-sixths jury verdict (10 out of 12
     jurors) in sanity phase special verdicts [2].

2.1 How Jeffrey Dahmer Was Evaluated
Anchor: #evaluation-methodology (Aliases: #clinical-testing, #psychiatric-evaluation)
Cross-Links: -> /case#investigation | -> /timeline#event-1992-sanity-trial
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Following his arrest on July 22, 1991, Dahmer underwent one of the most comprehensive forensic
psychiatric assessments in American legal history, spanning over six months [2, 10: O'Meara 2009]:

1. Clinical Interview Hours:
   Mental health professionals conducted more than 200 hours of direct, in-person clinical
   interviews with Dahmer at the Milwaukee County Jail [2, 4: WaPo Feb 13, 1992]. Dr. Park Dietz
   alone conducted 18 hours of direct interviews across multiple sessions [4].
2. Psychometric & Diagnostic Testing:
   Evaluators administered comprehensive psychological testing batteries:
   - Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale-Revised (WAIS-R): Dahmer scored in the bright-normal to
     superior range (Full-Scale IQ between 115 and 120), demonstrating preserved logical reasoning,
     abstract comprehension, and intact verbal skills [2, 8: UPI Feb 6, 1992].
   - Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI): Revealed severe elevations on clinical
     scales associated with depression, social alienation, and schizoid detachment, alongside
     high scores on psychopathic deviance, but did not display an active schizophrenic profile [2, 8].
   - Projective Testing: Rorschach Inkblot and Thematic Apperception Tests (TAT) indicated
     profound morbid preoccupations with inanimate anatomy, dissection, and bodily boundaries [2].
3. Neurological & Biological Examinations:
   - Comprehensive neurological workups, including electroencephalograms (EEG) and brain imaging
     (CT/MRI), were conducted to evaluate possible organic brain damage or temporal lobe epilepsy
     stemming from his childhood hernia surgery or head trauma.
   - Findings: All neurological tests were normal, ruling out gross organic brain lesions,
     epilepsy, or structural cerebral defects [2, 11: Jentzen 2017].
4. Collateral & Archival Investigation:
   Experts examined 60 hours of MPD detective interrogation tapes, military medical records
   (documenting alcohol abuse counseling at Fort Stewart, GA; FBI Part 01 p. 29), Revere High School
   dockets, probation records, crime scene photographs, and extensive family interviews [2, 15].

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Defense Expert Witnesses
Anchor: #defense-experts (Aliases: #berlin-testimony, #becker-testimony, #wahlstrom-testimony)
Cross-Links: -> /case#sanity-trial | -> /timeline#event-1992-sanity-trial
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The defense team (Gerald Boyle and Wendy Patrickus) called three primary expert witnesses to
establish that Dahmer suffered from qualifying mental diseases that destroyed his volitional
capacity to control his actions [2]:

A. Dr. Frederick S. Berlin (Testimony: February 3–4, 1992):
   - Professional Qualifications: Associate Professor of Psychiatry at Johns Hopkins University
     School of Medicine; Founder and Director of the Johns Hopkins Sexual Disorders Clinic;
     internationally recognized authority on sexual paraphilias [2, 5: WaPo Feb 4, 1992].
   - Primary Diagnoses: Necrophilia as a primary paraphilic mental disease; severe paraphilia;
     obsessive-compulsive disorder.
   - Core Testimony:
     1. Dr. Berlin testified that Dahmer suffered from an extreme, consuming form of necrophilia
        that functioned as a profound mental disease within the meaning of Wis. Stat. § 971.15 [5].
     2. He described Dahmer as being in the grip of progressive, intrusive erotic urges that
        gradually displaced his conscious resistance: "He was sick, not evil... He was driven by
        sexual obsessions over which he had no control" [5: WaPo Feb 4, 1992].
     3. Dr. Berlin concluded that Dahmer completely lacked substantial capacity to conform his
        conduct to the requirements of the law (volitional failure) [2, 5].
   - Cross-Examination Concessions:
     Prosecutor E. Michael McCann established that Dahmer never attempted to kill during daylight,
     never attacked individuals in front of witnesses, and refrained when family members or
     roommates were nearby, indicating situational awareness and selective behavioral inhibition [2, 5].

B. Dr. Judith V. Becker (Testimony: February 4–5, 1992):
   - Professional Qualifications: Professor of Psychiatry and Psychology at the University of
     Arizona; clinical psychologist specializing in the assessment and treatment of sexual
     offenders and paraphilias [2, 6: UPI Feb 5, 1992].
   - Primary Diagnoses: Necrophilia; Paraphilia Not Otherwise Specified; Mixed Personality Disorder.
   - Core Testimony:
     1. Dr. Becker testified that Dahmer's necrophilic fantasies began in early adolescence and
        progressively intensified into an overwhelming behavioral imperative [6].
     2. She described how Dahmer engaged in compulsive rituals to satisfy erotic desires that
        could only be achieved through inanimate, immobile human bodies [6].
     3. She concluded that Dahmer was suffering from a qualifying mental disease and lacked the
        capacity to conform his behavior to the law [2, 6].
   - Crucial Cross-Examination Breakthrough:
     On cross-examination by McCann, Dr. Becker acknowledged that Dahmer had released multiple
     prospective victims (including Ronald Flowers in 1988 and Luis Sandoval) when situations
     diverged from his expectations or when external risks arose [2, 6]. McCann utilized this
     testimony to argue that Dahmer retained volitional control and made deliberate choices.

C. Dr. Carl Wahlstrom (Testimony: February 5, 1992):
   - Professional Qualifications: Forensic Psychiatrist; Assistant Professor of Psychiatry at
     Rush-Presbyterian-St. Luke's Medical Center in Chicago [2, 7: UPI Feb 5, 1992].
   - Primary Diagnoses (Multiaxial Formulation):
     - Axis I: Necrophilia (primary paraphilia); Alcohol Dependence; Psychotic Disorder Not
       Otherwise Specified (NOS).
     - Axis II: Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD); Schizotypal Personality Disorder [7].
   - Core Testimony:
     1. Dr. Wahlstrom presented the most complex psychiatric formulation for the defense,
        arguing that Dahmer suffered from multiple co-occurring psychiatric illnesses [7].
     2. He highlighted severe borderline pathology, characterized by chronic emptiness, profound
        terror of abandonment, and identity fragmentation [7].
     3. He testified that Dahmer's crude surgical experiments (trepanning) were psychotic,
        bizarre attempts to create living, compliant companions who could never leave him [2, 7].
     4. He concluded that this constellation of severe disorders deprived Dahmer of substantial
        capacity to conform his conduct to the law [7].

3.2 Court-Appointed & Independent Experts
Anchor: #court-appointed-experts (Aliases: #palermo-testimony, #fosdal-testimony, #friedman-testimony)
Cross-Links: -> /case#sanity-trial | -> /timeline#event-1992-sanity-trial
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Judge Laurence Gram appointed independent mental health experts to conduct impartial evaluations
for the court [2]:

A. Dr. George F. Palermo (Testimony: February 6, 1992):
   - Professional Qualifications: Forensic Psychiatrist; Clinical Professor of Psychiatry at
     the Medical College of Wisconsin; Director of the Milwaukee County Court Clinic [2, 1: WaPo
     Feb 7, 1992].
   - Primary Diagnoses: Severe mixed personality disorder with antisocial, sadomasochistic, and
     obsessive features; alcohol dependence; paraphilia [1]. Explicitly rejected psychosis and
     rejected necrophilia as an independent mental disease [1].
   - Core Testimony:
     1. Dr. Palermo testified that Dahmer was not psychotic, not insane, and fully aware of his
        actions [1: WaPo Feb 7, 1992].
     2. He characterized Dahmer's primary motivation as a desire for total domination, control,
        and possession, driven by underlying hostility, lust, power, and severe terror of rejection [1].
     3. He characterized the murders in terms of sexual sadism and absolute control, testifying:
        "He killed them because he wanted to possess them... He was a sane man who chose to do
        evil things" [1: WaPo Feb 7, 1992].
     4. Dr. Palermo concluded unequivocally that Dahmer was legally sane under Wisconsin law,
        retaining substantial capacity both to appreciate the wrongfulness of his acts and to
        conform his conduct to the law [1, 2].

B. Dr. Samuel H. Friedman (Testimony: February 6–7, 1992):
   - Professional Qualifications: Clinical Psychologist, Milwaukee County Court Clinic [2, 8:
     UPI Feb 6, 1992].
   - Evaluative Role: Administered psychological test batteries (WAIS-R, MMPI, Rorschach) at
     the direction of Dr. Palermo [8].
   - Core Testimony:
     1. Dr. Friedman testified that Dahmer operated with intact cognitive faculties, above-average
        intellect, and no thought disorder or hallucinations [8].
     2. He described Dahmer as a solitary, detached predator who engaged in a pattern of hunting
        and trophy collection, using remains to satisfy erotic needs for companionship [8].
     3. He testified that Dahmer's psychological profile reflected severe character pathology,
        but did not impair his capacity to choose his actions or understand their wrongfulness [2, 8].

C. Dr. Frederick A. Fosdal (Testimony: February 8–10, 1992):
   - Professional Qualifications: Forensic Psychiatrist, Madison, Wisconsin; past president of
     the Wisconsin Psychiatric Association [2, 9: WaPo Feb 9, 1992].
   - Primary Diagnoses: Necrophilia; Alcohol Dependence; Personality Disorder NOS [9].
   - Core Testimony:
     1. Dr. Fosdal agreed with defense experts that Dahmer met the clinical criteria for necrophilia,
        confirming that Dahmer experienced genuine, compulsive erotic attraction to corpses [9].
     2. However, Dr. Fosdal drew a crucial legal distinction: he testified that necrophilia was
        a paraphilic sexual preference, NOT a "mental disease or defect" within the meaning of
        Wis. Stat. § 971.15 [9: WaPo Feb 9, 1992].
     3. He emphasized that paraphilic urges do not destroy volitional control or create an
        irresistible impulse: "An urge is not an order... A paraphilia does not compel murder" [9].
     4. He pointed to Dahmer's extensive concealment efforts, purchase of chemicals, and selective
        timing as conclusive evidence of behavioral control.
     5. Dr. Fosdal concluded Dahmer was legally sane on all counts [2, 9].

3.3 Prosecution Expert Witness: Dr. Park Elliott Dietz
Anchor: #dietz-testimony (Aliases: #park-dietz, #prosecution-psychiatrist)
Cross-Links: -> /case#sanity-trial | -> /timeline#event-1992-sanity-trial
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Dr. Park Elliott Dietz testified for the prosecution across three days (February 12–14, 1992),
providing the central forensic architecture that secured the jury's sanity verdict [2, 4: WaPo
Feb 13, 1992]:

A. Professional Qualifications:
   Forensic Psychiatrist; Clinical Professor of Psychiatry and Biobehavioral Sciences at UCLA
   School of Medicine; founder of Threat Assessment Group; prior testifying expert in high-profile
   forensic cases (e.g., John Hinckley Jr.) [2, 4].

B. Evaluative Scope:
   Dr. Dietz conducted 18 hours of direct clinical interviews with Dahmer in January 1992,
   reviewed all police recordings, analyzed autopsy reports, and performed detailed timeline
   reconstructions [4].

C. Primary Diagnoses:
   - Paraphilia Not Otherwise Specified (Necrophilia).
   - Alcohol Abuse and Dependence.
   - Explicitly Rejected: Borderline Personality Disorder, Schizophrenia, Psychosis, and any
     statutory "Mental Disease or Defect" [2, 4].

D. The Core Conceptual Breakthrough: Desire vs. Volition:
   Dr. Dietz established the theoretical distinction that dominated jury deliberations:
   1. The Function of Paraphilia: Paraphilia accounts for what an individual finds sexually
      arousing. Dahmer's paraphilia explained why he desired unconscious or inanimate male
      bodies.
   2. The Independence of Volition: Paraphilic arousal does not dictate or compel behavior.
      Having an atypical sexual desire does not deprive an individual of the capacity to choose
      how, when, or whether to act upon that desire [2, 4].

E. Five Pillars of Legal Sanity (Dr. Dietz's Evidentiary Demonstration):
   1. Deliberate Premeditation & Logistics: Dahmer engaged in complex, forward-looking planning—
      sourcing specialized chemicals (muriatic acid, lye), procuring sedative pharmaceuticals
      (triazolam / Halcion, chloroform), and preparing his apartment for homicidal acts [4].
   2. Instrumental Use of Alcohol: Dahmer did not kill because alcohol induced an uncontrolled
      rage. Rather, Dahmer deliberately used alcohol as a pharmacological tool to dull his own
      squeamishness, suppress moral revulsion, and overcome internal hesitation prior to killing [4:
      WaPo Feb 13, 1992].
   3. Selective Impulse Control & Restraint: Dahmer demonstrated consistent self-control whenever
      external risks were present:
      - He never attacked anyone in public or in front of witnesses.
      - He refrained from killing during periods when his grandmother or father was nearby.
      - He released individuals (e.g., Ronald Flowers) when circumstances became complicated [2, 4].
   4. Active Concealment & Flight Avoidance: Dahmer's actions following homicides—dismembering
      remains, bleaching bones, dissolving tissue in acid, fabricating deceptive cover stories
      to police and neighbors—demonstrated acute awareness of legal and moral wrongfulness [4].
   5. The Preferred Living Partner Finding: Dahmer admitted to Dr. Dietz that his primary preference
      was a living, permanently consenting partner. It was only when partners insisted on leaving
      that Dahmer resorted to drugging and murder to prevent abandonment [2, 4].

SECTION 5: THE CONTROL EXPERIMENTS: TREPANNING & INSTRUMENTALITY

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Crude Neurosurgical Interventions & The Pursuit of Submission
Anchor: #control-experiments (Aliases: #zombie-experiments, #trepanning)
Cross-Links: -> /case#experimentation | -> /topics/theories-motivations#possession-control
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

During the spring and summer of 1991, Dahmer's offending evolved to include crude, amateur
trepanning experiments performed on four victims: Errol Lindsey, Anthony Sears, Konerak
Sinthasomphone, and Jeremiah Weinberger [2, 11: Jentzen 2017; 15: FBI Part 01 p. 24].

A. Documented Procedure:
   - While victims were deeply sedated with triazolam (Halcion) mixed into drinks, Dahmer used
     a hand-held electric drill to create a burr hole through their frontal skulls [11, 15].
   - Dahmer then used a plastic syringe to inject solutions of hydrochloric (muriatic) acid
     or boiling water directly into the frontal lobes of their cerebral cortex [11, 15].
   - In all four instances, the intervention caused cerebral edema, coma, and death, either
     rapidly or within hours [11].

B. Competing Psychiatric Interpretations at Trial:
   - Defense Interpretation (Dr. Wahlstrom):
     The defense presented these grotesque procedures as evidence of severe psychotic
     disorganization and psychotic delusion. Dr. Wahlstrom argued that attempting to create a
     docile, lobotomized "living zombie" through crude drilling was inherently irrational and
     demonstrated that Dahmer had lost contact with reality [2, 7].
   - Prosecution & Forensic Interpretation (Drs. Dietz & Fosdal):
     Drs. Dietz and Fosdal testified that the experiments, while biologically ignorant, were
     logically consistent with Dahmer's stated objective: creating an uncomplaining, permanently
     compliant companion who would not abandon him [2, 4, 9].
     1. Instrumental Goal: Dahmer explained to evaluators that he did not derive pleasure from
        the act of drilling or inflicting pain; rather, he sought to eliminate the victim's
        will and consciousness while keeping the body warm and living [4].
     2. Avoiding Disposal Burdens: Dahmer stated that killing required extensive, exhausting
        dismemberment and disposal procedures; if a victim could be kept in a docile, living state,
        he would not need to continually seek new victims [4].
     3. Preserved Reality Testing: When the experiments repeatedly failed and resulted in death,
        Dahmer recognized that his technique did not work and reverted to dismemberment [2, 4].

6.1 Jury Deliberations & The 10–2 Sanity Finding
Anchor: #sanity-verdict (Aliases: #jury-verdict, #trial-verdict)
Cross-Links: -> /timeline#event-1992-sanity-trial | -> /case#sanity-trial | -> /case#sentencing-1992
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Following two weeks of testimony and closing arguments, the 12-member jury deliberated for
approximately 20 hours over two days [2, 12: WaPo Feb 16, 1992].

A. The Verdict Mechanics (February 15, 1992):
   - Under Wis. Stat. § 805.09(2), at least 10 of 12 jurors had to agree on whether Dahmer
     proved that he suffered from a mental disease or defect and lacked substantial capacity
     on each count [2].
   - Counts 1 and 2 (Homicides of Anthony Sears and Raymond Smith): The jury voted **10 to 2**
     that Dahmer was legally sane [12: WaPo Feb 16, 1992].
   - Counts 3 through 15 (Homicides of Ernest Miller through Joseph Bradehoft): The jury voted
     **11 to 1** or **12 to 0 (unanimous)** that Dahmer was legally sane [12].
   - Conclusion: The jury formally rejected the defense of mental disease or defect on all 15
     Wisconsin counts, establishing Dahmer's full criminal responsibility under state law [2, 12].

B. Legal Significance:
   The verdict meant that while Dahmer was recognized by psychiatric experts as suffering from
   severe sexual pathology, the jury determined he did not meet the rigorous statutory threshold
   for legal insanity. He possessed substantial capacity to appreciate the wrongfulness of his
   actions and retained the volitional capacity to refrain from killing [2, 12].

SECTION 7: EPISTEMOLOGICAL BOUNDARIES: WHAT EVALUATIONS ESTABLISH
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 What the Psychological Record Can and Cannot Establish
Anchor: #evaluations-boundaries (Aliases: #clinical-conclusions, #epistemology)
Cross-Links: -> /topics/theories-motivations#theories-boundaries | -> /overview#overview-conclusion
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To maintain scholarly rigor, the research webapp delineates the exact evidentiary boundaries
of the 1991–1992 psychiatric evaluations:

A. WHAT THE PSYCHOLOGICAL EVALUATIONS ESTABLISH (Documented Findings):
   1. Dahmer suffered from severe, clinically recognized paraphilic pathology (specifically
      necrophilia) and severe alcohol dependence [2, 4, 9].
   2. Dahmer operated with intact cognitive faculties, normal-to-bright intellect (IQ 115–120),
      and no gross organic brain lesions, intellectual disability, or active schizophrenic thought disorder [2, 8].
   3. Dahmer demonstrated extensive, forward-looking premeditation, procurement of materials,
      and active post-offense concealment, reflecting acute awareness of moral and legal wrongfulness [4].
   4. Dahmer exhibited selective behavioral control, demonstrating the capacity to delay or
      refrain from violence when external detection risks arose [4, 6].
   5. The 1992 Milwaukee County jury rejected the legal insanity defense on all counts under
      Wis. Stat. § 971.15 [12].

B. WHAT THE PSYCHOLOGICAL EVALUATIONS CANNOT ESTABLISH (Unwarranted Speculation):
   1. They do NOT establish that childhood experiences (e.g., 1964 hernia surgery or parental
      divorce) mechanically caused or predetermined serial homicide.
   2. They do NOT establish that alcohol alone caused the killings; alcohol functioned as an
      instrumental disinhibitor rather than an involuntary trigger [4].
   3. They do NOT establish that all statements made by Dahmer during psychiatric interviews
      were unvarnished truth; evaluators recognized retrospective minimization and image management.
   4. They do NOT establish a unified consensus diagnosis; evaluating clinicians fundamentally
      disagreed over whether Dahmer had Borderline Personality Disorder, Schizotypal Personality
      Disorder, or Sexual Sadism.
   5. They do NOT provide a single, universal "root cause" of the killings.

A. Diagnostic Manual Evolution (DSM-III-R vs. Modern Nosology):
   The psychiatric terms utilized throughout the 1992 trial reflect the diagnostic standards of
   the American Psychiatric Association's DSM-III-R (1987). Modern diagnostic criteria (DSM-5-TR,
   2022) have revised paraphilic disorder definitions, personality disorder models, and substance
   use classifications. The webapp reports historical diagnoses within their 1992 trial context
   rather than retroactively constructing speculative modern diagnoses [2, 10].

B. Complete Decoupling from Homosexuality:
   1. Medical Fact: Homosexuality is not a mental disorder, illness, or criminal predisposition.
      The American Psychiatric Association removed homosexuality from the DSM in 1973.
   2. Trial Evidence: Mental health experts on both sides of the 1992 trial explicitly affirmed
      that Dahmer's sexual orientation (homosexuality) had no causal relationship to his homicidal
      conduct [2, 4, 5].
   3. Paraphilic Distinction: Dahmer's homicides were driven by necrophilic fantasies of absolute
      possession, somnophilia, and total submission, which exist independently of sexual orientation.
      Equating homosexuality with violence or sexual deviance is medically and historically false.
