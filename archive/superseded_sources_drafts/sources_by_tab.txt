1. OVERVIEW TAB (/overview)
================================================================================

PRIMARY JUDICIAL & LAW ENFORCEMENT RECORDS
- State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer, Case No. F-912552 (Milwaukee County Circuit Court, 1992). Official complaint, charging schedule (15 homicide counts in Milwaukee), and preliminary hearing records.
- Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) Vault — Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer FOIA Archive (Parts 01, 02, 03, 06). Initial discovery of human remains, Tracy Edwards escape summary, and preliminary inventory of Apartment 213.
- Milwaukee Police Department Incident Report No. 91-057819 (July 22–23, 1991). Arrest of Jeffrey Dahmer, officer notes of Tracy Edwards's statements, and emergency scene preservation.

CONTEMPORARY JOURNALISTIC ARCHIVES (1991)
- The Milwaukee Sentinel & The Milwaukee Journal (July 23–26, 1991). Special editions covering the escape of Tracy Edwards, emergency police response to Oxford Apartments, and the initial execution of the search warrant.
- The New York Times (July 24–28, 1991). Contemporary reporting by Dirk Johnson and Isabel Wilkerson ("Horror in Milwaukee: Discovery of 17 Victims").
- Associated Press (AP) & United Press International (UPI) Wire Reports (July 23–30, 1991). Archival news wires detailing the arrest chronology, chemical drum discovery, HAZMAT mobilization, and interstate forensic tracking.

HISTORICAL OVERVIEW & FORENSIC SUMMARIES
- Schwartz, Anne E. (1992). "The Man Who Could Not Kill Enough: The Secret Murders of Milwaukee's Jeffrey Dahmer." Birch Lane Press / (2021) "Monster: The True Story of the Jeffrey Dahmer Murders." Union Square & Co. (Firsthand investigative account by the first reporter on the crime scene).
- Milwaukee County Medical Examiner's Office (1991–1992). Public findings summary on victim recovery, osteological examinations, and official death certifications.

2. CONTEXT TAB (/context)
================================================================================

MILWAUKEE HISTORICAL, ECONOMIC & DEMOGRAPHIC RECORDS (1987–1991)
- U.S. Census Bureau (1980 & 1990 Decennial Censuses). Demographic profiles, racial distribution, income levels, and housing occupancy rates for Milwaukee's Near West Side, Avenues West, and North Side neighborhoods.
- Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago. Historical economic bulletins on Midwest manufacturing decline, industrial automation, and plant closures (including Allis-Chalmers and A.O. Smith).
- Levine, Marc V. (Center for Economic Development, University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee). Research publications on the historical economic polarization and hyper-segregation of post-industrial Milwaukee.

POLICING CULTURE, COMMUNITY RELATIONS & CIVIC FRICTION
- City of Milwaukee Citizen Police Support Committee (1992). "Report on MPD Operations, Community Race Relations, and Structural Oversight."
- ACLU of Wisconsin Historical Archives (1970–1992). Documentation of police surveillance, discriminatory stop practices, and department policies under Police Chief Harold Breier (1964–1984), Robert Ziarnik (1984–1989), and Philip Arreola (1989–1996).
- Inquest and Litigation Records of the Ernest Lacy Incident (Milwaukee County, 1981). Public records and community mobilization documents regarding the death of a young Black man in MPD custody and subsequent department distrust.
- Egger, Steven A. (2002). "The Killers Among Us: An Examination of Serial Murder and Its Victims." Prentice Hall. (Analytical and sociological framework defining the concept of the "less-dead").

MILWAUKEE LGBTQ+ HISTORY & THE HIV/AIDS EPIDEMIC
- Takach, Michail / Wisconsin LGBTQ History Project. Archival research and oral histories on 1980s Milwaukee LGBTQ+ nightlife, bathhouse raids, bar communities (including the 219 Club, Phoenix, and C'est La Vie), and police harassment.
- Milwaukee City Health Department & Wisconsin AIDS/HIV Program Reports (1985–1991). Epidemiological and public health records detailing the impact of the HIV/AIDS epidemic on Milwaukee's gay community and the resulting social underground culture.

EARLY LIFE, FAMILY BACKGROUND & MILITARY RECORDS
- FBI Vault — Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer FOIA Archive (Parts 01 & 07). Certified U.S. Army personnel records (2nd Armored Division in Baumholder, West Germany; 1979–1981), medical evaluations, and alcohol abuse discharge documentation under Army Chapter 9.
- Dahmer, Lionel (1994). "A Father's Story." William Morrow & Co. (Attributed memoir covering childhood upbringing in Bath, Ohio, parental divorce of Lionel and Joyce Dahmer, adolescent isolation, and animal bone collecting).
- Bath Township Police Department & Summit County Sheriff's Office Records (Ohio, 1978). Preliminary missing person reports for Steven Mark Hicks and local sheriff activity.
- West Allis Police Department (Wisconsin, 1981–1988). Incident reports, disorderly conduct citation at Wisconsin State Fair (1982), and records concerning residence with grandmother Catherine Dahmer.

3. CASE TAB (/case)
================================================================================

SUBSECTION 3A: THE OFFENSE SERIES (17 HOMICIDES, 1978–1991)
- Milwaukee Police Department Homicide Division Interrogation Files (July 23 – August 1991). Formal confession statements given by Jeffrey Dahmer to Detectives Dennis Murphy and Patrick Kennedy, detailing encounter locations, sedative methods, homicides, and dismemberment across all 17 cases.
- FBI Vault — Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer FOIA Archive (Parts 01–19). Detailed victim chronological summaries, interstate investigative cooperation files, and laboratory evidence verification logs.
- Summit County Court of Common Pleas (Akron, Ohio). Indictment, investigation files, and guilty plea record for the aggravated murder of Steven Mark Hicks (Case No. CR-92-01-0130, May 1992).
- Kennedy, Patrick, & Robyn, Dirk (ed. 2006). "Dahmer's Confession: The Milwaukee Cannibal's Arrest and Interrogation." John Blake Publishing. (Primary interview documentation by the lead interrogating detective).
- Chicago Police Department Missing Persons Records (1991). Cross-jurisdictional files for Jeremiah Weinberger and Ernest Miller.

SUBSECTION 3B: INVESTIGATION, ARREST & CRIME LAB
- Milwaukee Police Department Crime Scene Search & Evidence Log (July 22–26, 1991). Detailed inventory of Apartment 213, Oxford Apartments: recovery of refrigerator/freezer contents, 57-gallon chemical drums, preservation vats, Polaroid photographs, drills, and cutlery.
- Milwaukee County Medical Examiner's Office Autopsy & Anthropological Files (July–September 1991). Identification reports led by Chief Medical Examiner Dr. Jeffrey Jentzen, forensic odontologist Dr. L. Thomas Johnson, and physical anthropologist Dr. Kenneth A. Stormo.
- Associated Press & UPI Investigative Wire Coverage (July 23 – August 15, 1991). Contemporaneous reporting on crime scene forensics, Oxford Apartments evacuation, and forensic excavation at the West Allis property.

SUBSECTION 3C: TRIAL & LEGAL PROCEEDINGS (1992)
- State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer, Case No. F-912552, Milwaukee County Circuit Court (Judge Laurence C. Gram Jr. presiding). Complete official trial transcripts:
  * Guilt Phase: Formal guilty-but-insane plea entered January 13, 1992.
  * Sanity Phase (Phase II): Jan 27 – Feb 15, 1992. Opening and closing statements of Milwaukee County District Attorney E. Michael McCann and Defense Counsel Gerald Boyle.
  * Jury Verdict Documentation: Form verdicts finding Dahmer legally sane (10–2 / 11–1) across all 15 counts (February 15, 1992).
  * Sentencing Hearing Transcripts (February 17, 1992): Imposition of 15 consecutive life sentences (totaling 937 years) without parole eligibility.
  * Victim-Impact Statements: Court transcripts of statements delivered by family members (including Rita Isbell, Joyce Balcome, Stanley Miller, and Donald Bradehoft).
- Wisconsin Statutory Criminal Code (1991–1992). Wis. Stat. § 971.15 (Mental responsibility/legal sanity standard adapting the American Law Institute test: capacity to appreciate wrongfulness or conform conduct).
- Court TV Complete Video Archive (January 27 – February 17, 1992). Full gavel-to-gavel recorded courtroom proceedings.

SUBSECTION 3D: 1994 PRISON AFTERMATH
- Wisconsin Department of Corrections Official Incident Reports (November 28, 1994). Investigation into the fatal assault of Jeffrey Dahmer and Jesse Anderson in the gymnasium of Columbia Correctional Institution (Portage, WI) by inmate Christopher Scarver.
- State of Wisconsin v. Christopher J. Scarver, Columbia County Circuit Court (1995). Indictment, psychiatric evaluation, and consecutive life sentence proceedings.

6. ANALYSIS TAB (/analysis)
================================================================================

SUBSECTION 6A: PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE & SANITY EVALUATION
- State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey L. Dahmer, Case No. F-912552 (1992). Sanity Phase Psychiatric Testimony Transcripts:
  * Dr. Park E. Dietz (Prosecution Forensic Psychiatrist) — Expert testimony diagnosing necrophilic paraphilia, partialism, and alcohol dependence; finding substantial capacity to appreciate wrongfulness and conform conduct under Wis. Stat. § 971.15.
  * Dr. Judith V. Becker (Defense Clinical Psychologist) — Expert testimony diagnosing Borderline Personality Disorder, necrophilic paraphilia, and schizoid traits.
  * Dr. Frederick S. Berlin (Defense Psychiatrist, Johns Hopkins University) — Expert testimony on paraphilic impulse disorders, compulsive erotic obsessions, and impaired volitional control.
  * Dr. Carl Wahlstrom (Defense Forensic Psychiatrist) — Expert testimony on borderline pathology and psychotic decompensation.
  * Dr. George B. Palermo (Court-Appointed Psychiatrist) — Clinical evaluation finding severe personality disorder with sexual sadism and antisocial traits.
  * Dr. Samuel Friedman (Defense Clinical Psychologist) — Testing analysis diagnosing Schizotypal Personality Disorder.
  * Dr. Frederick Fosdal (Prosecution Forensic Psychiatrist) — Evaluation finding severe antisocial/character disorder with intact capacity for conforming behavior.
- American Psychiatric Association (1987). "Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Third Edition, Revised (DSM-III-R)." (The diagnostic standard in legal and clinical effect during the 1992 trial).
- Palermo, George B. (1994). "The Faces of Violence." Charles C Thomas Publisher. (Clinical analysis of severe borderline pathology, necrophilia, and extreme social detachment).

SUBSECTION 6B: SYSTEMIC FAILURES & INSTITUTIONAL BREAKDOWNS

- Milwaukee Fire and Police Commission Trial & Disciplinary Board Records (1991–1992). Formal inquiry, dismissal findings, and official hearing transcripts regarding Officers John Balcerzak, Joseph Gabrish, and Peter Laufer.
- Federal Court Civil Rights Records:
  * Estate of Sinthasomphone v. City of Milwaukee, 785 F. Supp. 1343 (E.D. Wis. 1992) and 838 F. Supp. 1320 (E.D. Wis. 1993). Federal civil rights complaint under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 alleging equal protection violations, racial bias, and discriminatory police custom.
  * Balcerzak v. City of Milwaukee, 980 F. Supp. 983 (E.D. Wis. 1997) and 163 F.3d 993 (7th Cir. 1998). Arbitration appeal and reinstatement litigation regarding officer dismissals.
- Wisconsin Department of Health and Social Services Probation Supervision Records:
  * Weinberger v. State of Wisconsin, 906 F. Supp. 485 (W.D. Wis. 1995) and 105 F.3d 1182 (7th Cir. 1997). Official case records evaluating probation officer Donna Chester's caseload, face-to-face contacts, and supervisor waivers.
- U.S. Commission on Civil Rights (November 1994). "Racial and Ethnic Tensions in American Communities: The Milwaukee Report." Official findings concerning long-standing discriminatory practices, police culture, and community complaints.

EDITORIAL METHODOLOGY & RESEARCH STANDARDS
- Multi-Source Verification Rule: Every factual claim concerning dates, victim ages, crime locations, and police interactions is cross-referenced across at least two independent primary records or contemporary court documents.
- Discrepancy Transparency Principle: Where primary records conflict (such as the timeline of the Steven Tuomi homicide or minor age variations in 1991 newspapers), the archive explicitly states the divergence rather than enforcing artificial precision.
- Non-Graphic Representation & Human Dignity Standard: Strict omission of graphic crime-scene photographs or sensationalized autopsy imagery, prioritizing the biographical preservation and human dignity of the victims.
- Fact vs. Interpretation Distinction: Rigid separation between established legal/physical facts (court records, ballistics, pathology) and speculative or theoretical models (forensic psychiatry, psychiatric diagnoses, psychological theories).
