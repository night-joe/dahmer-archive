## 1. Verified Findings

### Section A: Historical, Economic, and Institutional Context (Milwaukee 1987–1991)

#### 1. Macroeconomic Restructuring & Deindustrialization (1960s–1991)
- **Manufacturing Employment Contraction in the City of Milwaukee:**
  - Manufacturing employment in the City of Milwaukee peaked in **1963 at 119,284 jobs** [SRC-05].
  - Over the subsequent quarter-century, employment contracted dramatically: **118,600 in 1967**; **91,400 in 1977**; **77,900 in 1982** (following the back-to-back 1980–1982 national recessions); and **63,900 in 1987** [SRC-05].
  - By 1990, the central city had lost over **46% of its industrial job base** relative to 1967, shedding tens of thousands of family-supporting, unionized jobs [SRC-05].
- **Metropolitan Area Manufacturing Contraction (Milwaukee-Waukesha PMSA):**
  - Total manufacturing employment in the Milwaukee Primary Metropolitan Statistical Area (PMSA) peaked in **1979 at approximately 215,000 to 220,000 jobs** [SRC-06].
  - Between 1979 and the 1991 recession, the metropolitan area suffered a net loss of over **50,000 manufacturing jobs** [SRC-06, SRC-07]. In the Seventh Federal Reserve District (Chicago), nearly one-fourth of manufacturing positions disappeared during the early 1980s downturn [SRC-06].
- **Major Corporate Closures & Industrial Downsizing:**
  - **Allis-Chalmers:** Once Milwaukee County's industrial anchor, operating a 160-acre complex in West Allis that employed over 20,000 workers at its post-WWII peak and roughly 10,000 in the late 1970s. After severe losses, Allis-Chalmers liquidated its tractor operations in 1985 and filed for **Chapter 11 bankruptcy on June 29, 1987**, terminating thousands of unionized livelihoods and destabilizing retiree pensions [SRC-05, SRC-08].
  - **A.O. Smith:** The automotive frame manufacturer at 3533 N. 27th Street on Milwaukee's North Side employed nearly 10,000 workers during the 1970s. Following severe layoffs in 1980–1982, an advertisement for a few hundred openings at Wisconsin State Fair Park drew an estimated **15,000 to 20,000 job applicants** lining up for blocks [SRC-05, SRC-08].
  - **Briggs & Stratton:** The world's largest producer of small engines employed roughly 11,000 union workers (AIW / UPIU Local 7232) in Milwaukee in 1980. Management shifted production to non-union Southern facilities throughout the 1980s, permanently eliminating thousands of central-city jobs [SRC-05, SRC-08].
  - **Brewing Industry:** Joseph Schlitz Brewing Company permanently closed its historic brewery in 1981 following a strike and corporate acquisition by Stroh, leaving Pabst and Miller as the remaining large breweries [SRC-08].
- **Racial Disparities and Central-City Joblessness (Marc V. Levine Studies):**
  - In 1970, **55.0% of employed African American men** in metropolitan Milwaukee worked in manufacturing, compared to 25.4% of white men [SRC-05].
  - Between 1970 and 1990, the employment rate for prime-age Black males in metro Milwaukee dropped by **over 20 percentage points** [SRC-05].
  - In the 1980 Census, **41.0%** of working-age Black males were not employed (either unemployed or detached from the labor force); by the 1990 Census, that figure reached **46.6%** (exceeding 50% by 2000) [SRC-05].
  - **Spatial Mismatch:** As manufacturing plants decentralized to suburban "WOW" counties (Waukesha, Ozaukee, Washington), exclusionary zoning and housing barriers prevented minority suburbanization: Black residency in Waukesha County remained below 1% in 1990 [SRC-05, SRC-07].
  - **Concentrated Poverty:** By 1990, inner-core North Side neighborhoods (including ZIP code 53206 and surrounding tracts) experienced poverty rates exceeding **40%**, creating one of the most economically segregated urban cores in the United States [SRC-01, SRC-05].

#### 2. 1990 U.S. Census Residential Segregation Indices
- **Hypersegregation Definition (Massey & Denton 1989, 1993):**
  - Douglas S. Massey and Nancy A. Denton introduced the sociological standard of **hypersegregation**, defined as exhibiting extreme segregation ($\ge 0.60$ or $\ge 0.70$) across at least four of five spatial dimensions: (1) *Evenness* (Dissimilarity Index, $D$), (2) *Exposure* (Isolation Index, $P^*$), (3) *Concentration* (Relative Concentration Index, $RCO$), (4) *Centralization* (Relative Centralization Index, $RCE$), and (5) *Clustering* (Spatial Proximity Index, $SP$) [SRC-04].
  - In both the 1980 and 1990 Censuses, metropolitan Milwaukee qualified as **hypersegregated** across four to five dimensions [SRC-04].
- **Empirical Segregation Metrics (1990 U.S. Census):**
  - **Index of Dissimilarity ($D$):**
    - The 1990 Black-White Dissimilarity Index for the Milwaukee-Waukesha PMSA was measured between **82.8 and 84.4** [SRC-01, SRC-02, SRC-04]:
      * $D = 82.8$: Farley & Frey (1994, *American Sociological Review*, Vol. 59) and U.S. Census Bureau CENSR-3 Table 5-2 (2002) using standardized 1990 PMSA census tracts.
      * $D = 84.4$: William H. Frey (2005, Research Report 05-573) using longitudinal boundary harmonization.
      * $D = 81.5$: Population Reference Bureau (2011) utilizing outlying fringe-adjusted boundaries.
    - Census Bureau Series CENSR-3 ranked Milwaukee-Waukesha as the **#1 most segregated large metropolitan area in the United States** in 1990 [SRC-01, SRC-02]. A score of ~83–84 means that over 83% of Black residents would have had to move to different tracts to achieve an even demographic balance matching the metro area.
  - **Isolation Index ($P^*$):**
    - Measured at **71.3** in the 1990 Census (up from 69.8 in 1980) [SRC-02].
    - This indicates that the average Black resident lived in a census tract that was 71.3% African American, even though African Americans constituted only 15% of the PMSA population [SRC-01, SRC-02].
- **Physical and Infrastructural Boundaries of Segregation:**
  - **Menomonee River Valley:** An industrial river basin cutting east-to-west across Milwaukee, historically serving as the city's racial "Mason-Dixon Line" dividing the predominantly Black North Side from the white and Latino South Side [SRC-07, SRC-08].
  - **16th Street Viaduct (James E. Groppi Unity Bridge):** Spanning 0.8 miles across the Menomonee Valley. On August 28, 1967, Father James Groppi and the NAACP Youth Council marched across the bridge into the South Side demanding open housing, meeting violent counter-protests. In 1988, the bridge was officially renamed the James E. Groppi Unity Bridge [SRC-08].
  - **Interstate 43 (North-South Freeway):** Constructed in the 1960s directly through the commercial and cultural core of Bronzeville along North 7th and 8th Streets, destroying hundreds of Black businesses, displacing thousands of families, and cementing an infrastructural barrier [SRC-05, SRC-08].
  - **Holton Street & Milwaukee River:** Physical boundary separating the central North Side from the East Side and Riverwest [SRC-08].
  - **The Near South Side:** Centered around Walker's Point, Mitchell Street, and South 16th Street (Cesar Chavez Drive), which emerged by 1990 as Milwaukee's vibrant Mexican-American and Puerto Rican Latino community center [SRC-07, SRC-08].

#### 3. Near West Side / Avenues West & Oxford Apartments Profile
- **Exact Geographic Coordinates:**
  - Oxford Apartments was located at **924 N. 25th Street**, on the east side of North 25th Street between West Kilbourn Avenue (900 North) and West State Street (1000 North) in the **Avenues West** neighborhood of the Near West Side (ZIP code **53233**, Census Tract **137**) [SRC-01, SRC-08].
- **Building Specifications:**
  - Constructed circa 1955; two-story (plus garden/basement level) brick building containing **49 multi-family rental units** [SRC-08, SRC-32].
  - Jeffrey Dahmer leased **Apartment 213** in May 1990 (paying ~$300/month) and resided there until his arrest on July 22, 1991. He murdered 12 of his 17 victims inside this apartment [SRC-22: pp. 2–4; SRC-23: pp. 11–12].
- **Neighborhood Transition & Socioeconomic Profile (1990 Census):**
  - Late 19th Century: Known as "Mansion Row" along Grand Avenue (now Wisconsin Avenue) for wealthy industrial leaders (e.g., Pabst Mansion at 2000 W. Wisconsin Ave) [SRC-08].
  - Mid-to-Late 20th Century: Subdivided into rooming houses, residential hotels, and multi-unit complexes [SRC-08].
  - **Rental Domination:** In 1990, Census Tract 137 and ZIP 53233 recorded a renter occupancy rate exceeding **85% to 90%**, among the highest in Wisconsin [SRC-01, SRC-08].
  - **Transience and Absentee Landlords:** A highly transient population combining Marquette University students, low-wage service workers, and fixed-income retirees, with absentee management and minimal tenant screening [SRC-08, SRC-32]. This environment enabled Dahmer to deflect neighbor complaints about intense putrefaction odors, chemical fumes, and late-night sawing noises by claiming his freezer had broken or that he was working on taxidermy hobbies [SRC-22: p. 2; SRC-32].
- **Demolition by Marquette's Campus Circle Project:**
  - In 1991–1992, Marquette University under President Father Albert J. DiUlio, SJ, launched the $50 million **Campus Circle Project** to redevelop Avenues West [SRC-08].
  - In 1992, Campus Circle purchased the Oxford Apartments for **$325,000**. Between **November 16 and November 20, 1992**, the entire 49-unit building was demolished to prevent macabre commercial exploitation [SRC-08, SRC-32]. The site remains a fenced, vacant grass lot.

#### 4. Milwaukee Police Department (MPD) Administrative History
- **Chief Harold A. Breier (February 1964 – June 1984):**
  - Joined MPD in 1940; appointed Chief of Police in February 1964; served over 20 years under statutory **lifetime tenure** ("chief-for-life") [SRC-09, SRC-10].
  - Operated an authoritarian, paramilitary command structure with absolute control over personnel, internal investigations, and rules. Vehemently opposed civilian oversight, affirmative action, and community policing, dismissing all complaints of brutality or racial bias [SRC-10, SRC-11].
- **The 1984 "Breier Bill" (Wisconsin Senate Bill 56 / 1983 Act 179):**
  - In April 1984, the Wisconsin Legislature enacted Senate Bill 56 (amending Wis. Stat. § 62.50). The statute transferred policy-making power to the civilian **Fire and Police Commission (FPC)** and authorized the FPC to issue binding policy directives to the chief [SRC-10, SRC-15].
  - Rather than submit to civilian governance, Chief Breier retired in **June 1984**, ending the era of unilateral police chief rule [SRC-09, SRC-10].
- **Chief Robert J. Ziarnik (1984 – 1989):**
  - Department insider who implemented modest administrative modernizations but struggled with an insular police culture and political conflicts with city hall, resigning in 1989 [SRC-09, SRC-10].
- **Chief Philip Arreola (November 1989 – 1996):**
  - Appointed November 1989 as Milwaukee's **first Hispanic police chief** and first modern outsider chief (previously Port Huron, Michigan Police Chief) [SRC-09, SRC-10].
  - Mandated by the FPC to introduce **community-oriented policing (COP)** and rebuild minority trust, launching pilot projects in 1989–1990 [SRC-09, SRC-10].
  - Met intense resistance from the union, the **Milwaukee Police Association (MPA)**, led by Bradley DeBraska [SRC-10, SRC-32].
  - **August 7, 1991 No-Confidence Vote:** When Chief Arreola suspended responding officers John Balcerzak, Joseph Gabrish, and Rick Tricia following the discovery of Dahmer's crimes and their May 27, 1991 surrender of Konerak Sinthasomphone, the MPA conducted an emergency union vote. Rank-and-file officers voted **94% "no confidence" (approx. 1,400 to 80)** against Arreola, demanding his resignation [SRC-10, SRC-31, SRC-32]. Arreola refused to step down, illustrating the profound institutional gulf between reform leadership and union culture.
- **Civilian Oversight Flashpoints:**
  - **Daniel Bell Homicide (February 2, 1958 / 1979 / 1981):** 22-year-old Daniel Bell was shot in the neck by MPD Patrolman Thomas Grady after fleeing a broken taillight. Grady planted a knife and claimed self-defense. In 1978–1979, Grady's partner Louis Krause confessed to the cover-up; Grady was convicted in 1979 of reckless homicide and perjury. In 1981, a federal civil rights jury awarded the Bell family $1.6 million, affirmed on appeal (*Bell v. City of Milwaukee*, 746 F.2d 1205 [7th Cir. 1984]), ruling that high-ranking MPD officials orchestrated an intentional 20-year racial cover-up [SRC-10, SRC-11].
  - **Ernest Lacy Homicide (July 9, 1981):** 22-year-old Ernest Lacy was stopped by Tactical Enforcement Unit officers near 23rd and Wisconsin Avenue (blocks from 25th St), mistaken for a rape suspect. Officers pinned him face-down, handcuffed him, and placed him in a police van where officers compressed his back and chest. Lacy died of cerebral anoxia (asphyxiation). The actual suspect was arrested hours later. Criminal charges against officers were dismissed or acquitted. Protests led by the **Coalition for Justice for Ernest Lacy** (Howard Fuller, Michael McGee) mobilized thousands. In 1984, the City settled for $600,000, and the Wisconsin Legislature enacted the **"Lacy Law,"** making failure to render medical care to an arrestee a felony [SRC-11, SRC-12, SRC-13].

#### 5. Milwaukee LGBTQ History & the HIV/AIDS Epidemic
- **Wisconsin Landmark 1982 Gay Rights Law:**
  - On **February 25, 1982**, Republican Governor Lee Sherman Dreyfus signed **Assembly Bill 70 (AB 70)** into law, enacted as **Chapter 112, Laws of 1981** (published March 1, 1982; effective March 2, 1982) [SRC-15].
  - Authored by Rep. David Clarenbach (D-Madison), Wisconsin became the **first state in the nation** to prohibit discrimination based on sexual orientation in employment, housing, credit, and public accommodations [SRC-15, SRC-16].
- **Established LGBTQ Infrastructure & Geography:**
  - Decades before 1991, Milwaukee sustained an active LGBTQ scene centered in the **Walker's Point** neighborhood on the Near South Side (especially South 2nd and South 5th Streets) [SRC-16].
  - Venues: **Club 219** (219 S. 2nd St, where Dahmer met several victims), The Phoenix, The Triangle, Ballgame, C'est La Vie, and **This Is It** (414 E. Wells St, operating continuously since 1968) [SRC-16].
  - **Black Nite Brawl (August 5, 1961):** LGBTQ patrons at the Black Nite bar (400 N. Plankinton Ave) fought off homophobic sailors, a milestone of resistance eight years before the Stonewall Riots [SRC-16, SRC-17].
- **HIV/AIDS Crisis Response:**
  - **BESTD Clinic (Brady East STD Clinic):** Founded in 1974 at 1240 E. Brady Street by gay activists and medical professionals as a free clinic [SRC-16, SRC-20].
  - First Wisconsin AIDS case diagnosed in **1982** [SRC-18].
  - **Milwaukee AIDS Project (MAP):** Formed in **January 1985** by the BESTD Clinic board; incorporated as an independent 501(c)(3) in August 1985 under Executive Director Doug Nelson, expanding to become the statewide **AIDS Resource Center of Wisconsin (ARCW)** (now Vivent Health) [SRC-18, SRC-19, SRC-20].
- **Police Surveillance, Entrapment & Systemic Estrangement:**
  - Despite statutory protections, MPD vice squads conducted surveillance, undercover park stings, and bathhouse raids (e.g., Club Baths) [SRC-16, SRC-17].
  - Officers regularly arrested gay men for lewd conduct and forwarded their names, home addresses, and employers to the *Milwaukee Journal* and *Milwaukee Sentinel* for publication, inflicting catastrophic personal and professional destruction [SRC-16, SRC-17].
  - **Pervasive Reporting Avoidance:** LGBTQ residents, particularly men of color and closeted individuals, feared contacting police when victimized by robberies, blackmail, or drugging, knowing they would face ridicule or arrest. This silence created an operational environment where predatory violence could recur undetected [SRC-16, SRC-17].

#### 6. Core Editorial Principle & Egger's "Less-Dead" Analytical Framework
- **Core Editorial Principle:** Macroeconomic decline, segregation, poverty, MPD institutional dysfunction, and homophobia did NOT cause Dahmer's crimes. Dahmer was driven by severe individual psychopathology and paraphilic compulsions for which he alone bears total moral and criminal culpability [SRC-21, SRC-27, SRC-29].
- The historical setting explains:
  1. The physical landscape: Why dense, transient apartment blocks like Oxford Apartments existed, where foul chemical odors and disturbances were dismissed by absentee managers.
  2. The social vulnerability: Why marginalized victims were navigating segregated spaces with limited economic safety nets.
  3. The institutional shield: Why families' missing-person reports were dismissed, why Black female bystanders (Sandra Smith, Nicole Childress, Glenda Cleveland) were ignored and threatened with arrest by responding officers on May 27, 1991, and why gay nightlife patrons avoided law enforcement contact.
- **Steven Egger's "Less-Dead" Framework:**
  - Criminologist Steven Egger (1990, 2002) coined "the less dead" to describe how serial murderers frequently prey on marginalized groups (runaways, gay men, minorities, sex workers) whose disappearances receive lower institutional priority and delayed investigation [SRC-21].
  - **Application Rule:** Egger's concept must be applied strictly as a sociological evaluation of institutional neglect. It must NEVER be used to assert that Dahmer calculated a sociological strategy based on police indifference. Rather, systemic bias functioned as an unintentional operational buffer that shielded his crimes from scrutiny.

#### 2. Childhood Chronology: Fact vs. Retrospective Psychiatry

  - Shortly before age 4, Dahmer underwent surgery for bilateral inguinal hernias.

- **Animal Carcasses, Bleaching & Scientific Curiosity (1968–1974):**
  - *Father's Role:* Around 1968–1970 (~age 8–10), Lionel Dahmer explored under the house with Jeffrey to clear out small animal bones left by prior tenants, demonstrating how to clean, bleach, and preserve bones with household chemicals as an educational chemistry lesson [SRC-23: p. 55; SRC-27: pp. 58–60].
  - *Stepmother Shari Dahmer's Statement:* FBI Vault Part 03, physical Page 55 (July 26, 1991 news clipping quoting family):
    `"Dahmer liked to use acid to scrape the meat off dead animals. He told his father, Lionel, he was doing experiments."`

To family and neighbors, this was perceived as an eccentric osteological/taxidermic hobby [SRC-27, SRC-28].

DAHMER stated that he got sexually aroused when he would look at the internal organs. DAHMER stated that he never had sex with any animals."`

- **Rigorous Disconfirmation of the Macdonald Triad:**
  - The classic serial killer triad (enuresis, pyromania, animal cruelty) does NOT apply to Dahmer.
  - *FBI Locator 1:* `Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer Part 03 of 19.pdf`, physical Page 8:

## 3. Unresolved Discrepancies

The evidentiary audit identified the following specific empirical and historical discrepancies across primary government files, census evaluations, and secondary memoirs that cannot be resolved through single-source selection and are transparently preserved with editorial guidance:

### 1. 1990 U.S. Census Black-White Dissimilarity Index Variations
- **The Variance:** Different authoritative demographic publications report slightly different figures for the Milwaukee-Waukesha metropolitan area in 1990:
  - **82.8:** Farley & Frey (1994, *American Sociological Review*, Vol. 59) and U.S. Census Bureau CENSR-3 Table 5-2 (2002), calculated using standardized 1990 PMSA census tracts.
  - **84.4:** William H. Frey (2005, Research Report 05-573) and CensusScope, calculated using longitudinal 2000 metropolitan boundary harmonization back-projected onto 1990 data.
  - **81.5:** Population Reference Bureau (2011), calculated using metropolitan boundaries adjusted for outlying rural/fringe tracts.
- **Resolution & Treatment:** The project reports the empirical range as **82.8 to 84.4** (with 81.5 noted as an outlying boundary adjustment). Under all methodological definitions, the Milwaukee-Waukesha metropolitan area ranked as the **#1 most racially segregated large metropolitan area in the United States in the 1990 Census**.

### 2. Chief Harold Breier's Appointment Date (1958 vs. 1964)
- **The Variance:** Informal summaries and early project notes occasionally confused the 1958 homicide of Daniel Bell with the commencement of Harold Breier's tenure as Chief of Police.
- **Resolution & Treatment:** City of Milwaukee and MPD archives confirm that Howard O. Johnson served as Chief of Police from 1957 until 1964. Harold A. Breier served as an inspector/detective in 1958 and was officially appointed Chief of Police in **February 1964**, serving until his retirement in **June 1984**. `02_context_content_plan_v02.txt` correctly listed 1964–1984; this audit confirms 1964 is the exact verified appointment date.

### 3. Milwaukee AIDS Project (MAP) / ARCW Name Evolution and Governance
- **The Variance:** General references vary between citing 1984 or 1985 for the founding of MAP, and occasionally conflate the project's inception with the formation of the AIDS Resource Center of Wisconsin (ARCW).
- **Resolution & Treatment:** Archival records of the Wisconsin LGBTQ History Project and UW-Milwaukee academic research (Wasielewski, 2018) establish:
  - **January 1985:** The BESTD Clinic Board of Directors established the Milwaukee AIDS Project (MAP) as a specialized clinic committee with municipal seed funding.
  - **August 1985:** MAP formally incorporated as an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation.
  - Doug Nelson was appointed Executive Director shortly thereafter, leading its expansion into the statewide AIDS Resource Center of Wisconsin (ARCW), later rebranded as Vivent Health. Both 1985 milestones (January committee formation, August independent incorporation) are historically verified.

### 4. Army Basic Training Installation: Fort McClellan, AL vs. Fort Leonard Wood, MO
- **The Variance:**
  - *Primary FBI Record:* `Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer Part 03 of 19.pdf`, physical Page 23 (official teletype to Director/Bonn, December 22, 1992):
    `"ENTERED THE ARMY ON 1/12/79, HAVING BASIC TRAINING AT FORT MCCLELLAN, ALABAMA, AND ADVANCED INFANTRY TRAINING AT FORT SAM HOUSTON, IN 1979."`
  - *Contrasting Memoir Accounts:* Lionel Dahmer, *A Father's Story* (1994), p. 77, and Brian Masters, *The Shrine of Jeffrey Dahmer* (1993), p. 48, state that Dahmer completed basic training at **Fort Leonard Wood, Missouri**, before transferring to Fort Sam Houston.
- **Resolution & Treatment:** Fort McClellan in Anniston, AL and Fort Leonard Wood in the Missouri Ozarks were both active basic combat training installations during 1979. Both sources possess high credibility (official FBI teletype quoting military records in Milwaukee FBI possession vs. father's contemporaneous correspondence). The locked plan notes both locations transparently.

### 5. Military Unit Designation Clerical Errors in FBI Records
- **The Variance:** FBI Vault Part 01 p. 29 references `"HEADQUARTERS COMPANY, 268TH ARMOR DIVISION, SECOND BATALLION, 68TH ARMOR IN BAUMHOLDER, GERMANY"` and Part 03 p. 23 references `"268 ARMOR DIVISION, SECOND BATTALION"`.
- **Resolution & Treatment:** The U.S. Army order of battle has never included a "268th Armor Division." The unit stationed in Baumholder, West Germany was the **2nd Battalion, 68th Armor Regiment** (`2-68 Armor`), an organic battalion of the **8th Infantry Division (Mechanized)**. The FBI teletype typist conflated the battalion designation (`2nd`) with the regiment number (`68th`) to invent a nonexistent division. Furthermore, Part 03 p. 23 expands the acronym "AIT" as "Advanced Infantry Training" rather than "Advanced Individual Training" for Medical Specialist MOS 91B. The locked plan documents the authentic military organization alongside the FBI typist errors.

### 6. Primary Vital Statistics Birth Date Variations
- **The Variances:**
  - *Oliver Lacy:* Official Milwaukee County charging complaint (August 1991) and press accounts list DOB as **June 23, 1968** (age 23 at disappearance). However, Illinois vital records and family death records report DOB as **June 23, 1967** (age 24 at disappearance).
  - *Jeremiah Weinberger:* FBI Vault Part 01 p. 25 records DOB as **September 25, 1967**; FBI Part 06 p. 9 and contemporary press report DOB as **September 29, 1967**.
  - *Steven Mark Hicks:* Summit County Sheriff missing report and FBI Part 03 p. 51 record DOB **June 22, 1959**; FBI Part 01 p. 25 teletype records DOB **June 27, 1959** (typographical error).
  - *James Doxtator Disappearance:* MPD initial notes record disappearance as **January 16/18, 1988** (age 14); FBI Vault Part 01 p. 60 records disappearance as **"spring of 1988"** (which, if after March 1, would make him 15).
- **Resolution & Treatment:** All variations are preserved and marked with asterisks (`*`) in the locked master registers matching the standards in `04_victims` (v05 locked) and `05_timeline` (v04 locked).

### 7. Attributed Parental Memoir Claims vs. Objective Medical Facts
- **The Variance:** In *A Father's Story* (1994), Lionel Dahmer attributes Jeffrey's emotional withdrawal and personality change to a double-hernia surgery performed shortly before his fourth birthday in early 1964. Popular media accounts have repeated this recollection as an established medical cause of his detachment.
- **Resolution & Treatment:** Medical records confirm the surgical hernia repair occurred, but forensic psychiatric evaluations (Dr. Park Dietz, Dr. Fred Berlin) confirm zero evidence of any causal connection between pediatric hernia surgery and adult necrophilic paraphilia. The locked plan strictly attributes this claim to parental recollection, maintaining a rigorous non-deterministic boundary.
