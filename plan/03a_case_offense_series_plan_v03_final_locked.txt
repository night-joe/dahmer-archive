1.2 RESTRAINED, SCHOLARLY EDITORIAL MANDATE
The Offense Series presents the documented historical progression of Dahmer's
offenses within a rigorous academic, legal, and forensic framework. Sensationalized
framing, true-crime melodrama, and lurid bodily depictions are strictly excluded:
  1. Core Analytical Equation:
     The presentation adheres to: EVENT + PATTERN + EVIDENCE + AFTERMATH.
     It expressly rejects: GORE + SPECTACLE + GRAPHIC NARRATIVE.
  2. Prohibited Terminology:
     No theatrical or sensationalized rhetoric (e.g., "house of horrors," "monster,"
     "macabre museum," "slaughterhouse," "fiend").
  3. Controlled Clinical Terminology:
     Factual, precise medical and legal vocabulary is enforced throughout:
     "drugged," "strangled," "incapacitated," "dismembered," "remains were recovered,"
     "remains were chemically dissolved," "forensic identification established."
  4. Human-Centered Dignity:
     Victims are identified by their full names, ages, human relationships, and
     aspirations. They are never treated as mere passive objects or crime-scene data.
  5. Attribution of Suspect Statements:
     All statements originating from Dahmer's post-arrest interrogations are
     explicitly attributed as his personal account to detectives (e.g., "Dahmer
     stated to investigators," "according to Dahmer's confession"), rather than
     presented uncritically as objective, uncorroborated fact.

SECTION 2: CHRONOLOGICAL & STATUTORY ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

2.1 THE THREE-PHASE RESEARCH FRAMEWORK
The seventeen homicides are grouped chronologically across three distinct
geographic and residential phases. This division serves as an editorial and
organizational framework for research navigation; it is NOT presented as an
objective, formalized criminological taxonomy:

  1. PHASE ONE — BATH TOWNSHIP, OHIO (1978):
     - Single offense: Steven Mark Hicks (June 18, 1978).
     - Physical Environment: Dahmer family residence, 4480 West Bath Road, Bath
       Township, Summit County, Ohio.
     - Defining Features: Offense committed immediately following high school
       graduation; isolated occurrence; followed by a nine-year hiatus during
       which Dahmer served in the U.S. Army and relocated to Wisconsin.
     - Jurisdiction: State of Ohio (Summit County Court of Common Pleas).

  2. PHASE TWO — WEST ALLIS & MILWAUKEE RESIDENCES (1987–1989):
     - Four offenses: Steven Tuomi (Sept 1987), James Doxtator (Jan 1988),
       Richard Guerrero (March 1988), Anthony Sears (March 1989).
     - Physical Environment: Ambassador Hotel (Room 507, Milwaukee) and the
       residence of Dahmer's grandmother, Catherine Dahmer, at 2357 S. 57th Street,
       West Allis, Wisconsin.
     - Defining Features: Resumption of fatal offenses; establishment of the
       enticement-drugging-strangulation pattern; basement dismemberment; transition
       from complete disposal to initial trophy retention (Anthony Sears skull).
     - Legal Context: Pre-1989 Wisconsin criminal statutes; offense committed while
       on pretrial release (Anthony Sears).

  3. PHASE THREE — OXFORD APARTMENTS, APARTMENT 213 (1990–1991):
     - Twelve offenses: Raymond Smith, Edward Smith, Ernest Miller, David Thomas,
       Curtis Straughter, Errol Lindsey, Anthony Hughes, Konerak Sinthasomphone,
       Matt Turner, Jeremiah Weinberger, Oliver Lacy, Joseph Bradehoft.
     - Physical Environment: Leased residential unit, Apartment 213, Oxford
       Apartments, 924 North 25th Street, Milwaukee, Wisconsin.
     - Defining Features: Independent residential base; dramatic increase in
       frequency (culminating in 4 killings in 17 days in July 1991); systematic
       accumulation of physical remains, bleached skulls, and soft tissue; surgical
       trephination experimentation; Polaroid photographic documentation.
     - Legal Context: Post-1989 Wisconsin criminal statutes; all offenses committed
       during active probation supervision with waived home visits.

2.2 THE MANDATORY 4-MILESTONE SEPARATION MODEL
A primary methodological defect in popular and journalistic accounts of the Dahmer
case is the conflation of distinct historical events—specifically, equating a
missing-person report date or an alleged charge date with the physical date of death.
This specification strictly enforces four independent, unblurred milestones for
every victim:

  - MILESTONE 1: LAST KNOWN CONTACT / SIGHTING DATE
    The final confirmed date and time a family member, acquaintance, coworker, or
    civilian witness saw or spoke with the victim alive. Where exact day is unconfirmed
    by primary records, the broader verified period is recorded with qualification.
  - MILESTONE 2: MISSING-PERSON REPORT DATE
    The calendar date local law enforcement formally received and logged a missing-
    person complaint from family or associates. For several victims, no formal
    report was ever filed with Milwaukee police prior to Dahmer's arrest.
  - MILESTONE 3: ALLEGED OFFENSE DATE & STATUTORY CHARGE
    The specific date of death alleged in sworn prosecutorial complaints (State of
    Wisconsin Case No. F-912542; State of Ohio Case No. CR-91-08-1686), derived
    primarily from suspect confessions, and the exact governing statutory code.
  - MILESTONE 4: FORENSIC IDENTIFICATION DATE & METHOD
    The date and scientific methodology (forensic odontology, AFIS latent fingerprint
    comparison, or forensic anthropology) by which medical examiners or crime
    laboratories conclusively identified the victim's remains.

2.3 STATUTORY CHARGING CLASSIFICATION ACROSS ERAS AND JURISDICTIONS
Dahmer was NOT charged with a uniform battery of identical offenses. Prosecutions
were divided across two sovereign states and two distinct eras of Wisconsin law:

  1. STATE OF OHIO — AGGRAVATED MURDER:
     - Governing Statute: Ohio Rev. Code § 2903.01 ("purposely, and with prior
       calculation and design, cause the death of another").
     - Applied To: Steven Mark Hicks (June 18, 1978 homicide).
     - Indictment: Summit County Grand Jury Indictment, Case No. CR-91-08-1686,
       filed September 24, 1991 following forensic identification of bone fragments.
     - Adjudication: Guilty plea entered May 1, 1992 before Judge James R. Williams
       (Common Pleas Judge James R. Williams presided over criminal plea and sentencing;
       prior baseline errata named William Spicer, who was Summit County Probate Judge);
       sentenced to life imprisonment with parole eligibility after 20 years,
       to run consecutive to Wisconsin sentences [5, 28].

  2. STATE OF WISCONSIN — PRE-1989 FIRST-DEGREE MURDER:
     - Governing Statute: Wis. Stat. § 940.01 (1987) ("Whoever causes the death
       of another human being with intent to kill that person or another...").
     - Applied To (2 Counts):
       * James "Jamie" Edward Doxtator (Count 13 of August 22, 1991 Information).
       * Richard Guerrero (Count 14 of August 22, 1991 Information).
     - Legislative History: Governed all homicides occurring prior to January 1, 1989.
       Both homicides occurred in 1988 at the West Allis residence.

  3. STATE OF WISCONSIN — POST-1989 FIRST-DEGREE INTENTIONAL HOMICIDE:
     - Governing Statute: Wis. Stat. § 940.01(1) (created by 1987 Wis. Act 399,
       effective January 1, 1989: "Except as provided in sub. (2), whoever causes
       the death of another human being with intent to kill that person or another
       is guilty of a Class A felony").
     - Applied To (13 Counts):
       * Anthony Lee Sears (Count 01, alleged March 26, 1989)
       * Raymond Smith (Count 02, alleged May 29, 1990)
       * Ernest Miller (Count 03, alleged September 3, 1990)
       * David Courtney Thomas (Count 04, alleged September 24, 1990)
       * Curtis Straughter (Count 05, alleged February 18, 1991)
       * Errol Lindsey (Count 06, alleged April 7, 1991)
       * Anthony Hughes (Count 07, alleged May 24, 1991)
       * Konerak Sinthasomphone (Count 08, alleged May 27, 1991)
       * Matt Turner (Count 09, alleged June 30, 1991)
       * Jeremiah Weinberger (Count 10, alleged July 7, 1991)
       * Oliver Lacy (Count 11, alleged July 15, 1991)
       * Joseph Bradehoft (Count 12, alleged July 19, 1991)
       * Edward W. Smith (Count 15, alleged June 1990; added August 22, 1991)

  4. THE CORPUS DELICTI EXCLUSION — STEVEN WALTER TUOMI:
     - Legal Standard: Under Wisconsin common law (State v. Verhasselt, 83 Wis. 2d 647,
       266 N.W.2d 342 (1978); Bies v. State, 53 Wis. 2d 322 (1972)), an extrajudicial
       confession alone is legally insufficient to establish corpus delicti or
       sustain a criminal bindover absent independent corroborating evidence that
       a crime occurred.
     - Application: Although Dahmer confessed to killing Steven Tuomi at the
       Ambassador Hotel in September or November 1987 (FBI Part 03 p. 10), no
       physical remains, biological traces, luggage records, or hotel evidence were
       ever recovered.
     - Disposition: On August 22, 1991, Milwaukee County District Attorney E. Michael
       McCann formally announced that the State of Wisconsin could not ethically or
       legally file homicide charges for the death of Steven Tuomi [3, 14, 16].
       Tuomi remains the only acknowledged Wisconsin homicide excluded from prosecution.

SECTION 3: PRIOR DOCUMENTED OFFENSES & INSTITUTIONAL SUPERVISION
================================================================================

Dahmer's offense history began a decade prior to his 1991 arrest. The series was
punctuated by multiple misdemeanor convictions, felony sexual assault proceedings,
and active probation supervision:

3.1 OCTOBER 1981 — BATH TOWNSHIP, OHIO (PUBLIC INTOXICATION / DISORDERLY CONDUCT)
- Anchor: #case-prior-1981-bath (Alias: #prior-1981-bath)
- Documented Incident: Dahmer was arrested by Bath Township police officers following
  a disturbance at a Ramada Inn involving severe public intoxication and disorderly
  conduct. The incident occurred several months after his Chapter 9 honorable discharge
  from the U.S. Army for alcohol rehabilitation failure.
- Adjudication: Pleaded guilty in municipal court; assessed a fine [2 (Pt 01 p. 27)].
- Significance: Earliest post-military civilian arrest; confirms chronic alcoholism
  documented in military medical records.

3.2 AUGUST 8, 1982 — WEST ALLIS, WISCONSIN (STATE FAIR PARK INDECENT EXPOSURE)
- Anchor: #case-prior-1982-state-fair (Alias: #prior-1982-statefair)
- Documented Incident: Dahmer was arrested by Wisconsin State Fair Park police
  officers in West Allis after exposing his genitals to fairgoers [2 (Pt 01 p. 27)].
- Adjudication: Convicted in municipal court of disorderly conduct; assessed a fine.
- Significance: Earliest Wisconsin criminal history entry in state and federal dockets;
  first documented sexual offense in Wisconsin.

3.3 SEPTEMBER 8, 1986 — MILWAUKEE, WISCONSIN (MCKINLEY BEACH INDECENT EXPOSURE)
- Anchor: #case-prior-1986-mckinley (Alias: #prior-1986-mckinley)
- Documented Incident: Arrested by Milwaukee Police Department officers after exposing
  himself to two boys (ages 11 and 14) near McKinley Beach along the Lake Michigan
  shoreline [2 (Pt 01 p. 27)].
- Adjudication: Charged initially with felony lewd and lascivious behavior. On March 10,
  1987, the charge was reduced to misdemeanor disorderly conduct in Milwaukee County
  Circuit Court; Dahmer was sentenced to one year of probation and fined $250.
- Supervision: Dahmer completed one year of unsupervised misdemeanor probation with
  no psychiatric evaluation ordered.

3.4 SEPTEMBER 25, 1988 — MILWAUKEE, WISCONSIN (CHILD SEXUAL ASSAULT OF SOMBAT SINTHASOMPHONE)
- Anchor: #case-prior-1988-assault (Aliases: #sombat-assault, #case-sombat-1988)
- Documented Incident: Dahmer offered $50 to 13-year-old Sombat Sinthasomphone (a
  Laotian refugee student) to pose for photographs at Catherine Dahmer's residence
  (225 24th Street). Dahmer drugged the boy with sleeping pills in a beverage,
  fondled his genitals, and took nude photographs. Sombat escaped after regaining
  partial consciousness and reported the assault to his family and police.
- Legal Processing & Case F-882410: Dahmer was arrested on September 27, 1988. He
  confessed to drugging and fondling the boy. Charged with Second-Degree Sexual
  Assault (Wis. Stat. § 940.225(2)) and Child Enticement (Wis. Stat. § 948.07) [29].
- Pretrial Release Homicide: While on pretrial release awaiting sentencing, Dahmer
  murdered Anthony Lee Sears on March 25–26, 1989.
- Sentencing (May 24, 1989): Milwaukee County Circuit Judge William Gardner sentenced
  Dahmer to one year in the Milwaukee County House of Correction with Huber work-release
  privileges (allowing him to work his night shift at Ambrosia Chocolate Company),
  followed by five years of probation.
- Probation Supervision Failure: Following release from the House of Correction in
  March 1990, Dahmer was assigned to Probation Agent Donna Chester under "maximum
  supervision." However, home-visit requirements were formally waived. All twelve
  homicides in Apartment 213 were committed while Dahmer was on active felony
  probation supervision [2 (Pt 01 p. 27)].

3.5 JULY 22, 1991 — MILWAUKEE, WISCONSIN (TERMINATING INCIDENT: ASSAULT & ESCAPE OF TRACY EDWARDS)
- Anchor: #case-tracy-edwards (Aliases: #edwards-escape, #case-terminating-offense)
- Documented Incident: At approximately 11:30 P.M. on Monday, July 22, 1991, 32-year-old
  Tracy Edwards escaped from Apartment 213 with a pair of handcuffs dangling from his
  left wrist. Edwards had been enticed to the apartment by Dahmer with an offer of $100
  to pose for photographs. Once inside, Dahmer handcuffed Edwards, brandished a large
  kitchen knife, forced him into the bedroom to watch the film The Exorcist III, and
  chanted while leaning against Edwards's chest, stating he wanted to eat his heart.
- Escape & Police Encounter: Edwards waited for an opportunity, punched Dahmer in the
  face, and fled through the front door. At 25th and Kilbourn Avenue, Edwards flagged
  down MPD patrol Officers Robert Rauth and Rolf Mueller [2 (Pt 01 pp. 4, 46, 63;
  Pt 03 pp. 11–12, 55)].
- Scene Entry & Arrest: Officers returned to Apartment 213 accompanied by Edwards to
  retrieve the handcuff key. When Dahmer reached toward a nightstand drawer, Officer
  Mueller opened it and observed Polaroid photographs depicting dismembered human
  bodies. After a physical struggle, Dahmer was subdued and arrested at 11:30 P.M.,
  terminating the offense series.

PHASE ONE: BATH TOWNSHIP, OHIO (1978)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. STEVEN MARK HICKS
Anchor: #case-steven-hicks (Alias: #steven-hicks)
-------------------------------------------------
Demographic & Jurisdictional Summary:
  - Victim: Steven Mark Hicks
  - Age: 18* (DOB June 22, 1959 / June 27, 1959 [2 (Pt 01 pp. 25, 39; Pt 03 p. 51)])
  - Residence: Coventry Township, Summit County, Ohio
  - Jurisdiction & Charge: State of Ohio (Summit County); Aggravated Murder (Ohio Rev. Code § 2903.01)

Case Milestones & Evidentiary Anchors:
  - Milestone 1: Last Known Contact — June 18, 1978. Hicks was hitchhiking along
    Route 21 near Chippewa Lake Park to attend a rock concert [1, 2 (Pt 03 pp. 51–52)].
  - Milestone 2: Missing-Person Report Date — June 24, 1978. Formally filed by his
    father, Richard Hicks, with the Summit County Sheriff's Office [2 (Pt 01 p. 60)].
  - Milestone 3: Alleged Offense Date & Charge — June 18, 1978. Indicted September 24,
    1991 for Aggravated Murder (Ohio Rev. Code § 2903.01; Summit County Case No. CR-91-08-1686).
  - Milestone 4: Forensic Identification Date & Method — September 13–14, 1991. Summit
    County Coroner Dr. William A. Cox and forensic odontologists matched excavated
    vertebrae, pelvic fragments, and tooth/molar specimens [4, 5, 17].

Standardized 5-Component Case Profile:
  1. Background & Encounter Pretext:
     Hicks, a recent high-school graduate, was hitchhiking along Ohio State Route 21.
     Dahmer, who was alone at his parents' residence following his own high-school
     graduation and his parents' separation, picked up Hicks under the pretext of
     offering him a ride and invited him to the house to drink beer and listen to music.
  2. Incapacitation Method & Offense Execution:
     After several hours of drinking, Hicks stated he intended to leave. Unwilling
     to let his companion depart, Dahmer struck Hicks from behind on the head with
     a 10-pound steel barbell, rendering him unconscious, and subsequently strangled
     him to death with the barbell bar across his throat [2 (Pt 01 p. 5; Pt 03 p. 9)].
  3. Postmortem Actions & Disposal/Preservation Evolution:
     Dahmer dragged the body into the crawl space beneath the house, dismembered the
     remains, and buried them in shallow backyard trenches. In 1980, during a brief
     visit home, Dahmer exhumed the skeleton, pulverized the bones with a sledgehammer,
     and scattered the fragments across the wooded ravine behind the property. Late
     July and August 1991 excavations by Bath Township police and the FBI recovered
     hundreds of bone and dental fragments [2 (Pt 01 p. 60; Pt 03 pp. 51–52)].
  4. Institutional Intersections & Law Enforcement Contacts:
     The Summit County Sheriff's Office investigated the disappearance in 1978 as a
     standard runaway/missing-person report. No connection was established to Dahmer
     or the Bath Road residence until Dahmer confessed to Milwaukee detectives on
     July 23, 1991 [2 (Pt 01 p. 5)].
  5. Criminological & Pattern Significance:
     First known homicide in the 17-victim series. Characterized by sudden panic upon
     impending departure rather than chemical pre-drugging. Initiated a nine-year
     interlude (1978–1987) during military service and initial Wisconsin residency
     before lethal offenses resumed.
  6. Sources & Legal Cross-References:
     FBI Vault Part 01 pp. 5, 25, 39, 60, 64; Part 03 pp. 9, 51–52; Summit County
     Common Pleas Case CR-91-08-1686; NYT July 31, 1991 [4]; Wash. Post May 2, 1992 [5].

Demographic & Jurisdictional Summary:

6.1 CLINICAL ANALYSIS OF SURGICAL EXPERIMENTATION & TREPHINATION BURR HOLES
Anchor: #case-control-experiments (Alias: #drilling-experiments)

During the 1992 sanity phase trial (State of Wisconsin v. Jeffrey Dahmer), psychiatric
experts and medical examiners testified extensively regarding Dahmer's documented
attempts to perform primitive surgical procedures on living victims:

  1. The Forensic Evidence:
     Physical examination of skeletal remains conducted by Chief Medical Examiner
     Dr. Jeffrey Jentzen and documented in MPD physical evidence rosters (FBI Part 06
     pp. 6–7) confirmed drill burr holes in four specific victim skulls:
     - Errol Lindsey (MPD Item BW)
     - Anthony Hughes (MPD Item BS)
     - Konerak Sinthasomphone (MPD Item BR)
     - Jeremiah Weinberger (MPD Item BQ)
     Investigators recovered an electric hand drill, assorted drill bits, and
     syringes from Apartment 213.

  2. The Documented Method:
     According to Dahmer's statements to Detectives Patrick Kennedy and Dennis Murphy,
     and corroborating testimony by prosecution psychiatrist Dr. Frederick Fosdal and
     defense psychiatrist Dr. Fred Berlin:
     - Victims were administered heavy doses of sleeping pills (primarily Halcion) in drinks.
     - While the victim was unconscious, Dahmer used an electric hand drill to penetrate
       the skull, creating a burr hole into the cranial cavity / frontal lobe.
     - Dahmer injected chemicals—hydrochloric acid or boiling water—through the burr hole
       using a syringe.

  3. The Clinical Objective — Compulsion for Submissive Control:
     Sensationalized media accounts popularized the term "zombie experiments." In the
     clinical and trial record, however, experts framed this behavior as an attempt to
     create an unresisting, compliant, permanently submissive companion:
     - Prosecution expert Dr. Park Dietz testified that Dahmer sought to eliminate the
       possibility of rejection, departure, or autonomous will without having to
       manage an inert corpse.
     - When the procedures proved uniformly lethal (producing coma, brain swelling,
       or renewed agony leading Dahmer to strangle the victim), Dahmer acknowledged
       that the attempts were failures.
     - The court record established that these actions reflected extreme necrophilic
       and paraphilic pathology aimed at absolute control, but did not constitute
       a loss of cognitive sanity under Wis. Stat. § 971.15 [8].

6.2 EVOLUTION OF TROPHY RETENTION & REMAINS PRESERVATION
Anchor: #case-trophy-retention (Alias: #remains-preservation)

The disposition of victim remains underwent a clear evolutionary progression across
the thirteen-year span:

  - STAGE 1: COMPLETE SKELETAL PULVERIZATION & DISPOSAL (1978–1988)
    In the Hicks, Tuomi, Doxtator, and Guerrero homicides, Dahmer prioritized complete
    elimination of evidence. Hicks's bones were buried, exhumed, and pulverized with a
    sledgehammer. Tuomi's remains were disposed of in municipal trash. Doxtator's and
    Guerrero's skeletons were crushed and scattered in the grandmother's backyard. No
    permanent relics were retained.

  - STAGE 2: INITIAL CHEMICAL EXPERIMENTATION & PERMANENT TROPHY RETENTION (1989)
    With Anthony Sears (March 1989), Dahmer preserved his first permanent physical relic:
    the skull was defleshed, boiled in bleach, spray-painted an off-white hue, and
    retained (MPD Item BU). Dahmer also preserved Sears's scalp and viscera, transporting
    them to Apartment 213 in 1990.

  - STAGE 3: SYSTEMATIC ACCUMULATION & RELIQUARY IN APARTMENT 213 (1990–1991)
    Following his move to Oxford Apartments, preservation became an organized feature:
    * Skulls: Eight human skulls were recovered from Apartment 213:
      - 4 bleached and painted skulls: Sears (BU), Raymond Smith (BV), Miller (BT),
        Straughter (BX).
      - 4 drilled experimental skulls: Lindsey (BW), Hughes (BS), Sinthasomphone (BR),
        Weinberger (BQ).
    * Cold Storage: Refrigerator and freezer compartments were utilized for immediate
      preservation: Oliver Lacy's head in the refrigerator; Lacy's heart in a freezer
      container; Ernest Miller's bicep muscle; Matt Turner's, Jeremiah Weinberger's,
      and Joseph Bradehoft's heads in the freezer.
    * Chemical Dissolution: A 57-gallon drum containing hydrochloric acid was installed
      in the bedroom to dissolve soft tissue from torsos, leaving bones for cleaning.
    * Proposed Skeletal Altar: Dahmer told detectives he planned to construct an
      elaborate altar in his apartment flanked by painted human skeletons and skulls,
      concealed behind a black curtain, serving as a private shrine [2 (Pt 03 pp. 11–12)].

6.3 PHOTOGRAPHIC DOCUMENTATION AS EVIDENTIARY RECORD
Anchor: #case-photography-evidence (Alias: #polaroid-evidence)

Dahmer systematically utilized a Polaroid camera to photograph victims at various
stages of sedation, death, and dismemberment:
  - Crime Scene Recovery: Eighty-four Polaroid photographs were discovered in an open
    nightstand drawer in Apartment 213 on the night of July 22, 1991.
  - Evidentiary Value: The photographs provided indisputable, objective proof linking
    specific missing men to Apartment 213, particularly victims whose remains had been
    completely destroyed or dissolved:
    * MPD Item CF: Depicted David Courtney Thomas deceased in the apartment, enabling
      positive photographic identification on July 29, 1991 despite zero recovered remains.
    * MPD Item CE: Depicted Ernest Miller's body and Dahmer's forearm.
    * MPD Item CD: Depicted Jeremiah Weinberger and Dahmer's leg.
  - Clinical Function: Psychiatric experts noted that the photographs functioned as
    substitute erotic stimuli allowing Dahmer to re-experience the offenses and maintain
    control during intervals between homicides [8].

6.4 PARAPHILIC NECROPHILIA & POSTMORTEM ACTS IN THE PSYCHIATRIC RECORD
Anchor: #case-necrophilia-record (Alias: #psychiatric-record)

In both the interrogation statements and expert testimony during the 1992 sanity phase:
  - Dahmer acknowledged engaging in postmortem sexual acts with the bodies of several
    victims prior to dismemberment.
  - Defense experts Dr. Fred Berlin and Dr. Judith Becker testified that Dahmer suffered
    from severe paraphilia (necrophilia) that overwhelmed his volitional capacity, arguing
    he lacked substantial capacity to conform his conduct to the law under Wis. Stat. § 971.15.
  - Prosecution experts Dr. Park Dietz and Dr. Phillip Resnick countered that while
    Dahmer exhibited extreme sexual paraphilia and borderline personality disorder, he
    demonstrated intact volitional capacity by planning offenses, altering techniques to
    avoid detection, drugging victims surreptitiously, lying to police officers, and
    restricting his killings to periods and locations where he would not be interrupted.
  - The jury's 10–2 verdict finding Dahmer legally sane affirmed that extreme paraphilic
    desires do not negate legal criminal responsibility under Wisconsin law [8].
