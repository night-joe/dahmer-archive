STATUS
------
This document is the working reference for the Timeline tab.

It is NOT final website copy.
The timeline has been fact-checked and structurally refined from the user's
draft. Some dates in the historical record remain approximate or conflict
between sources. Where that happens, the final timeline should preserve the
uncertainty rather than force a false precision.

CORE PURPOSE
------------
The Timeline is the chronological spine of the research project.

It should answer:
- What happened?
- When did it happen?
- Which institution or authority was involved?
- Why is the event relevant to the broader historical record?

The Timeline should not become a second Systemic Failures article. It can
identify institutional contact and significance, but detailed interpretation
belongs in the relevant topic pages.

Use "Date / Period" where an exact date is not reliably established.

DATE-HANDLING RULE
------------------
Dates should be labeled according to what the source actually establishes.

Possible date labels:
- exact date
- last seen
- month/year
- approximate period
- later-documented event

Do not convert:
- last-seen dates into proven death dates
- dates from later recollections into contemporaneous certainty
- Dahmer's approximate recollection into exact chronology

This is especially important for Steven Tuomi.

TIMELINE ORGANIZATION
---------------------
The full /timeline page should be divided into visually distinct historical
eras:

1. 1960–1978 — Early life and first known killing
2. 1979–1986 — Military service and early legal contacts
3. 1987–1989 — Return to killing and pre-probation period
4. 1990 — Apartment 213 and escalation
5. 1991 — Final months, discovery, arrest and investigation
6. 1992 — Trial, sentencing and immediate aftermath
7. 1994 — Death in prison
8. Later legal / institutional re-examination

The eras are visual groupings, not separate pages.

FACT-CHECKED MASTER TIMELINE
----------------------------

1960
-----
May 21, 1960
Event:
Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer is born in Milwaukee, Wisconsin.

Institutional Contact:
Vital records / none beyond ordinary birth registration.

Significance:
Establishes the beginning of the biographical timeline. The FBI records his
place of birth as Milwaukee, Wisconsin.

Refinement:
Do not force an institutional-failure interpretation onto a birth record.

1978
----
June 18, 1978
Event:
Steven Hicks is last seen after accepting a ride from Dahmer in the Bath
Township, Ohio area. Dahmer later confessed to killing him.

Institutional Contact:
Ohio missing-person / local law-enforcement records.

Significance:
First known killing in Dahmer's later confession. Hicks remained an
unresolved missing-person case until evidence developed after the 1991
investigation.

Important:
The date June 18 is strongly supported as Hicks's last known sighting.
Dahmer's later account supplied the murder details.

Source:
FBI Vault, Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer Part 03.

1979–1981
----------
December 1979 / 1979 enlistment through March 1981
Event:
Dahmer serves in the U.S. Army, trains as a medical specialist/combat medic,
and is stationed in West Germany.

Institutional Contact:
United States Army.

Significance:
Military records document repeated alcohol-related counseling and other
disciplinary/administrative problems. He was enrolled in an alcohol
rehabilitation program but did not enter treatment, and he was honorably
discharged in March 1981.

Refinement:
Do NOT write:
"discharged without psychiatric intervention."

The available record supports repeated counseling for alcohol abuse and a
rehabilitation referral/program, not a conclusion that no intervention of any
kind occurred.

The FBI-derived military records document counseling on multiple dates and
the March 1981 honorable discharge.

1981
----
October 1981
Event:
After returning to Ohio, Dahmer is arrested in Bath Township for disorderly
conduct connected to intoxication/open alcohol at a Ramada Inn.

Institutional Contact:
Bath Township Police / local court.

Significance:
Adds an important documented legal contact between military discharge and
Dahmer's later Wisconsin arrests.

Refinement:
This event was missing from the original timeline and should be included if
the goal is a complete institutional chronology.

1982
----
August 8, 1982
Event:
Dahmer is arrested by State Fair Park police for disorderly conduct.

Institutional Contact:
Wisconsin State Fair Park Police / local court.

Significance:
Earliest confirmed Wisconsin criminal-history entry in the FBI record.

Refinement:
Do not include the "$60 fine" unless a primary source is attached. The FBI
record confirms conviction/fine but the amount is not established there.

1986–1987
----------
September 8, 1986
Event:
Dahmer is arrested by Milwaukee police for lewd and lascivious behavior.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department / court.

Significance:
Begins another documented criminal-history episode.

Refinement:
The charge was later reduced/changed. Do NOT say he was convicted of lewd and
lascivious behavior.

March 10, 1987
Event:
The 1986 charge is reduced to disorderly conduct; Dahmer is convicted and
sentenced to one year of probation and fined.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee court / probation.

Significance:
Documents a prior probationary criminal-justice contact before the 1988
child-sexual-assault case.

1987
----
September 15, 1987
Event:
Steven Tuomi is last seen in Milwaukee after leaving work.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Important starting point for the Tuomi chronology.

November 1987 — date uncertain
Event:
Dahmer later identifies Steven Tuomi as his second victim and says the death
occurred at the Ambassador Hotel.

Institutional Contact:
Hotel / later police investigation.

Significance:
Marks the return to killing after the 1978 Hicks case.

Refinement:
Do not present "November 20, 1987" as an independently established exact
death date unless the final source note can support it. The FBI interview
record says Dahmer placed the killing "somewhere around 1986 or 1987,"
while Tuomi's known last sighting was September 15, 1987.

This is one of the timeline's deliberate uncertainty markers.

1988
----
January 16, 1988
Event:
James "Jamie" Doxtator disappears and is later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee-area missing-person records.

Significance:
First known Milwaukee killing in the renewed sequence.

March 24, 1988
Event:
Richard Guerrero disappears and is later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee police / missing-person investigation.

Significance:
Adds a second unresolved disappearance before Dahmer's 1989 conviction.

September 1988
Event:
Dahmer sexually assaults 13-year-old Somsack Sinthasomphone.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department / Milwaukee County Circuit Court.

September 27, 1988
Event:
Dahmer is arrested and charged in the Somsack Sinthasomphone case.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department / Milwaukee County Circuit Court.

Significance:
This is the most consequential pre-1991 criminal-justice intervention
because Dahmer enters the probation system afterward.

1989
----
March 25, 1989
Event:
Anthony Sears is last seen and is later established as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police / missing-person records; Dahmer was free while his 1988
case was still pending.

Significance:
The killing occurred between Dahmer's 1988 arrest and May 1989 sentencing,
making the chronology relevant to the later examination of pretrial release.

Refinement:
Do not call this a proven "pretrial-services failure" unless the source
establishes that conclusion. State the documented legal status and let the
Systemic Failures article analyze the supervision issue.

May 24, 1989
Event:
Dahmer is sentenced for the 1988 sexual-assault/child-enticing convictions.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court / Wisconsin probation system.

Significance:
He receives one year in the Milwaukee House of Correction with work-release
privileges and five years of probation. Probation becomes a central
institutional context for the 1990–1991 killings.

Refinement:
Do not collapse the sentencing into "five years probation" alone; both the
custodial sentence and probation are important.

1990
----
March 1990
Event:
Dahmer is released from the House of Correction and begins serving probation.

Institutional Contact:
Wisconsin probation/parole system.

Significance:
Places him under active community supervision during the period in which
most later killings occurred.

May 1990
Event:
Dahmer moves from his grandmother's West Allis home into Apartment 213 at
924 N. 25th Street, Oxford Apartments, Milwaukee.

Institutional Contact:
Landlord/building management; Wisconsin probation.

Significance:
Creates the physical setting for the majority of the remaining murders.

Refinement:
Do not write that his probation officer "failed mandatory quarterly home visits."
The relevant Wisconsin rule for maximum supervision required face-to-face
contact at least every 14 days and home visits at least every 30 days unless
waived by a supervisor. Donna Chester did not make home visits because waivers
were approved by her supervisor.

May 1990
----------
May 20, 1990
Event:
Raymond "Ricky Beeks" Smith is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person investigation.

Significance:
First known murder at Apartment 213.

June 14, 1990
Event:
Edward "Eddie" Smith is last seen leaving the Phoenix Bar and later
identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Second known murder at Apartment 213.

September 3, 1990
Event:
Ernest Miller is last seen visiting in Milwaukee and later identified as a
Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Third known murder at Apartment 213.

September 24, 1990
Event:
David Thomas is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Fourth known murder at Apartment 213 in 1990.

1990–1991
---------
1990–1991
Event:
Apartment residents and neighbors report persistent unpleasant odors coming
from Apartment 213. Dahmer gives explanations involving spoiled food/freezer
problems.

Institutional Contact:
Oxford Apartments management / neighbors.

Significance:
Becomes an important community-information thread for later analysis of
housing and institutional response.

Refinement:
Do not state that "complaints were ignored" as an absolute unless a source
demonstrates the full chain of notice. The timeline should document the
complaints and explanations; Systemic Failures should analyze the response.

1991
----
February 18, 1991
Event:
Curtis Straughter is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Marks renewed killing activity in 1991 after the 1990 sequence.

Refinement:
Some secondary sources use slightly different dates. Use the strongest
contemporaneous record and retain a note if conflict remains.

April 7, 1991
Event:
Errol Lindsey is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Shows increasing frequency as Dahmer's final months approach.

May 24, 1991
Event:
Anthony "Tony" Hughes is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Two days later, the case intersects directly with police action involving
another victim.

May 27, 1991
Event:
Konerak Sinthasomphone is found outside Dahmer's apartment in a distressed
condition. Police respond and return him to Dahmer's custody; Konerak is
later killed.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department.

Significance:
This becomes the most closely examined institutional failure in the case.
The response was immediately investigated after Dahmer's arrest, and the
episode later became the subject of federal civil litigation concerning
police conduct and departmental policies.

Refinement:
Do not state "no background check" or other procedural details as settled
fact unless the final source establishes them. The final page should focus on
the documented sequence and link to Systemic Failures for analysis.

June 30, 1991
Event:
Matt Turner disappears and is later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Chicago/Milwaukee law-enforcement records.

Significance:
Marks the beginning of the final rapid sequence of killings.

July 6, 1991
Event:
Jeremiah Weinberger is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Chicago missing-person records / Milwaukee investigation.

Significance:
Second known victim in the final sequence.

July 12, 1991
Event:
Oliver Lacy is last seen and later identified as a Dahmer victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person records.

Significance:
Third known victim in the final sequence.

July 16, 1991
Event:
Joseph Bradehoft leaves for a job interview and does not return; he is later
identified as Dahmer's final known victim.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee missing-person investigation.

Significance:
Final known killing before the July 22 arrest.

Refinement:
Use "final known victim" or "final victim in the 17-killing sequence" rather
than relying on a precise death date when source records differ.

July 22, 1991 — 11:30 p.m.
Event:
Tracy Edwards escapes from Apartment 213 with a handcuff attached to one
wrist and flags down Milwaukee police officers Robert Rauth and Rolf Mueller.
They accompany him back to the apartment.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department.

Significance:
The escape directly leads to police entry, discovery of evidence and
Dahmer's arrest.

July 22–23, 1991
Event:
Police searching Apartment 213 find Polaroid photographs and human remains.
Dahmer is arrested.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department / Milwaukee County medical examiner / FBI
assistance.

Significance:
The investigation shifts from an assault incident to a multi-homicide
investigation.

July 23, 1991 onward
Event:
Dahmer begins extensive interviews with detectives and confesses to multiple
killings; the interviews continue over the following days/weeks.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police homicide investigators.

Significance:
His statements become a major source for reconstructing the victim sequence,
locations, and circumstances.

Refinement:
The FBI and contemporary reporting describe roughly 60 hours of interviews
and the confession of 17 killings. Use "more than 60 hours" only when the
source supports that exact characterization.

July 25, 1991
Event:
Dahmer is formally charged with four counts of first-degree intentional
homicide and as a habitual criminal.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court / District Attorney's Office.

Significance:
Marks the formal beginning of the Wisconsin murder prosecution.

July 26, 1991
Event:
Milwaukee Police Chief Philip Arreola announces that three officers involved
in the May 27 Sinthasomphone encounter are suspended pending investigation.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee Police Department / Internal Affairs.

Significance:
Shows the department's immediate institutional response once the earlier
police contact becomes known.

August 6, 1991
Event:
Eight additional homicide counts are filed, bringing the Wisconsin murder
charges to 12.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court.

Significance:
The scale of the prosecution expands as investigators identify additional
victims and evidence.

August 22, 1991
Event:
Three further homicide counts are filed, bringing the Wisconsin total to 15.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court.

Significance:
Completes the set of 15 Wisconsin homicide counts later resolved in the
1992 trial.

September 1991
----------------
September 1991
Event:
Ohio investigators identify human remains from Dahmer's former family
property as belonging to Steven Hicks.

Institutional Contact:
Summit County investigators / coroner / forensic identification.

Significance:
Provides independent forensic confirmation of the earliest known homicide
and extends the case beyond Wisconsin.

Refinement:
The exact identification date should be cited to the forensic record when the
final page is written.

1992
----
January 13, 1992
Event:
Dahmer enters a plea of guilty but insane to the 15 Wisconsin murder charges.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court.

Significance:
Because guilt is admitted, the major contested issue becomes Dahmer's mental
responsibility at the time of the killings.

January 27, 1992 onward
Event:
The sanity phase of the Milwaukee trial begins.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court / psychiatric experts.

Significance:
Competing psychiatric testimony addresses legal insanity rather than whether
Dahmer committed the killings.

February 15, 1992
Event:
Jury finds Dahmer legally sane.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court jury.

Significance:
Rejects the guilty-but-insane defense.

February 17, 1992
Event:
Judge Laurence Gram Jr. sentences Dahmer to 15 consecutive life terms in
Wisconsin.

Institutional Contact:
Milwaukee County Circuit Court.

Significance:
Concludes the Wisconsin criminal case.

Refinement:
Do not describe the jury as "convicting" him after trial. Dahmer had already
pleaded guilty; the jury determined his legal sanity.

May 1, 1992
Event:
Dahmer pleads guilty in Ohio to the 1978 murder of Steven Hicks and receives
a life sentence consecutive to his Wisconsin terms.

Institutional Contact:
Summit County Court of Common Pleas, Ohio.

Significance:
Produces a 16th murder conviction and closes the second state's criminal
case.

Refinement:
Do not use the draft's "exceeds 940 years" wording. Published accounts give
different arithmetic for the Wisconsin sentence. The clearest stable fact for
this project's timeline is 15 consecutive life terms in Wisconsin plus one
additional life sentence in Ohio.

1992 — civil/institutional aftermath
-------------------------------------
1992–1995
Event:
Federal civil litigation examines the May 27, 1991 police encounter and
broader allegations concerning police conduct and policies involving
minorities and gay men.

Institutional Contact:
U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Wisconsin / City of Milwaukee
/ Milwaukee Police Department.

Significance:
Moves the case beyond criminal prosecution into constitutional, civil-rights
and institutional accountability questions.

Refinement:
Do not summarize the litigation as a simple judicial finding that "the police
caused the murders." The cases involved different allegations, procedural
postures, and legal standards.

November 1992
Event:
The Oxford Apartments at 924 N. 25th Street are demolished after being
purchased by the Campus Circle Project, a nonprofit redevelopment organization
affiliated with Marquette University.

Institutional Contact:
Campus Circle Project / neighborhood redevelopment organizations.

Significance:
The building is removed after becoming a focus of public attention and
unwanted tourism; plans were announced to landscape the site with grass and
flowers.

Refinement:
The City of Milwaukee Redevelopment Authorities should NOT be listed as the
primary institution for the purchase/demolition. UPI reported Campus Circle
Project and Career Youth Development as the organizations behind the effort.

1994
----
November 28, 1994
Event:
Dahmer is beaten to death by fellow inmate Christopher Scarver during a
cleaning detail at Columbia Correctional Institution in Portage, Wisconsin.
Inmate Jesse Anderson is also fatally attacked.

Institutional Contact:
Wisconsin Department of Corrections / Columbia Correctional Institution.

Significance:
Ends Dahmer's life in custody and becomes a separate institutional episode
about prison management and inmate supervision.

Refinement:
Do not state "security failures allowed unsupervised contact" as established
fact without a specific finding. Contemporary/later reporting does not support
turning that claim into a settled conclusion. The timeline should document
the attack; a later analysis section can examine supervision questions and
official findings.

INTERACTIVE TIMELINE PLAN
-------------------------
The webapp should make the timeline useful as a research tool rather than a
static table.

Approved ideas:

1. ERA NAVIGATION
   - Clickable year/era markers.
   - Selecting an era scrolls to that period.

2. FILTERS
   Optional filters:
   - Crime / victim event
   - Police / law enforcement
   - Court / legal
   - Probation / supervision
   - Housing / community
   - Investigation / forensic
   - Aftermath / institutional review

3. EXPANDABLE EVENTS
   - Default row is compact.
   - Clicking expands the event for a short explanation.
   - Expanded view can show source notes and internal links.

4. CROSS-LINKING
   Examples:
   - Konerak event -> /topics/systemic-failures
   - Probation events -> /topics/systemic-failures
   - Hicks -> /victims#steven-hicks
   - Trial events -> /case/trial
   - Oxford demolition -> /impact/community-civic-impact

5. MOBILE
   - Prefer a vertical timeline.
   - Each event becomes a readable block.
   - Avoid making the user horizontally scroll an entire four-column desktop
     table.
   - The four-field information remains accessible through stacked labels.
