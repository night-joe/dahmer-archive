CORE PURPOSE
------------
This page should answer:
- What motivations did Dahmer himself describe?
- What explanations did the 1992 experts offer?
- What later academic interpretations have been proposed?
- Where do those explanations overlap?
- Where do they conflict?
- Which claims are supported by the record, and which remain hypotheses?

This page follows the Psychological Profile & Evaluation page.

Important division:
PSYCHOLOGICAL PROFILE & EVALUATION
    = What clinicians and experts documented/evaluated.

THEORIES & MOTIVATIONS
    = How those observations have been interpreted as explanations of the
      offending pattern.

No single explanation should be presented as "the" motive.

EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
-------------------
The final article should use three levels of certainty:

1. DOCUMENTED
   Dahmer said/claimed X.
   A court record documents X.
   An expert testified X.

2. INTERPRETATION
   Expert/researcher Y interpreted the evidence as X.

3. HYPOTHESIS
   Later scholarship proposes X, but evidence remains limited.

Always identify which level is being used.

OPENING
-------
Suggested opening:

    "No single theory explains Dahmer's crimes. The surviving record contains
    several overlapping explanations: a desire for control and permanent
    possession, sexual and paraphilic interests, fear of abandonment or
    rejection, alcohol use, and increasingly compulsive behavior. These ideas
    come from different sources and serve different purposes. Some describe
    Dahmer's own explanations, some come from the psychiatrists who evaluated
    him, and others are later academic interpretations."

This should replace the more absolute language "what the record shows" with a
clearer distinction between evidence and interpretation.

THEORY 1 — POSSESSION, CONTROL AND COMPANIONSHIP
------------------------------------------------
The draft's "Possession over pain" concept should be retained but renamed:

    POSSESSION, CONTROL AND COMPANIONSHIP

Core evidence:
- Dahmer repeatedly described a desire to keep victims with him and prevent
  them from leaving.
- At the 1992 trial, defense attorney Gerald Boyle framed the killings around
  Dahmer's desire for permanent, completely compliant companions.
- Park Dietz gave a contrasting but related account: he testified that Dahmer
  preferred a living, consenting partner when that partner would stay, and that
  the killings were a means to obtain a more permanent form of control or
  possession.
- George Palermo emphasized fear of rejection, hostility, lust and power, but
  his interpretation differed from the defense's emphasis on necrophilia.

IMPORTANT REFINEMENT
--------------------
Do NOT state:
    "Death itself wasn't the goal."

Better:
    "Several accounts presented at trial interpreted killing as instrumental
    to Dahmer's larger desire for possession, sexual access, or permanent
    companionship. That interpretation was not identical across the experts."

This preserves the central idea without pretending all evidence points to one
motivation.

FREEZE-DRYING IDEA
------------------
Dahmer told Park Dietz that he had considered freeze-drying a body as a way of
preserving a preferred partner for continued sexual access, but abandoned the
idea because of the equipment cost.

Source:
Associated Press / Washington Post, February 12, 1992.

Use:
- as evidence of a desire for permanence/control
- as an example of alternative solutions he considered

Do NOT write:
    "This proves that Dahmer did not want to kill."

It supports an interpretation of his stated preferences; it does not erase the
fact that he repeatedly killed.

THE "ZOMBIE" IDEA
-----------------
The trial record includes extensive testimony about Dahmer's attempts to
create a permanently submissive or "zombie-like" partner.

Park Dietz and Frederick Fosdal both discussed the idea in testimony, though
they interpreted its significance differently.

Use:
    "The experiments were presented at trial as attempts to solve Dahmer's
    desire for a permanently compliant partner without repeatedly replacing
    victims."

The detailed mechanics belong in:
    Case -> The Offense Series
and
    Psychological Profile & Evaluation

This page should focus on what the experiments meant within competing theories
of motivation.

THEORY 2 — BEHAVIORAL ADDICTION / COMPULSION
--------------------------------------------
The 2022 study by Adam Lankford and Jenna K. Hayes is a valuable later academic
interpretation.

Full citation:
Lankford, A., & Hayes, J. K. (2022). "Could Serial Killing Actually Be Addictive?
A Close Examination of Compulsion and Escalation in the Jeffrey Dahmer Case."
Sexual Health & Compulsivity, 29(3-4), 198–224.
DOI: 10.1080/26929953.2022.2126416

What the study did:
- assembled a longitudinal chronology of Dahmer's life and offending
- examined the frequency and escalation of the crimes
- compared known behavior/statements with the DSM-5's eleven Substance Use
  Disorder criteria as an analogy
- also considered the ICD criteria for compulsive sexual behavior disorder
  and/or paraphilic disorder
- argued that Dahmer's behavior was consistent with a strong behavioral
  addiction

CRITICAL LIMITATION
-------------------
The authors did NOT diagnose Dahmer with a DSM-5 Substance Use Disorder caused
by serial killing.

Instead, they deliberately used the substance-use criteria as an analytical
comparison because the DSM-5 did not provide a validated diagnostic category
for "serial killing addiction."

The final website must make this distinction explicit.

Better wording:
    "Lankford and Hayes proposed that Dahmer's behavior was consistent with a
    behavioral-addiction model. To test the analogy, they compared his known
    behavior and statements with the DSM-5 criteria for Substance Use Disorder,
    while acknowledging that those criteria were designed for substances, not
    serial killing."

The study is a single case analysis and should be identified as such.

DAHMER'S OWN 1993 ACCOUNT
-------------------------
In a 1993 Inside Edition interview conducted in prison, Dahmer described
obsessive thoughts and desires for control and said that they had intensified
over time. He also described his behavior as becoming "almost addictive."

Use this material as:
    post-conviction self-report

Do not treat it as an objective clinical diagnosis.

The FBI FOIA file confirms that the FBI obtained an uncut/un-edited copy of the
Inside Edition interview for Behavioral Science Unit research and training.

Source:
FBI Vault, Jeffrey Lionel Dahmer Part 03, June 23, 1993.
Inside Edition interview, January 11–12, 1993; aired in February 1993.

Important:
Dahmer's own retrospective explanations are evidence about what he said, not
proof that his explanation was complete or psychologically accurate.

THEORY 3 — ALCOHOL AS A TOOL / DISINHIBITOR
--------------------------------------------
This is one of the clearest trial disagreements.

Park Dietz testified for the prosecution that Dahmer used alcohol deliberately
to overcome his inhibition against killing. Dietz emphasized that Dahmer did
not simply become violent because he was drunk; rather, he drank to reduce his
revulsion or hesitation before committing the killings.

Dietz also cited examples in which Dahmer did not kill:
- when circumstances created a serious risk of being caught
- when a victim did not fit his fantasy
- when a victim could remain voluntarily

This was presented as evidence that Dahmer retained behavioral control.

IMPORTANT:
Do not turn Dietz's testimony into:
    "Alcohol had no effect on him."

The more accurate distinction is:
    "Dietz interpreted Dahmer's drinking as instrumental rather than as an
    uncontrollable trigger for violence."

Source:
UPI, February 12, 1992;
Washington Post, February 13–14, 1992.

CONTRAST WITH OTHER EVIDENCE
----------------------------
The defense experts emphasized alcohol dependence alongside severe
paraphilic/personality pathology and argued that the combination contributed
to impaired control.

Therefore:
    Alcohol was both a clinically documented problem and, in the prosecution
    account, a deliberate behavioral tool.

The two ideas are not mutually exclusive.

THEORY 4 — SEXUAL GRATIFICATION / PARAPHILIA
--------------------------------------------
The psychological trial record consistently recognized a sexual component to
the crimes, but experts disagreed on how that component should be conceptualized.

Relevant documented themes:
- sexual attraction to men
- paraphilic interests
- necrophilic interests
- sexual gratification associated with bodies and control
- fantasy involving domination/control

Defense experts such as Frederick Berlin and Judith Becker placed necrophilia
and related sexual pathology near the center of the offending pattern.

Park Dietz and George Palermo offered different interpretations, emphasizing
control, choice, personality disturbance, or the relationship between sexual
desire and killing.

Use:
    "Sexual gratification was a recurring element in the expert explanations,
    but the experts disagreed about whether necrophilia itself was the central
    disorder and how much it impaired behavior."

Important:
- Do not equate homosexuality with pathology.
- Do not use "sexual deviance" as the site's own terminology without quoting
  or attributing the historical source.
- Do not imply that paraphilia generally causes violence.

THEORY 5 — FEAR OF REJECTION / ABANDONMENT
------------------------------------------
George Palermo's testimony emphasized:
- fear of rejection
- desire for control
- hostility
- frustration
- the wish to eliminate the source of attraction

This interpretation is important because it differs from the defense's
"permanent companion" formulation.

Later research can examine whether the behavioral record supports a meaningful
connection between rejection, abandonment, control and violence.

However, the site should not convert Palermo's interpretation into an
established cause.

Use:
    "Palermo interpreted the crimes partly through fear of rejection and the
    pursuit of control. This was his clinical formulation, not a consensus
    explanation."

Source:
Associated Press / Washington Post, February 6, 1992.

THEORY 6 — HOSTILITY / POWER / SADISM
-------------------------------------
The 1994 paper "Destructive Hostility: The Jeffrey Dahmer Case: A Psychiatric
and Forensic Study of a Serial Killer" by Jeffrey Jentzen, George Palermo and
colleagues proposed that unconscious hostility had been channeled into
destructive and sadistic behavior.

IMPORTANT SOURCE STATUS
-----------------------
The Marquette University e-publication record identifies the 1994 article and
its authors but also states that the article was withdrawn.

Therefore:
- it may be used as a historical example of a forensic interpretation
- it must NOT be presented as an uncontested peer-reviewed consensus
- cite its withdrawn status in the Sources notes
- do not use it as the primary basis for any final conclusion

The page should also distinguish "sexual sadism" as a historical expert
description from a modern diagnosis.

THEORY 7 — BEHAVIORAL ESCALATION
--------------------------------
A useful synthesis is to examine whether the pattern itself changed over time:

1978
    First known killing.

1987–1989
    Repeated killings after a long gap, followed by increasingly stable
    victim-approach and disposal routines.

1990–1991
    Greater frequency, more extensive preservation/retention, experimentation,
    and increasing logistical strain.

This is compatible with several competing explanations:
- behavioral addiction
- increasing compulsivity
- reinforcement of sexual fantasy
- tolerance/escalation models
- greater confidence and opportunity
- changing circumstances

The timeline pattern alone cannot decide among them.

    /timeline
    /case/offense-series

WHAT THE RECORD SUPPORTS
------------------------
The strongest synthesis is:

1. Dahmer repeatedly described control and possession as important to his
   behavior.
2. Sexual gratification and paraphilic interests were central themes in the
   trial's psychiatric evidence.
3. Alcohol dependence was documented, but experts disagreed about whether
   alcohol primarily impaired control or was deliberately used to reduce
   inhibition.
4. Defense and prosecution experts disagreed about how severe the compulsive
   component was.
5. Later scholarship has proposed a behavioral-addiction model, but that is a
   research hypothesis derived from a single case, not a recognized diagnosis
   of "serial killing addiction."
6. No single motivation has been established as the complete explanation.

WHAT THE RECORD DOES NOT SUPPORT
--------------------------------
Do NOT state as fact that:
- childhood trauma caused the murders
- homosexuality caused the murders
- alcoholism caused the murders
- necrophilia automatically caused the murders
- Dahmer was simply "a psychopath"
- Dahmer killed only because he wanted companionship
- Dahmer killed only for sexual gratification
- Dahmer was unable to control himself
- planning proves he had no mental disorder
- one psychiatrist's explanation is the definitive answer

These claims oversimplify a record in which experts themselves disagreed.
