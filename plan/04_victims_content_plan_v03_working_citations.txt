CORE EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE
------------------------
The Victims section focuses on the people who were killed: who they were, what is
publicly known about their lives, and what can responsibly be reconstructed from
the historical record.

Where the record is thin, leave it thin rather than filling gaps with speculation.

The page does not:
- rank victims
- number them as a hierarchy of importance
- sensationalize their deaths
- use later media controversies as substitutes for biographies

1. STEVEN HICKS, 18
-------------------
Steven Mark Hicks had recently graduated from high school in Coventry Township, Ohio [1]. His father,
Richard Hicks, described him as a deeply caring young man; in an Associated Press interview, he recalled
that Steven had become inconsolably distraught after shooting a rabbit during a hunting trip [1]. Hicks
was hitchhiking to a rock concert at Chippewa Lake Park when he was picked up by Dahmer on June 18, 1978 [1, 23].
He was last seen alive on that date [1, 2, Part 01 pp. 24–25].

Following Dahmer's arrest and confession in July 1991, investigators searched the grounds of Dahmer's former
home in Bath Township, Ohio, recovering human bone fragments [5]. Forensic identification was formally
confirmed in September 1991 by the Summit County coroner's office, which matched a recovered molar, molar
fragment, and cervical vertebra against Hicks's dental and medical X-ray records [23]. Dahmer pleaded guilty
to Hicks's murder in Ohio in May 1992, receiving his sixteenth life term [6].

Evidence Notes:
- Last Known Alive: June 18, 1978 [1, 2, Part 01 pp. 24–25, 23].
- Date of Birth: FBI records transcribe Hicks's DOB as June 27, 1959 [2, Part 01 pp. 24–25] (with a June 22,
  1959 variant reported elsewhere in FBI case notes); both dates confirm he was 18 years old on June 18, 1978.
- Missing Report: June 24, 1978 [2].
- Forensic Identification: September 1991 via dental and skeletal radiographs [23].


2. STEVEN TUOMI, 28*
--------------------
Steven W. Tuomi grew up in Ontonagon in Michigan's Upper Peninsula and moved to Milwaukee, where he
worked as a short-order cook at a local restaurant [1]. Former Ontonagon high school classmates remembered
him as quiet, thoughtful, and artistic; one recalled a stained-glass lamp that Tuomi had painstakingly
crafted in art class [1]. Tuomi was last seen and last heard from by family and acquaintances in Milwaukee
on September 15, 1987 [1].

In post-arrest FBI interviews, Dahmer confessed that Steven Tuomi was his second murder victim, stating
that the killing occurred in a room at the Ambassador Hotel in Milwaukee "somewhere around 1986 or 1987" [2, Part 03 p. 10].
Because Dahmer claimed to have had no memory of the killing after heavy drinking, and because no physical
remains were ever recovered, Milwaukee County prosecutors determined they lacked independent physical evidence
to establish probable cause [4, 19, 21, 22]. District Attorney E. Michael McCann confirmed that Tuomi was the
sole Milwaukee victim for whom Dahmer was not charged [21, 22].

Evidence Notes:
- Last Known Alive / Last Heard From: September 15, 1987 [1].
- Death Chronology: Dahmer's confession stated the Ambassador Hotel incident occurred "somewhere around
  1986 or 1987" [2, Part 03 p. 10]. The record does not support a precise date of death.
- Age Reference Ambiguity: Displayed as `28*`. Retrospective reporting [1] and contemporary 1991 reports [19]
  cite age 28. Milwaukee police investigative notes record that detectives "believed" his DOB was December 19,
  1962 [2, Part 01 p. 43], which would imply age 24 on September 15, 1987. The divergence appears to stem from
  different reference points (age at 1991 case reporting vs. age at 1987 disappearance) alongside an uncertain
  death date.


3. JAMES "JAMIE" DOXTATOR, 14
-----------------------------
Jamie Doxtator was the oldest of four children and came from Stockbridge and Oneida ancestry [1].
Remembered as a boy who was tall for his age—standing nearly six feet tall at age fourteen—he enjoyed
playing pool and riding his bicycle around his neighborhood [1]. His mother, Debbie Vega, later spoke
publicly about his life and the enduring grief of his loss [1].

Doxtator disappeared in January 1988 [20], with January 16, 1988 recorded in the principal retrospective
as the date he was last seen [1]. Dahmer later confessed to encountering Doxtator outside a Milwaukee bar
and taking him to his grandmother's West Allis residence [1, 2, 20].

Evidence Notes:
- Ancestry Attribution: Stockbridge and Oneida heritage is attributed to family interviews [1]. An early
  FBI investigative summary transcribed detectives' preliminary description as a "Hispanic male" with
  DOB March 1, 1973 [2, Part 01 p. 43]; this investigative label is preserved as a document attribute and
  does not overwrite family-attested Native American ancestry.
- Name Spelling: The FBI transcript notes the spelling "Doxator" [2, Part 01 p. 43]; the project standard
  maintains the family and community spelling "Doxtator."
- Date Gap: Contemporary wire reporting anchors his disappearance to January 1988 [20]; primary official
  corroboration for the specific day January 16 remains an open gap held by the retrospective account [1].


4. RICHARD GUERRERO, 25*
-------------------------
Richard Guerrero grew up in Milwaukee in a close-knit family of Mexican descent [1]. His sister, Janie
Hagen, remembered him as deeply devoted to his family, recalling that he routinely helped their mother
with household errands and frequently babysat her young daughter [1]. Guerrero disappeared in late March
1988 and was never seen alive again by his family [1, 17, 19]. In their desperate search for answers, his
family hired a private investigator who subsequently defrauded them of their savings [1].

Dahmer later confessed to killing Guerrero at his grandmother's house in West Allis in March 1988 [1, 19].

Evidence Notes:
- Disappearance Date: March 1988 [1, 17, 19]; FBI missing-person entry records a report date of March 29, 1988 [2].
- Three-Source Age Discrepancy: Displayed as `25*`.
  1. Milwaukee Journal Sentinel retrospective reports age 25 [1].
  2. Contemporary UPI wire reports explicitly state he was 23 when reported missing in March 1988 [17, 19].
  3. FBI Vault records document his DOB as December 12, 1965 [2], which calculates to age 22 on March 29, 1988.
  All three records are preserved transparently.


5. ANTHONY SEARS, 24
--------------------
Anthony Sears was a manager at a Baker's Square restaurant in Milwaukee who had recently earned a promotion
in recognition of his reliability and skill [1]. Sears had strong ambitions to become a professional model,
carefully saving his earnings with the goal of moving to Atlanta or Los Angeles [1]. His mother recalled
that he had spoken with excitement about his future plans, including marrying his girlfriend once he had
saved sufficient funds [1].

Sears was last seen alive leaving a Milwaukee club on the night of March 25, 1989 [1]. Dahmer confessed to
luring Sears to his grandmother's residence in West Allis [1, 3, 20].

Evidence Notes:
- Date of Birth: January 28, 1965, confirmed by FBI identification records [2, Part 01 p. 24], confirming
  he was 24 years old at the time of his death [1, 19, 20].
- Chronology Conflict: Sears was last seen on March 25, 1989 [1]. The Wisconsin criminal complaint alleges
  the homicide occurred on or about March 26, 1989 [3]. Contemporary wire reporting placed the encounter on
  the night of March 26 and mentioned he planned to return home for Easter on March 27 [20]; however, Easter
  Sunday in 1989 fell on March 26, demonstrating an uncorrected journalistic discrepancy within the wire report.


6. RAYMOND "RICKY BEEKS" SMITH, 33*
-------------------------------------
Raymond Lamont Smith, known affectionately to his family and friends as Ricky Beeks, had been living with
his half-sister in Milwaukee following his release from prison as he worked to re-establish his life [1].
He was the father of a ten-year-old daughter who lived in Rockford, Illinois [1]. Smith disappeared on
May 29, 1990, after walking to a neighborhood convenience store [1].

Dahmer later admitted meeting Smith at the Milwaukee Grand Avenue Mall and bringing him to his newly leased
apartment at the Oxford Apartments (Apartment 213), where he murdered him [1, 3, 19].

Evidence Notes:
- Identity & Aliases: FBI records record his name as Raymond Lamonte Smith, a.k.a. Ricky Lee Beeks / Cash T [2, Part 01 p. 25].
- Missing Report Status: FBI investigative summaries indicate that Smith had not been formally reported missing
  to the Milwaukee Police Department by relatives [2, Part 06].
- Age Discrepancy & Reference Point: Displayed as `33*`. Retrospective reporting [1] and contemporary July/August
  1991 press coverage [16, 19] list his age as 33. FBI files document his DOB as August 10, 1957 [2, Part 01 p. 25],
  which establishes that he was 32 years old on the May 29, 1990 disappearance date, and would have turned 33 in
  August 1990 prior to the case discovery.


7. EDWARD "EDDIE" SMITH, 28*
-----------------------------
Edward Smith was an aspiring professional model remembered by friends for his vibrant personality and
distinctive sense of style, often wearing a stylish turban-like head wrap that led friends to give him the
playful nickname "the Sheikh" [1]. He was raised in a close-knit Christian family in Milwaukee [1, 9].
Smith was last seen by his sister Carolyn at her home on June 14, 1990 [17], and was formally reported missing
to police in late June [2, 17]. Not long after his disappearance, his sister received an anonymous phone call
stating simply that Eddie was dead [1, 17].

At Dahmer's sentencing hearing in February 1992, Carolyn Smith delivered an emotional statement on behalf
of the family, speaking directly of the enduring devastation his loss caused their household [9].

Evidence Notes:
- Disappearance Dates: Last seen by family on June 14, 1990 [17]; FBI records indicate a missing-person report
  date of June 23, 1990 [2].
- Phone Call Source: The sister's contemporaneous account of the anonymous phone call is documented in contemporary
  wire reporting [17] and later retrospective profiles [1].
- Age Discrepancy & Reference Point: Displayed as `28*`. Press reports [17] and the MJS retrospective [1] list his
  age as 28. FBI records record his DOB as August 2, 1962 [2], which implies he was 27 on June 14, 1990, and turned
  28 in August 1990 before the 1991 investigation.


8. ERNEST MILLER, 24*
---------------------
Ernest Miller was a gifted young artist who had graduated from the Milwaukee High School of the Arts and
was preparing to begin classes at an arts institute in Chicago, where he hoped to train for a career as a
professional dancer [1]. His aunt, Vivian Miller, recalled that he had sung in church choirs and danced
at community showcases from early childhood [1]. While visiting relatives in Milwaukee over the Labor Day
weekend, Miller disappeared [1, 17].

Contemporary reports indicate he was last seen by friends on September 2, 1990 [1, 17]. Sworn witness statements
and police incident records show that Miller left his family's residence at approximately 1:30 A.M. on
September 3, 1990, and relatives filed an official missing-person report on September 4 [2, 3]. The criminal
complaint alleges Dahmer encountered Miller outside the 219 Club and killed him on or about September 3, 1990 [3, 19].

Evidence Notes:
- Disappearance & Complaint Chronology: September 2 (last seen by acquaintances) [1, 17]; September 3 at 1:30 A.M.
  (departure from family residence) [2, 3]; September 3 (alleged offense date) [3, 19]; September 4 (formal missing
  report) [2].
- Age Discrepancy & Reference Point: Displayed as `24*`. Press reporting in 1991 [17, 19] and the MJS retrospective [1]
  record his age as 24. FBI files list his DOB as May 5, 1967 [2], confirming he was 23 years old in September 1990,
  and turned 24 in May 1991 prior to Dahmer's arrest.


9. DAVID THOMAS, 23
-------------------
David Thomas was the father of a two-year-old daughter, Courtia Beanland [1]. His former partner, Chandra
Beanland, remembered him as an affectionate, fun-loving young man who was devoted to his child [1]. While
Thomas occasionally spent periods away from home, Beanland became alarmed when he failed to return in
late September 1990 and formally filed a missing-person report [1, 17]. He was last seen alive on
September 24, 1990 [1].

The formal criminal complaint alleges that Dahmer encountered Thomas near 24th and State Streets and brought
him to Apartment 213, killing him on or about September 24, 1990 [3, 19]. Thomas was subsequently identified
through photographs recovered from Dahmer's apartment rather than skeletal remains [2, 19].

Evidence Notes:
- Age & Date Corroboration: DOB and age 23 are consistent across the retrospective [1], contemporary reporting [17, 19],
  and formal complaint records [3].
- Last Seen Date: September 24, 1990 [1, 17].
- Editorial Cleanliness: Unsupported and vague colloquial labels from early drafts (e.g., "passionate hustler")
  have been permanently excised per the core editorial principle.


10. CURTIS STRAUGHTER, 17–18*
-----------------------------
Curtis Straughter joined Gay Youth Milwaukee at age fifteen, seeking community and peer support [1]. He
worked as a resident nursing assistant at a local nursing home and was actively studying to earn his high
school equivalency diploma, with ambitions to enroll in modeling school [1]. Straughter lived with his
grandmother, who helped raise him [1, 3].

Straughter disappeared in early 1991. The MJS retrospective records that he was last seen on March 7,
1991 [1]. However, sworn statements in the Wisconsin criminal complaint and FBI investigative files record
that his grandmother last saw him on February 18, 1991, and the state alleged the homicide occurred on or
about February 18 [2, Part 01 p. 25, 3]. FBI files further note that a missing-person report was not formally
processed until Milwaukee detectives interviewed his grandmother on July 24, 1991 [2, Part 06]. At Dahmer's
sentencing, his mother, Catherine Straughter, gave a poignant statement describing the emptiness left by his
death [9].

Evidence Notes:
- Documented Discrepancy (Age & Last Seen): Displayed as `17–18*`.
  1. DOB April 16, 1973 is confirmed by FBI identification records [2, Part 01 p. 25] and the criminal complaint [3].
     This conclusively proves he was 17 years old during both the February 18 and March 7 timelines.
  2. Contemporary news stories on July 26, 1991 reported his age as 18 [16, 19], which reflects his age at
     identification following his eighteenth birthday in April 1991.
  3. Last Seen Date Conflict: February 18, 1991 (grandmother's sworn statement and complaint) [2, 3] vs.
     March 7, 1991 (MJS retrospective) [1]. Both dates remain documented without forced reconciliation.


11. ERROL LINDSEY, 19
---------------------
Errol Lindsey was the youngest of six children in a close Milwaukee family [1]. Remembered by friends as
exceptionally dependable and gentle, Lindsey was employed making decorative plaster figures [1]. His former
eighth-grade art teacher treasured a watercolor painting he had made, remembering him as a quiet and gifted
student [1]. His mother, Mildred Lindsey, recalled that Errol was a devoted son who routinely went straight to
work and came home to be with his family [1].

On April 7, 1991, Lindsey left his family's home to run an errand to have a key made and never returned [1].
His family filed a missing-person report with police on April 11, 1991 [2, Part 06]. Dahmer confessed to
luring Lindsey to Apartment 213 [1, 3, 15]. Lindsey's sister, Rita Isbell, delivered an unforgettable and
visceral victim impact statement during Dahmer's sentencing hearing on February 17, 1992 [9].

Evidence Notes:
- Age & DOB: FBI Vault records confirm DOB March 3, 1972 [2, Part 01 p. 25], confirming he was 19 years old
  when killed in April 1991 [1, 16, 19].
- Dates: Last seen April 7, 1991 [1]; missing-person report filed April 11, 1991 [2, Part 06]; forensic
  identification announced July 26, 1991 [16].
- Editorial Boundary: Later media controversies regarding dramatizations of Rita Isbell's courtroom statement
  are intentionally relocated to Media & Cultural Portrayal [11, 12, 13], keeping Lindsey's biographical
  profile centered on his life and character.


12. ANTHONY "TONY" HUGHES, 31
-----------------------------
Anthony Hughes lived in Madison, Wisconsin, and was visiting family and friends in Milwaukee in late May
1991 [1]. Deaf since infancy following a severe bout of pneumonia, Hughes communicated expressively and
effectively through lip-reading, American Sign Language, and handwritten notes carried on notepads [1, 2, Part 04, 15].
Family and acquaintances remembered him as an outgoing, charismatic, and popular young man who had a large
circle of friends across Madison and Milwaukee [1]. His mother, Shirley Hughes, was a steadfast presence
throughout the subsequent judicial proceedings [1, 9].

Hughes was last seen alive on the night of May 24, 1991, dancing with friends at the 219 Club in Milwaukee [1, 15].
His mother reported him missing to the Madison Police Department on May 31, 1991, after he uncharacteristically
failed to return home or contact his family [2, Part 06]. Dahmer confessed to meeting Hughes at the club,
communicating via written notes, and persuading him to visit Apartment 213 [1, 15]. Formal identification
was confirmed via fingerprint and dental records on July 25–26, 1991 [15, 16].

Evidence Notes:
- Date of Birth & Age: FBI records confirm DOB August 26, 1959 [2, Part 01 p. 24], confirming age 31 [1, 15, 16, 19].
- Communication Fact-Check: Outdated historical descriptors (such as "deaf-mute") found in contemporary wire
  clippings [16] are avoided; the profile utilizes respectful, factually accurate descriptions of his
  communication methods [1, 15].
- Media Boundary: In accordance with core editorial standards, commercial film adaptations are excluded to keep
  the profile respectful and life-centered.


13. KONERAK SINTHASOMPHONE, 14
------------------------------
Konerak Sinthasomphone was a member of a large Laotian immigrant family that had fled Laos following the 1975
communist takeover, lived in the Nong Khai refugee resettlement camp in Thailand, and arrived in Milwaukee in
1980 [1, 7]. His father had been a farmer in Laos [1]. Konerak was about three years old when the family arrived
in the United States; by 1991, he was a freshman at Pulaski High School, spoke Lao and English, and was an avid
soccer player who frequently joined neighborhood matches at Mitchell Park [1].

On May 27, 1991, Konerak was drugged by Dahmer but managed to escape naked and disoriented into the street outside
the Oxford Apartments [1, 8, 15]. Two Black women, Sandra Smith and Nicole Childress, observed his distress,
intervened to protect him, and placed emergency 911 calls [8, 10]. Milwaukee police officers Joseph Gabrish and
John Balcerzak responded, but accepted Dahmer's assertion that the boy was his adult partner, threatening the
intervening bystanders with arrest and returning Konerak into Dahmer's apartment [8, 10, 15]. Dahmer murdered him
shortly after police departed [1, 15].

WHY HIS CASE MATTERS
Konerak was the younger brother of Somsack Sinthasomphone, who had survived a sexual assault by Dahmer in
1988 for which Dahmer was on probation at the time of Konerak's murder [1, 15]. The police handover of a
vulnerable 14-year-old child despite clear witness warnings sparked intense public outrage, civil rights
protests, and demands for police accountability across Milwaukee's Black, Southeast Asian, and LGBTQ+
communities (examined in depth in the Systemic Failures section) [8, 10].

Evidence Notes:
- Date of Birth & Age: FBI records confirm DOB December 2, 1976 [2, Part 01 p. 24], confirming age 14 [1, 19].
- Family Migration Dates: 1980 arrival from Thailand confirmed by contemporary family reporting [7]. The specific
  month of November 1980 originates from an internal Milwaukee Sentinel press clipping referenced in FBI Part 04
  (p. 141) [2], which remains an acknowledged sub-document citation gap.


14. MATT TURNER, 20
-------------------
Matt Turner was a native of Flint, Michigan, who had moved to Chicago and was residing in a Chicago halfway
house as he sought to build an independent life [1]. Turner had strong aspirations to break into commercial
modeling [1]. On June 30, 1991, after attending Chicago's annual Gay Pride parade, Turner met Dahmer at a bus
terminal and accepted an offer to travel to Milwaukee [1, 15]. Turner occasionally utilized the alias
Donald Montrell [1, 2, Part 01 p. 25; Part 06 p. 9].

Turner was last seen alive on June 30, 1991 [1, 18]. The criminal complaint alleges that Dahmer killed Turner
at Apartment 213 on or about June 30, 1991 [3, 15]. Following Dahmer's arrest, Turner's identity was confirmed
through fingerprint comparison by the Chicago Police Department AFIS system [2, Part 01 p. 25; Part 06 p. 9].

Evidence Notes:
- Identity & Aliases: FBI records confirm the alias Donald Montrell and Chicago AFIS fingerprint identification [2, Part 01 p. 25; Part 06 p. 9].
- Date of Birth: FBI Part 06, PDF p. 9 confirms DOB July 3, 1970 [2, Part 06 p. 9]. (The unverified "Part 01 p. 170"
  variant claiming July 30, 1970 has been retired).
- Cross-Article Age Clarification: Turner was 20 years old on June 30, 1991. Early UPI wire articles published
  on July 25–26, 1991 listed his age as 21 [15, 16], reflecting his age following his July 3 birthday, while the
  August 6 UPI report listed him as 20 [19]. This variation reflects different reporting reference points rather
  than a genuine dispute regarding his July 3, 1970 birth date.


15. JEREMIAH WEINBERGER, 23
----------------------------
Jeremiah Weinberger was a native of Puerto Rico who had relocated to Chicago, where he lived with a roommate
and worked as a customer service representative at a neighborhood video store [1]. His roommate remembered
him as a gentle, artistic, and meticulous young man who took immense pride in his personal style and appearance [1].
While visiting a Chicago bar over the July 4 holiday weekend, Weinberger encountered Dahmer and agreed to
accompany him to Milwaukee by Greyhound bus [1, 15].

Weinberger was last seen alive in Chicago on July 6, 1991 [1, 18], and his roommate filed a missing-person
report with the Chicago Police Department on that date [2, Part 06 p. 9]. The Wisconsin criminal complaint alleges
that Dahmer murdered Weinberger in Apartment 213 on or about July 7, 1991 [3, 15].

Evidence Notes:
- Age & Date of Birth Variants: Age 23 is consistent across all retrospective and wire sources [1, 15, 18, 19].
  Internal FBI files contain multiple recorded birth date variants: Part 01, p. 25 lists September 25, 1967;
  Part 06, p. 9 lists September 29, 1967; and earlier FBI teletype transcriptions record September 28, 1967 [2].
  All three variants confirm he was 23 years old in July 1991.
- Dates: Last seen and reported missing July 6, 1991 [1, 2, Part 06 p. 9, 18]; alleged offense date on or about
  July 7, 1991 [3, 15].


16. OLIVER LACY, 23*
---------------------
Oliver Lacy grew up in Oak Park, Illinois, where he was a standout track athlete at Oak Park and River Forest
High School [1]. Remembered by his mother, Catherine Lacy, as an outspoken, energetic, and loving young man,
Lacy was the father of a two-year-old son, Emmanuel, and was engaged to be married [1]. Following the death
of his father, Lacy wore his late father's cross necklace as a constant memorial, moving to Milwaukee in the
spring of 1991 to look for employment [1].

Lacy was last seen alive leaving his apartment on July 12, 1991 [1, 18]. When he failed to return or contact
his family, his fiancée filed an official missing-person report on July 15, 1991 [2, Part 06 p. 9]. The criminal
complaint alleges Dahmer encountered Lacy on July 15 and brought him to Apartment 213 [3, 15]. Following Dahmer's
arrest, Lacy's identity was established via fingerprint and dental comparison, making him the first victim
officially identified by investigators [1, 4, 18].

Evidence Notes:
- Disappearance and Complaint Chronology: Last seen July 12, 1991 [1, 18]; missing-person report filed July 15,
  1991 [2, Part 06 p. 9]; complaint encounter allegation on or about July 15, 1991 [3, 15]; identification announced
  July 24, 1991 [4].
- Internal FBI DOB Conflict: Displayed as `23*`. Retrospective reporting [1] and contemporary press reports [18, 19]
  cite age 23. Verified FBI files contain an internal discrepancy: FBI Part 01, PDF p. 24 records DOB June 23, 1968
  (confirming age 23 on July 12, 1991) [2, Part 01 p. 24], whereas FBI Part 06, PDF p. 9 records DOB June 23, 1967
  (which calculates to age 24) [2, Part 06 p. 9]. Both records are documented without forced selection.


17. JOSEPH BRADEHOFT, 25
------------------------
Joseph Bradehoft grew up in Illinois and Minnesota, where he lived with his wife and three young children,
ages two to seven [1]. He was an avid outdoorsman who loved sports, camping, and fishing [1]. In July 1991,
seeking steady employment to support his young family, Bradehoft moved to Milwaukee, where his brother Donald
had rented an apartment for him [1].

On the morning of July 16, 1991, Bradehoft left his apartment to attend a job interview and never returned [1, 18].
Dahmer confessed to encountering Bradehoft at a bus stop near the Marquette University campus and bringing him
to Apartment 213, where he killed him on or about July 19, 1991 [3, 15, 19]. Bradehoft was the seventeenth and
final victim murdered by Dahmer prior to Tracy Edwards's escape on the night of July 22, 1991 [1, 3, 19].

Evidence Notes:
- Age & Date of Birth: FBI records confirm DOB January 24, 1966 [2, Part 01 p. 24], confirming age 25 [1, 18, 19].
- Chronology Distinction: Last seen leaving for a job interview on July 16, 1991 [1, 18]; criminal complaint
  alleges encounter and murder occurred on or about July 19, 1991 [3, 15]. Earlier FBI investigative notations
  recording that his former wife saw him on June 4 and spoke with him by phone on July 4 are catalogued as prior
  family contacts, not conflicting last-seen reports [2].

ABOUT THE RECORD: METHODOLOGY & HISTORICAL PROVENANCE
=====================================================================

1. Uneven Public Record
   The surviving public documentation for the seventeen victims is inherently uneven. Some victims—such as
   Konerak Sinthasomphone, Tony Hughes, and Errol Lindsey—were the focus of extensive contemporary investigative
   reporting, courtroom testimony, and civil litigation. For others—such as Raymond Lamont Smith, Ernest Miller,
   and David Thomas—the contemporary documentary trail is comparatively sparse, relying primarily on brief
   wire reports, official missing-person entries, and retrospective family accounts. We do not invent detail
   or fill these gaps with speculation.

2. Categories of Sources
   - Primary Official Records: FBI Vault FOIA files [2], Wisconsin Criminal Complaints [3], and coroner reports.
     These provide authoritative dates of birth, formal missing-person entries, and forensic identification records,
     though they occasionally contain internal administrative discrepancies.
   - Contemporary Wire & Press Coverage: UPI archives [4, 7, 15–21], Washington Post [6, 22, 23], New York Times [5],
     and Marquette AP records [8] from 1991–1992. These document real-time investigative disclosures and court proceedings.
     Crucially, contemporary age labels frequently reflect the victim's age at the time of reporting rather than
     at disappearance.
   - Retrospective Biographical Reporting: Principal reporting from the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel (2022) [1]
     provides essential, human-centered family recollections, schooling, and personal aspirations.
   - Court Proceedings & Victim Impact Statements: Court TV recordings [9] document sworn statements delivered
     by surviving family members during the February 1992 sentencing phase.

3. Discrepancy Policy
   Where primary and secondary sources disagree on dates of birth, ages, or last-seen dates, we do not force
   an artificial consensus. Instead, the discrepancy is displayed transparently with an asterisk (*) and fully
   documented in the accompanying source notes.
